The **Lepromin Test** is an intradermal skin test used in the clinical classification and prognostic evaluation of **leprosy** (_Mycobacterium leprae_).

---

### 1. Immunological Classification

- **Hypersensitivity Type:** The Lepromin test is a classic example of **Type IV (Delayed-Type / Cell-Mediated) Hypersensitivity**.
- **Mediators:** Similar to the Tuberculin (Mantoux) skin test for tuberculosis, the reaction is mediated by **T lymphocytes and macrophages** (cell-mediated immunity) rather than antibodies.

---

### 2. Clinical Purpose & Diagnostic Value

- **Not for Primary Diagnosis:** The test is **not used to diagnose active leprosy**, because patients with the most severe form (lepromatous leprosy) test negative due to a lack of cell-mediated immunity.
- **Prognosis & Disease Classification:** It measures the host's **cell-mediated immune (CMI)** response against _M. leprae_ antigens, helping classify the spectrum of leprosy:
    - **Tuberculoid Leprosy (TT):** Strong cell-mediated immunity \(\rightarrow\) **Lepromin Positive** (good prognosis, localized disease).
    - **Lepromatous Leprosy (LL):** Defective cell-mediated immunity \(\rightarrow\) **Lepromin Negative** (poor CMI, high bacterial load, systemic involvement).

---

### 3. Phases of Response

- **Fernandez Reaction (Early Phase – 48 Hours):** Demonstrates localized delayed-type hypersensitivity (erythema and induration).
- **Mitsuda Reaction (Late Phase – 3 to 4 Weeks):** Demonstrates cell-mediated granulomatous immunity (a distinct papule or nodule at the injection site).
