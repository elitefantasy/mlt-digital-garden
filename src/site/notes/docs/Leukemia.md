- blood cancer
- uncoordinated, excessive formation and accumulation of abnormal White Blood Cells (WBCs) in the bone marrow and the bloodstream.


**Leukemia vs. Leukocytosis** While both conditions result in an abnormally high white blood cell count, there is a critical clinical distinction:

* **Leukocytosis:** Involves an increased count of completely **mature** WBCs.  
* **Leukemia:** Involves the uninhibited proliferation of **premature or immature** WBCs, which are clinically referred to as **"blast cells"**.

## Clinical Features in general
- Anemia
- low wbc, low platelate, organ accumulation
- swollen lymph node, bleeding gum, petechiae(tiny red spot on skin)

## Pathophsiology
Normally, the bone marrow contains Pluripotent Hematopoietic Stem Cells (PHSC) that divide into two main lineages:

1. **Colony Forming Blastocytes (Myeloid Series):** These mature into Red Blood Cells (RBCs), Granulocytes (Neutrophils, Eosinophils, Basophils), Monocytes, and Platelets.  
2. **Lymphoid Stem Cells (Lymphoid Series):** These mature into Lymphocytes.

In a leukemic patient, an abnormality causes these stem cells to multiply excessively without undergoing the normal maturation process. As a result, huge amounts of immature "blast cells" accumulate in the bone marrow, disrupting normal cellular production, and eventually spill out in massive numbers into the systemic blood circulation.

### Neutrophil / Leukocyte Alkaline Phosphatase (NAP / LAP) Test

**NAP (also called LAP)** is a cytochemical test that measures alkaline phosphatase activity inside mature neutrophils. It is mainly used to differentiate **CML (low NAP)** from a **leukemoid reaction (high NAP)**.

#### Principle
- LAP is an enzyme present in the **granules of neutrophils**.
- Alkaline phosphatase hydrolyzes the substrate, releasing a product that couples with the dye to form an **insoluble colored precipitate** at the site of enzyme activity.
- Neutrophils with more enzyme activity show **darker blue/brown granular cytoplasmic staining**; the intensity is graded semiquantitatively.

#### Scoring
- Examine **100 mature neutrophils** (segmented neutrophils/bands).
- Grade staining intensity from **0 to 4+**:
    - 0 = no granules
    - 1+ = faint/few granules
    - 2+ = moderate granules
    - 3+ = strong granules
    - 4+ = very strong/dense granules filling cytoplasm
- Add the scores of all 100 cells.
- Normal score is laboratory-dependent; commonly quoted ranges are about **40–100** or **20–100**.

#### Interpretation

|NAP/LAP score|Common causes|
|---|---|
|**Increased**|Leukemoid reaction, severe infection/inflammation, pregnancy, polycythemia vera, primary myelofibrosis, essential thrombocythemia, Down syndrome, steroid therapy, stress|
|**Normal**|Healthy individuals; some reactive neutrophilias|
|**Decreased**|Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), aplastic anemia, some cases of MDS/AML|
