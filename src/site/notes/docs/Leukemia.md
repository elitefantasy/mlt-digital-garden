- blood cancer
- uncoordinated, excessive formation and accumulation of abnormal White Blood Cells (WBCs) in the bone marrow and the bloodstream.


**Leukemia vs. Leukocytosis** While both conditions result in an abnormally high white blood cell count, there is a critical clinical distinction:

* **Leukocytosis:** Involves an increased count of completely **mature** WBCs.  
* **Leukemia:** Involves the uninhibited proliferation of **premature or immature** WBCs, which are clinically referred to as **"blast cells"**.

## Clinical Features in general
- Anemia
- low wbc, low platelate, organ accumulation
- swollen lymph node, bleeding gum, petechiae(tiny red spot on skin)

## Pathophsiology
Normally, the bone marrow contains Pluripotent Hematopoietic Stem Cells (PHSC) that divide into two main lineages:

1. **Colony Forming Blastocytes (Myeloid Series):** These mature into Red Blood Cells (RBCs), Granulocytes (Neutrophils, Eosinophils, Basophils), Monocytes, and Platelets.  
2. **Lymphoid Stem Cells (Lymphoid Series):** These mature into Lymphocytes.

In a leukemic patient, an abnormality causes these stem cells to multiply excessively without undergoing the normal maturation process. As a result, huge amounts of immature "blast cells" accumulate in the bone marrow, disrupting normal cellular production, and eventually spill out in massive numbers into the systemic blood circulation.

this result in 
- decreased rbcs: anemia
- decreased platelate : bleeding
- dysfunctional wbcs : infection
