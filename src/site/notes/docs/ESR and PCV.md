## ESR & Packed Cell Volume (PCV)

### A. Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR)

* **Principle:** Rate at which RBCs settle out of un-clotted blood in 1 hour. Governed by **Rouleaux formation** (stacking of RBCs like coins), which occurs when plasma proteins (fibrinogen, globulins) neutralize the negative surface charge (*Zeta Potential* caused by sialic acid) on RBC membranes.  
* **Testing Methods:**  
	* **Westergren Method (Gold Standard):** Tube length 300 mm, graduated 0–200 mm, open at both ends. anticoagulat used is ==1;;3.8% Sodium Citrate in a 1:4 ratio (1 part anticoagulant + 4 parts blood).  ==
	* **Wintrobe Method:** Tube length 110 mm (11 cm), closed at bottom end. anticoagulant used is  ==1;;EDTA blood.== 
* **Normal Values:** Males: 0–15 mm/hr; Females: 0–20 mm/hr.

*Increased ESR:* Tuberculosis, bacterial infections, pregnancy, anemia, malignancy, active inflammation.
*Decreased ESR:* Polycythemia, sickle cell disease, spherocytosis, congestive heart failure.


### B. Packed Cell Volume (PCV) / Hematocrit

* **Definition:** Percentage of whole blood volume occupied by packed erythrocytes.  
* **Methods:** Macro Method (Wintrobe tube centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 30 min) or Micro Method (capillary tubes centrifuged at 10,000–12,000 rpm for 5 min).  
* **Normal Reference Values:** Adult Males: 40%–54%; Adult Females: 36%–46%; Newborns: 50%–65%.  
* **Clinical Significance:** Decreased in anemias and overhydration; elevated in polycythemia, dehydration, and severe burns.
