# Leprosy Hansen Disease
**Leprosy** is a chronic granulomatous infectious disease caused mainly by _Mycobacterium leprae_. It primarily affects the **skin**, **peripheral nerves**, upper respiratory mucosa, and eyes. 

### Causative organism

| Feature                | _Mycobacterium leprae_                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Type                   | Slender acid-fast bacillus                                                                                    |
| Stain                  | Ziehl–Neelsen stain;                                                                                          |
| Growth                 | Cannot be grown on ordinary artificial culture media                                                          |
| Intracellular location | Mainly within macrophages and Schwann cells of peripheral nerves                                              |
| Important property     | Prefers cooler body areas, so lesions commonly occur in skin, ears, nose, hands, feet, and superficial nerves |


### Transmission and incubation

- Spread usually requires prolonged close contact with an untreated infectious person, mainly through nasal respiratory droplets.
- Incubation is long—often several years—so disease develops slowly

### Pathogenesis

The bacillus enters the body, multiplies slowly, and infects macrophages and Schwann cells. The **host cell-mediated immune response** determines the clinical form:

- **Strong cell-mediated immunity** → localized disease, few bacilli, tuberculoid form.
- **Weak cell-mediated immunity** → widespread disease, many bacilli, lepromatous form.

The most important complication is peripheral neuropathy. Loss of pain and temperature sensation allows repeated unnoticed trauma, leading to ulcers, secondary infection, resorption of digits, and deformities

### Clinical classification

|Feature|Tuberculoid / Paucibacillary (PB)|Lepromatous / Multibacillary (MB)|
|---|---|---|
|Immunity|Strong cell-mediated immunity|Poor cell-mediated immunity|
|Skin lesions|Few, well-defined, dry, hypopigmented or erythematous patches|Numerous, symmetrical, diffuse lesions, nodules, plaques|
|Sensation|Early and marked sensory loss|Sensory loss occurs later and is more diffuse|
|Nerves|One or few nerves enlarged|Multiple peripheral nerves involved|
|Bacilli in smear|Usually absent or scanty|Numerous acid-fast bacilli|
|Infectivity|Low|Higher|

WHO operational classification commonly considers **1–5 lesions as paucibacillary** and **more than 5 lesions as multibacillary**, though clinical and smear findings also guide assessment.

### Cardinal signs of leprosy

Diagnosis is suggested by one or more of these major signs:

1. **Skin lesion with definite loss of sensation**—especially pain, temperature, or light touch.
2. **Thickened peripheral nerve** with sensory loss and/or muscle weakness.
3. **Demonstration of acid-fast bacilli** in a slit-skin smear or tissue sample.

Commonly examined nerves include the **ulnar**, **common peroneal (lateral popliteal)**, **posterior tibial**, **greater auricular**, and **radial cutaneous** nerves.

### Laboratory diagnosis

- **Slit-skin smear:** Taken from active lesion margins, earlobes, or other cool sites; stained for acid-fast bacilli.
- **Bacillary index (BI):** Indicates bacterial load in smears; it is usually high in multibacillary disease.
- **Skin biopsy:** Shows granulomas in tuberculoid disease and foamy macrophages packed with bacilli in lepromatous disease.
- **Fite–Faraco stain:** Preferred histopathology stain because _M. leprae_ can be damaged by the harsh decolorization used in routine Ziehl–Neelsen staining.
- **Lepromin test:** Indicates host immune response and helps in classification/prognosis; it is **not a diagnostic test** for active leprosy

### Reactions in leprosy

- **Type 1 reaction (reversal reaction):** A delayed hypersensitivity reaction, usually in borderline disease. Existing lesions become swollen and red; neuritis can cause sudden nerve-function loss.
- **Type 2 reaction (erythema nodosum leprosum, ENL):** Immune-complex-mediated reaction, mainly in lepromatous or borderline lepromatous disease. Features include fever, tender red nodules, neuritis, arthritis, orchitis, and eye involvement.

Reactions need prompt treatment because nerve damage may become permanent.

### Treatment

Multidrug therapy prevents drug resistance and is highly effective.

|Category|Standard WHO regimen|
|---|---|
|Paucibacillary leprosy|Rifampicin + dapsone for 6 months|
|Multibacillary leprosy|Rifampicin + clofazimine + dapsone for 12 months|

Important adverse effects:

- **Dapsone:** Hemolysis, methemoglobinemia, and rarely severe hypersensitivity.
- **Rifampicin:** Orange-red discoloration of body fluids and hepatotoxicity.
- **Clofazimine:** Red-brown to blackish skin discoloration and dryness.

### Prevention

Early diagnosis, complete multidrug therapy, contact examination, health education, and prevention of nerve injury are central to control. BCG vaccination has some protective effect, and single-dose rifampicin may be used as post-exposure prophylaxis for eligible contacts in control programs

**Exam point:** Think of leprosy as a disease of **skin patches + sensory loss + thickened peripheral nerves**. The key difference is immunity: tuberculoid disease has strong immunity and few bacilli, while lepromatous disease has weak immunity and many bacilli.


# Ridley–Jopling types

Leprosy is an **immunological spectrum**: from strong cell-mediated immunity with very few bacilli at the **tuberculoid** end, to poor cell-mediated immunity with many bacilli at the **lepromatous** end. The full sequence is:


$\text{TT} \rightarrow \text{BT} \rightarrow \text{BB} \rightarrow \text{BL} \rightarrow \text{LL}$


This classification includes TT, BT, BB, BL, and LL

|Type|Full form|Immunity|Lesions and nerves|Bacilli / smear|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**TT**|Polar tuberculoid leprosy|Very strong CMI|One or few sharply defined, dry, hypopigmented/erythematous anesthetic patches; marked early nerve thickening|Very few or absent|
|**BT**|Borderline tuberculoid leprosy|Fairly strong CMI|More lesions than TT; asymmetrical; satellite lesions may occur; sensory loss and nerve involvement are common|Few bacilli|
|**BB**|Mid-borderline / borderline-borderline leprosy|Intermediate and unstable CMI|Numerous, irregular lesions of variable shape; often annular or “punched-out” appearance; nerve involvement is variable|Moderate number|
|**BL**|Borderline lepromatous leprosy|Low CMI|Numerous, more symmetrical macules, papules, plaques, or nodules; diffuse nerve involvement|Many bacilli|
|**LL**|Polar lepromatous leprosy|Very poor/absent effective CMI|Numerous symmetrical shiny macules, papules, nodules, diffuse infiltration; late widespread nerve damage; may cause leonine facies|Very numerous bacilli|

TT and LL are the two stable poles, while **BT, BB, and BL are borderline forms** that can shift toward either end depending on immunity and treatment

## Easy memory line

**“TT = Tough T-cell response; LL = Low lymphocyte response.”**

- Moving from **TT to LL**: immunity decreases, number of lesions increases, symmetry increases, and bacillary load increases.
- **BB** is the most immunologically unstable form and lies in the middle of the spectrum.


# Lepromin Test
The **Lepromin Test** is an intradermal skin test used in the clinical classification and prognostic evaluation of **leprosy** (_Mycobacterium leprae_).

---

### 1. Immunological Classification

- **Hypersensitivity Type:** The Lepromin test is a classic example of **Type IV (Delayed-Type / Cell-Mediated) Hypersensitivity**.
- **Mediators:** Similar to the Tuberculin (Mantoux) skin test for tuberculosis, the reaction is mediated by **T lymphocytes and macrophages** (cell-mediated immunity) rather than antibodies.

---

### 2. Clinical Purpose & Diagnostic Value

- **Not for Primary Diagnosis:** The test is **not used to diagnose active leprosy**, because patients with the most severe form (lepromatous leprosy) test negative due to a lack of cell-mediated immunity.
- **Prognosis & Disease Classification:** It measures the host's **cell-mediated immune (CMI)** response against _M. leprae_ antigens, helping classify the spectrum of leprosy:
    - **Tuberculoid Leprosy (TT):** Strong cell-mediated immunity \(\rightarrow\) **Lepromin Positive** (good prognosis, localized disease).
    - **Lepromatous Leprosy (LL):** Defective cell-mediated immunity \(\rightarrow\) **Lepromin Negative** (poor CMI, high bacterial load, systemic involvement).

---

### 3. Phases of Response

- **Fernandez Reaction (Early Phase – 48 Hours):** Demonstrates localized delayed-type hypersensitivity (erythema and induration).
- **Mitsuda Reaction (Late Phase – 3 to 4 Weeks):** Demonstrates cell-mediated granulomatous immunity (a distinct papule or nodule at the injection site).
