 - **Anatomy:** The liver is the largest organ in the human body, consisting of a right lobe (four times larger) and a left lobe.  
* **Dual Blood Supply (Inlets):** oxygenated blood via the **hepatic artery** (from the aorta) and nutrient-rich, deoxygenated blood via the **portal vein** (from the gastrointestinal tract).  
* **Drainage (Outlets):** Blood exits the liver via a single common exit, the **hepatic vein (central vein)**, which drains into the inferior vena cava (IVC). Bile synthesized by the liver exits via the **bile duct** to the duodenum.  
* **Classical Lobule Model:** The liver is divided into millions of hexagonal classical lobules.  
	* **Center:** Contains the Central Vein (hepatic vein).  
	* **Corners:** Have 4 to 6 Portal Triads. A Portal Triad consists of branches of the hepatic artery, portal vein, and bile duct traveling together.  
	* **Cords of Hepatocytes:** Trains of cells connecting the central vein and the portal triads. The very first hepatocyte in the cord forms the **limiting plate**.  
	* **Sinusoids:** Blood capillaries running between adjacent cords of hepatocytes, lined by endothelial cells and interspersed **Kupffer cells** (macrophages).  
	* **Space of Disse:** The space between the hepatocytes and sinusoids. It contains **Ito cells** (fat-storing cells that synthesize Vitamin A). When the liver is injured (e.g., by a virus), Ito cells convert into myofibroblasts and form collagen, causing fibrosis and cirrhosis.
