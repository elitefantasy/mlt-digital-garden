## Hemoglobin: Structure, Variants, Estimation & Disorders**
- Normal hemoglobins are tetramers of two a-like and two B-like globin polypeptide chains. a-like: 141 amino acids B-like: 146 amino acids
![Pasted image 20260831190945.png\|401](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260831190945.png)
- 4 globin polypeptide chain + 4 heme groups
-  Iron is present in the **Fe2+ (ferrous)** state.
- One Hb molecule carries 4 O2 molecules.  
* **Normal Variants:**  
  * **HbA (α2β2):** Major adult form (95%–99%).  
  * **HbA2 (α2δ2):** Minor adult form (\~1%–3%).  
  * **HbF (α2γ2):** Fetal hemoglobin (\>95% in fetus/newborn, \<1% in adults). High O2 affinity. 

**Hb Estimation Methods:**  
* **Sahli's Acid Hematin Method:** Blood mixed with N/10 HCl forms brown acid hematin; compared visually against standard comparator. Measures HbA; old manual method.  
* **Cyanmethemoglobin Method (Drabkin's Reagent):** Reference / Gold Standard method. Potassium ferricyanide & potassium cyanide convert Hb forms to cyanmethemoglobin; measured at 540 nm on spectrophotometer. 

**Abnormal Hb Derivatives:**  
  * **Carboxyhemoglobin:** Hb bound to Carbon Monoxide (CO). High affinity (200x \> O2). Causes toxic hypoxia.  
  * **Methemoglobin:** Iron oxidized to Fe3+ (ferric) state. Incapable of binding oxygen.  
  * **Sulfhemoglobin:** Irreversible sulfur binding; caused by sulfonamides.  
# Hemoglobinopathies: 
Hemoglobinopathies are classified into two major groups: **quantitative defects** (thalassemia syndromes, where globin chain production is reduced or absent) and **qualitative/structural defects** (abnormal hemoglobin variants, where the globin chain structure is altered).
## 1. Quantitative defects — Thalassemia syndromes
[[docs/Thalassemia\|Thalassemia]]
These result from decreased or absent synthesis of one or more globin chains, leading to imbalanced chain production and ineffective erythropoiesis/hemolysis

### By chain involved
- **α‑thalassemias** (α-globin gene defects; 4 α-globin genes total: αα/αα)
    - Silent carrier (−α/αα)
    - α‑thalassemia trait / minor (−−/αα or −α/−α)
    - HbH disease (−−/−α)
    - Hb Bart’s hydrops fetalis (−−/−−)
- **β‑thalassemias** (β-globin gene defects; 2 β-globin genes)
    - β‑thalassemia minor / trait (heterozygous)
    - β‑thalassemia intermedia
    - β‑thalassemia major (homozygous/compound heterozygous, transfusion‑dependent)
- **δβ‑thalassemias / high‑HbF thalassemias**
    - (δβ)⁰ thalassemia
    - Hereditary persistence of fetal hemoglobin (HPFH) – increased HbF without major clinical disease


## 2. Qualitative/structural defects — Abnormal hemoglobin variants

These arise from point mutations or small deletions that change the amino acid sequence of a globin chain, altering hemoglobin function/stability.[

- **Sickle cell disorders** (HbS and related) [[docs/Sickel Cell Anemia\|Sickel Cell Anemia]]
    - HbSS (sickle cell anemia)
    - HbSC disease
    - HbS/β‑thalassemia (S/β⁰ or S/β⁺)
    - HbS with other variants (e.g., SD, SO‑Arab)
    - HbS/HPFH
- **Other common structural variants**
    - HbC disease (HbCC, HbAC trait)
    - HbE disease (HbEE, HbAE trait; HbE also has a thalassemic phenotype)
    - HbD disease (e.g., HbSD)
- **Unstable hemoglobins**
    - Mutations causing precipitation of hemoglobin → Heinz bodies, hemolytic anemia (e.g., Hb Köln, Hb Zurich)
    - acquired instability -> oxidant hemolysis : Drug induced , G6PD deficiency
- **Hemoglobins with altered oxygen affinity**
    - **High O₂ affinity** → tissue hypoxia → compensatory polycythemia (e.g., Fetal Red Cells, Decreased 2,3-BPG , carboxyhemoglobinemia, HbCO,  Chesapeake, Hb Montefiore)
    - **Low O₂ affinity** → cyanosis with relatively mild symptoms (e.g., increased RBC 2,3 BPG, Hb Kansas, Hb Beth Israel)
- **Methemoglobin‑forming variants (Hb M)**
    - Stabilize iron in Fe³⁺ state → cyanosis unresponsive to oxygen
- **Clinically silent variants**
    - Many rare Hb variants have no significant hematologic or clinical effect and are detected incidentally on electrophoresis/HPLC.
