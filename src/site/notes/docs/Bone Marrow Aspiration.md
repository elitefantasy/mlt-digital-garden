## 1\. Definition and Purpose
**Definition:** Bone marrow aspiration is an invasive clinical procedure performed to collect liquid bone marrow from the inner cavities of bones for diagnostic examination and follow-up of blood and marrow diseases.

### Main Purposes:

* Morphologic evaluation of **hematopoietic cells** (erythroid, myeloid, megakaryocytic, lymphoid lines).  
* Differential count of marrow cells and **blast** percentage.  
* **Assessment of iron stores** (Prussian blue stain or Perl’s stain ) and other special stains.  
* Evaluation of **bone marrow fibrosis**: Routine H\&E staining is often insufficient for visualizing fibers, so specific stains are used:  
	* **Reticulin Stain**: Uses the Gomori silver impregnation technique to detect early fibrosis.  
	* Trichrome Stain: Uses stains like Masson's trichrome to identify advanced collagen fibrosis.  
* Collection of material for cytochemistry, flow cytometry, cytogenetics, and molecular tests.

## 2\. Bone Anatomy and Physiology

To understand the procedure, it is essential to know the three primary layers of a bone:
![Pasted image 20260824183059.png\|465](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260824183059.png)

* **Periosteum**: The outermost, superficial shining layer of the bone.  
* **Compact Bone** (Cortex): The hard, dense middle layer.  
* **Bone Marrow Cavity** (Medullary Cavity): The innermost space where the soft bone marrow is situated. During childhood, this cavity contains mostly red bone marrow, which partially converts to yellow marrow in adulthood.

## 5\. Procedure Details and "Loss of Resistance"

Step-by-Step Technique:

1. Identify and clean puncture site; apply local anesthesia to skin and periosteum.  
2. Introduce the needle with stylet through a small skin incision.  
3. Loss of Resistance: As the needle pierces the dense cortex, practitioners feel strong resistance. A sudden "slight give" indicates the needle has successfully entered the soft marrow cavity.  
4. Remove stylet, attach 10 mL syringe, and aspirate 0.2–0.5 mL for the best particle quality.  
5. Withdraw needle, apply firm pressure, and dress the site.

Note on Pain: Patients may feel sharp pressure during aspiration; local anesthesia (e.g., lignocaine) is used for the superficial layers.

## Indications for Bone Marrow Trephine Biopsy

While bone marrow aspiration and trephine biopsy are often performed together as complementary techniques, a trephine biopsy is particularly diagnostic for conditions where aspiration may yield a "**dry tap**" or where evaluation of **marrow architecture and cellularity** is crucial for an accurate diagnosis. It provides a panoramic view of the marrow architecture that liquid aspirates cannot capture.  

Specific conditions where trephine biopsy is essential include:
* **Aplastic Anemia**: Essential for confirming hypocellular marrow and ruling out other marrow failure syndromes.  
* **Myelofibrosis**: Diagnostic for identifying marrow fibrosis and collagen deposition, which often prevent successful aspiration.  
* **Metastatic Infiltration and Focal Lesions**: Critical for detecting focal infiltrative processes, such as metastatic **carcinomas** or **granulomas**, which may be underrepresented or missed in a liquid sample.

Trephine biopsy is the **gold standard** for assessing overall bone marrow cellularity and identifies infiltrative processes by preserving the spatial relationship between cells and the bone architecture.


## Requirements
- Aspiration needle Should be stout and made of hard stainless steel. 
- About 7-8 cm in length with a well fitting stylet provided with a adjustable guard to prevent over penetration. 
- The most commonly used needle is ==Salah / Klima / jamshedi== .
- Bore size 0.2mm. 
- Spirited swab Betadine Local anaesthesia (2% lignocaine and Xylocaine). 
- Two 10-20 ml syringe Clean grease free and iron free glass slide Smooth edge glass slide spreader to make aspirate film.

**Types of needle**
a) salah needle : guard with a **side** screw
b) klima needle : guard with screw **along the length (longitudinal)**
c) jamshidi needle : longer, both aspiration and biopsy

**anticoagulants for d/f studies**
- chromosome / pcr / cytogenetics studies : 0.5ml of bone marrow aspirate in Sodium Heparin tube and EDTA
- flow cytometry : in EDTA
- Fixatives
	- slides : methanol
	- aspirated histological section : ethanol + 15% formalin

## 7\. Complications and Prevention

* Pain \&amp; Hematoma: Most common; prevented by proper anesthetic and post-op pressure.  
* Dry Tap: Failure to obtain marrow, often due to fibrosis or packed marrow.  
* Cardiac Tamponade: Rare risk specific to sternal aspiration if the needle over-penetrates.

## 8\. Lab Role and Reporting

MLT Responsibilities: High-quality smear preparation is critical. Smears must be air-dried before staining (e.g., Romanowsky, Prussian blue).  
Reporting: Includes cellularity, M:E ratio (Normal 2:1 to 4:1), and presence of blasts (\<5% normal) or dysplasia.

## 9\. Pathologic Patterns and Revision Points

* Aplastic Anemia: Hypocellular, replaced by fat.  
* Leukemia: Hypercellular with ≥20% blasts.  
* Multiple Myeloma: Plasma cells \>10%.

M:E Ratio: Increased in infection/CML; decreased in hemolytic anemia.

# Staining Techniques
Different stains are used to examine bone marrow smears. \
1. Leishman Stain . Most commonly used Used to examine cell morphology 
2. Wright Stain . Used for blood and marrow cells 
3. Giemsa Stain Used for detailed cellular structure
4. Prussian Blue Stain . Used to detect iron stores in marrow 
5. PAS Stain (Periodic Acid Schiff) Used for leukemia diagnosis 
6. Sudan Black B Used in acute leukemia classification

## Romanowsky Stains 
**Common stains used for marrow smears:** 
Wright stain , Wright-Giemsa stain , Giemsa stain , May-Grünwald-Giemsa stain 

They demonstrate: Nuclear morphology , Cytoplasmic granules. RBC maturation , Myeloid maturation , Megakaryocytes

**Wright Stain**
Principle
Wright stain contains eosin (acidic dye) and methylene blue (basic dye). Eosin stains basic components (hemoglobin, cytoplasm) -> pink/red Methylene blue stains acidic components (nucleus, RNA) blue/purple This contrast allows clear identification of bone marrow cells

Reagents Required
Wright stain solution . Buffer solution (pH 6.4-6.8) Distilled water Bone marrow smear slide

Procedure
1. Prepare smear . Make a thin bone marrow smear on a clean glass slide and allow it to air dry. 
2. Fixation : Flood the slide with Wright stain for 1-3 minutes (methanol in stain acts as fixative). 
3. Dilution Add equal amount of buffer solution and mix gently on the slide.
4. Staining . Leave for 5-7 minutes. 
5. . Washing Wash gently with distilled water. 
6. Drying Air dry the slide. 
7. Microscopic Examination Observe under oil immersion (100x) microscope.
