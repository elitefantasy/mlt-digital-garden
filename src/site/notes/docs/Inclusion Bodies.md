# **Inclusion Bodies** 

# **Viral Inclusion Bodies**

### **Negri bodies**

* **Location:** Cytoplasm of neurons (especially pyramidal cells of hippocampus, Purkinje cells).  
* **Appearance:** Round/oval, eosinophilic, sharply outlined inclusions.  
* **Composition:** Viral ribonucleoprotein (RNP) aggregates; sites of rabies virus replication.  
* **Association:**  
* **Rabies virus**; classically pathognomonic but not always present.

### **Guarnieri bodies**

* **Location:** Cytoplasm of epithelial cells (skin, mucosa).  
* **Appearance:** Eosinophilic (pink) intracytoplasmic inclusions in eosin stains.  
* **Composition:** Aggregates of poxvirus particles and viral proteins; sites of viral replication.  
* **Association:**  
* **Poxviruses** – smallpox (variola), vaccinia, other orthopoxviruses.

### **Henderson–Patterson bodies (Molluscum bodies)**

* **Location:** Cytoplasm of keratinocytes in stratum spinosum/granulosum of epidermis.  
* **Appearance:** Large eosinophilic (early) → basophilic (mature) intracytoplasmic inclusions; “ghost cells” filled with viral material.  
* **Composition:** Viral particles and altered keratin.  
* **Association:**  
* **Molluscum contagiosum virus** (poxvirus family).

### **Owl’s eye inclusion bodies**

* **Location:** Nucleus of infected cells (endothelial, epithelial, leukocytes, etc.).  
* **Appearance:** Large basophilic intranuclear inclusion with a clear halo, giving an “owl’s eye” look.  
* **Composition:** Viral nucleocapsids and associated proteins.  
* **Association:**  
* **Cytomegalovirus (CMV)**; highly specific but not very sensitive for CMV tissue disease.

# **RBC Inclusion Bodies**

### **Howell–Jolly bodies**

* **Composition:**  
* **DNA remnants** (nuclear fragments).  
* **Appearance:** Small, round, dark purple/blue dots, usually single per RBC.  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Post-splenectomy / hyposplenism / asplenia** (spleen normally removes them)  
  * **Megaloblastic anemia** (ineffective erythropoiesis, nuclear–cytoplasmic asynchrony)

### **Heinz bodies**

* **Composition:** Denatured **hemoglobin** precipitates.  
* **Appearance:** Not seen on routine Wright stain; visualized with **supravital stains** (e.g., new methylene blue) as pale or refractile inclusions.  
* **Associations:**  
  * **G6PD deficiency** (oxidative stress)  
  * Other hemoglobinopathies, oxidative drugs/chemicals

### **Pappenheimer bodies**

* **Composition:**  
* **Iron-containing granules** (hemosiderin in autophagosomes/mitochondria).  
* **Appearance:** Small, irregular, basophilic/black granules, often clustered at RBC periphery.  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Sideroblastic anemia**  
  * **Hyposplenism / post-splenectomy**  
  * Some hemoglobinopathies, iron-loading states

### **Basophilic stippling**

* **Composition:** Aggregates of **ribosomal RNA** and ribosomes.  
* **Appearance:** Fine or coarse blue-purple granules distributed throughout cytoplasm.  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Lead poisoning** (classic)  
  * **Thalassemia**, other hemoglobinopathies  
  * **Megaloblastic anemia**, dyserythropoiesis

### **Cabot rings**

* **Composition:** Remnants of **mitotic spindle microtubules** and/or nuclear membrane.  
* **Appearance:** Thin, ring-shaped, figure-of-8, or loop-like structures.  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Severe dyserythropoiesis**  
  * **Megaloblastic anemia**, myelodysplastic syndromes, lead poisoning

# **WBC Inclusion Bodies**

### **Döhle bodies**

* **Location:** Cytoplasm of **neutrophils** (sometimes other granulocytes).  
* **Appearance:** Pale blue, peripheral, irregular patches.  
* **Composition:** Aggregates of rough endoplasmic reticulum (RNA).  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Infection / inflammation** (toxic changes)  
  * Pregnancy, burns, May–Hegglin anomaly

### **Auer rods**

* **Location:** Cytoplasm of **myeloblasts / promyelocytes**.  
* **Appearance:** Elongated, needle-like, red-purple cytoplasmic inclusions.  
* **Composition:** Fused primary granules (lysosomal enzymes).  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Acute myeloid leukemia (AML)**, especially acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL, t(15;17)).

# **Other Important Inclusion Bodies**

### **Russell bodies**

* **Location:** Cytoplasm of **plasma cells**.  
* **Appearance:** Large, round, eosinophilic inclusions.  
* **Composition:** Accumulated **immunoglobulins** within distended RER.  
* **Associations:** Chronic antigenic stimulation, plasma cell dyscrasias (e.g., multiple myeloma, chronic inflammation).

### **Councilman bodies**

* **Location:** Hepatocytes (liver).  
* **Appearance:** Eosinophilic, shrunken, apoptotic hepatocytes (“acidophil bodies”).  
* **Composition:** Apoptotic cell remnants.  
* **Associations:**  
* **Viral hepatitis** (especially yellow fever, also hepatitis A/B/C).

### **Barr bodies**

* **Location:** Nucleus of somatic cells (e.g., epithelial cells, neutrophil “drumstick” lobes in females).  
* **Appearance:** Dense, darkly staining chromatin mass at nuclear periphery.  
* **Composition:** Inactivated **X chromosome** (lyonization).  
* **Associations:**  
  * **Normal females** (XX → 1 Barr body)  
  * **Klinefelter syndrome** (47,XXY → 1 Barr body), Triple X (2 Barr bodies).
