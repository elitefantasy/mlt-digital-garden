Spectrophotometry  
1\. Introduction

* **Definition:** Spectrophotometry is an analytical method used to measure the intensity of light as a beam passes through a sample solution.  
* **Purpose:** It allows for the quantitative and qualitative analysis of substances by determining their concentration, purity, and molecular structure.  
* **Core Concept:** The technique is based on the absorption and transmission of light by a chemical substance.

2\. Fundamental Principle: The Beer-Lambert Law

* **Beer-Lambert Law:** The foundation of spectrophotometry. It states that the absorbance of light is directly proportional to the concentration of the absorbing solute and the path length of the light through the solution.  
* **Equation:** (A \= \\epsilon l c)  
  * **A:** Absorbance (unitless).  
  * **(\\epsilon):** Molar extinction coefficient (or molar absorptivity).  
  * **l:** Path length of the light.  
  * **c:** Concentration of the substance.

3\. Instrumentation

A spectrophotometer typically consists of the following components:

* **Light Source:** Produces a beam of light. Common types include tungsten lamps (visible range) or xenon/deuterium lamps (UV range).  
* **Monochromator:** Disperses polychromatic light into specific, narrow wavelengths using prisms or diffraction gratings.  
* **Sample Holder (Cuvette):** Holds the liquid solution. Usually made of glass (for visible range) or quartz (for UV range).  
* **Detector (Photodetector):** Measures the intensity of light transmitted through the sample and converts it into an electrical signal.  
* **Display/Readout System:** Converts the signal into numerical data (absorbance or transmittance).

4\. Types of Spectrophotometers

* **Based on Wavelength Range:**  
  * **UV Spectrophotometer:** Operates in the 200–400 nm range.  
  * **Visible Spectrophotometer:** Operates in the 400–700 nm range.  
  * **UV-Visible Spectrophotometer:** Combines both ranges.  
  * Note: colorimeter runs only one visible wavelength range   
  * Note : white light comes under non visible wavelength.  
* **Based on Beam Path:**  
  * **Single-beam:** Measures the blank and sample sequentially.  
  * **Double-beam:** Measures sample and reference simultaneously for greater stability.

5\. Applications

* **Clinical/Biomedical:** Estimation of DNA, RNA, proteins, hemoglobin, and glucose levels.  
* **Industrial/Research:** Drug analysis, enzyme kinetics, water quality testing, food color analysis, and purity assessment of compounds.  
* **Environmental:** Detection of pollutants and contaminants.

6\. Comparison: Colorimeter vs. Spectrophotometer

* **Wavelength Selection:** Colorimeters use simple filters, while spectrophotometers use sophisticated monochromators (prisms/gratings).  
* **Accuracy:** Spectrophotometers offer higher accuracy, sensitivity, and precision compared to colorimeters.  
* **Usage:** Colorimeters are suitable for routine, less complex lab work; spectrophotometers are required for detailed quantitative and qualitative analysis
