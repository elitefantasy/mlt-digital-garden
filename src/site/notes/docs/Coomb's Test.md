-   define Coombs test :: antiglobulin test used to detect antibodies IgG / Complement molecules directed against red blood cells (RBCs).
-   It is mainly used to diagnose immune‑mediated hemolysis (e.g. autoimmune hemolytic anemia, hemolytic disease of the newborn) and to check blood compatibility before transfusion.

Think of it as a test that asks: “Are there IgG / complement molecules on RBCs, or in serum, that can attack RBCs?”

-   how Coomb’s Reagent is made
    -   Human produce IgG / IgM antibodies
    -   Rabit mein ye human blood daala
    -   Rabit start producing Antibodies against human
    -   This newly produced antibodies are extracted
    -   Now we have polyspecific and mono specific antibodies these are coomb’s reagent
    -   Polyspecific: contain anti human igG , anti human C3
    -   Monospecific : contains either anti human igG or anti human complement(c3) not both

## Types of Coombs Test

### 1. Direct Coombs Test (Direct Antiglobulin Test, DAT)
![Pasted image 20260928182735.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260928182735.png)
-   Detects antibodies / complement attached to patient rbc.
-   Sample: patient’s RBCs (cells are washed to remove unbound proteins).

Clinical significance (main uses):
-   Autoimmune hemolytic anemia (warm and cold types).
-   Hemolytic disease of the newborn (Rh/ABO incompatibility).
-   Drug‑induced immune hemolysis.
-   Transfusion reactions (immune hemolysis post‑transfusion).

Procedure
-   5% cell suspension prepare in saline
-   1 drop of this suspension added in test tube
-   Washed 3 times
-   After last wash -> completely decant
-   In this test tube add 2 drop of AHG(Anti Human Globulin)
-   Mix, centrifuge , 1500 rpm , 1 min
-   Resuspend cell , observe microscopic , macroscopic

Interpretation
-   Agglution: positive DCT , meaning Ab or complement attached to RBCs

### 2. Indirect Coombs Test (Indirect Antiglobulin Test, IAT)
![Pasted image 20260928182856.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260928182856.png)
-   Detects antibodies against RBC antigens circulating freely in patient’s serum (not yet attached to RBCs).
-   Sample: patient’s serum + reagent RBCs of known antigen profile.

**Clinical significance (main uses):**
-   Antibody screening and crossmatching prior to blood transfusion.
-   Prenatal testing in pregnant women (Rh incompatibility, alloantibodies).
-   Screening for unexpected alloantibodies in donors/recipients.

**Procedure**
How to prepare pooled cell ?
-   Minimum 3 sample from 3 different people of same blood group(Rh is not considered , eg: O+ , O-)
-   All three are mixed in single test tube
-   Create cell suspension of 5% of this sample
-   Sedimented rbc , wash 3 times
After preparing pooled cell

-   Take 3 test tube labeld as Test, Positive control , negative control
-   Step 1: add 2 drop of serum in (T) ; 1 drop Anti D serum in (PC) ; 1 drop normal saline in (NC) ; all three are mixed with pooled cell
-   Step2: Incubation for 1 hour
-   Step3: 3 time washing
-   Step4: 2 drop AHG in (T) , (PC) , (N) ; look for agglutination

#### Principle / Immunology Concept
-   Many clinically important RBC antibodies are IgG (“incomplete antibodies”) that sensitize RBCs but do not cause visible agglutination on their own.
-   Anti‑human globulin reagent (Coombs serum) contains antibodies against human IgG (and often complement C3).
-   AHG binds to Fc portion of IgG (or to C3) already bound to RBC membranes; this bridges RBCs together → visible agglutination = positive Coombs test.

So, Coombs test is essentially a “bridge” test: AHG bridges sensitized RBCs to reveal hidden IgG/complement on them.

#### Key Viva / Short‑note Points
-   Invented by Coombs, Mourant and Race (1945).
-   Detects “incomplete” IgG antibodies and complement on RBCs or in serum.
-   Direct = patient RBCs; checks in vivo sensitization (AIHA, HDN).
-   Indirect = patient serum + reagent RBCs; used for antibody screening, crossmatch, antenatal testing.
-   Principle: AHG bridges sensitized RBCs → agglutination.
-   Essential quality control: Coombs control cells to confirm reagent activity and proper washing.
