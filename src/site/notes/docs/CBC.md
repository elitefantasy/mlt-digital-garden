## Components of CBC
### A. Red Cell Indices

| Index | Formula | Normal Range | Clinical Significance |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **MCV** | (Hct (%) / RBC (x10^12/L)) × 10 | 80–100 fL | Classifies RBC size (Microcytic: \<80 fL, Normocytic: 80–100 fL, Macrocytic: \>100 fL). |
| **MCH** | (Hb (g/dL) / RBC (x10^12/L)) × 10 | 27–31 pg | Average mass of hemoglobin per RBC. Indicates normochromic, hypochromic, or hyperchromic states. |
| **MCHC** | (Hb (g/dL) / Hct (%)) × 100 | 32–36 g/dL | Measures hemoglobin concentration per unit volume of RBCs. Used to identify conditions like spherocytosis or hypochromia. |
| **RDW** | (Standard Deviation of MCV / Mean MCV) × 100 | 11.5–14.5% | Quantifies variation in RBC size (anisocytosis); high values are early indicators of nutritional deficiencies. |

### C. Platelets (PLT)

Essential for primary hemostasis. A low count (**thrombocytopenia**) increases bleeding risk, while a high count (**thrombocytosis**) can predispose patients to inappropriate clotting.

| Parameter | Normal Range | Clinical Significance |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Platelet Count** | 150–450 x 10^9/L | Essential for primary hemostasis; low (thrombocytopenia) causes bleeding risk, high (thrombocytosis) increases clotting risk. |

# CBC Principle

Modern hematology analyzers have largely replaced manual chamber counting (like the Neubauer chamber, which is now primarily for specialized settings).

* **Electrical Impedance (Coulter Principle):** As cells pass through a small orifice, they create an impedance change in an electrical current, which is proportional to cell volume.  
* **Flow Cytometry:** Uses lasers to analyze cell granularity, diameter, and complexity (used to generate the WBC differential).  
* **Spectrophotometry:** Used specifically to quantify Hemoglobin concentration.
