### I. Overview & Definition
?
- **Definition:** **Turner Syndrome** is a genetic chromosomal disorder that primarily affects females.
- **Chromosomal Abnormality:** While a typical human has 46 chromosomes, individuals with Turner Syndrome possess **only 45 chromosomes**.
- **Karyotype:** This condition is medically denoted as **45, X** (or 45, X0), indicating that one of the sex chromosomes (an X chromosome) is completely or partially missing.

### **II. Chromosomal Background**

To understand the pathogenesis of Turner Syndrome, it is helpful to look at normal human chromosomal architecture:

1. **Somatic Chromosomes:** Normal human somatic cells contain **23 pairs of chromosomes**, totaling **46 chromosomes**.
2. **Autosomes:** Out of these, **22 pairs (44 chromosomes)** are autosomes, which regulate general bodily characteristics and metabolic functions.
3. **Sex Chromosomes:** One pair (2 chromosomes) is responsible for determining biological sex.
    - **XX** represents a biological female.
    - **XY** represents a biological male.

---

### **III. The "Double Dose" Effect & X-Inactivation (Bar Bodies)**

- **The Problem:** Because females normally possess two X chromosomes (XX) and males possess only one (XY), females would experience a potentially harmful **"double dose effect"** if both of their X chromosomes were fully active.
- **The Solution:** To prevent this genetic dosage imbalance, nature inactivates one of the X chromosomes in each cell of a female's body.
- **Bar Body (Sex Chromatin):** This inactivated, highly condensed X chromosome is visible under a microscope and is known as a **Bar Body** or **Sex Chromatin**. It is typically present in normal females but absent in normal males. Even though one X chromosome is inactive, normal females still possess a total of 46 chromosomes.

---

### **IV. The Status of Bar Bodies in Turner Syndrome**

- **Bar Body Absence:** In individuals with Turner Syndrome, there is **only one X chromosome present** in the cells.
- **No Inactivation:** Since there is no second X chromosome to condense, **no X-inactivation occurs**, and **Bar Bodies are completely absent** (0 Bar Bodies).
- **Physiological Necessity:** The single remaining X chromosome must remain active because its genetic material is absolutely essential for the basic survival and functioning of the body's cells.

---

### **V. Clinical Features & Symptoms**

The loss of the second X chromosome stops normal somatic development, presenting as a series of characteristic physical traits:
?
- **Stunted Growth / Short Stature:** The physical growth of the female stops abnormally early, causing them to remain very small or short in stature.
- **Broad Face**.
- **Broad Chest:** Often described clinically as a broad or shield-like chest.
- **Webbed Neck:** The neck appears abnormally broad, wide, or webbed.
- **Abnormal/Impaired Development:** Overall bodily growth and development do not proceed normally, resulting in congenital abnormalities.

### **VI. Laboratory & Cytological Diagnosis**

- **Buccal Smear/Cytological Screening:** Cytological examinations can screen for sex chromatin by taking a sample of epithelial cells from the cheek (buccal mucosa).
- **Diagnostic Finding:** Unlike a normal female who is Bar Body positive, a female with Turner Syndrome will show a **complete absence of Bar Bodies** upon cytological evaluation.
