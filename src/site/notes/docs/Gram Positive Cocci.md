# Differentiating Staphylococcus and Streptococcus
-   **Microscopic Arrangement:**
    -   Staphylococcus: Spherical cells arranged in grape-like clusters.
    -   Streptococcus: Spherical cells arranged in pairs or chains.
-   **🧪 Catalase Test:**
    -   **Principle:** Mixes hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) with bacterial colony suspension. The catalase enzyme breaks H₂O₂ → H₂O + O₂, yielding immediate effervescence/bubbles.
    -   Staphylococcus: Catalase Positive (effervescence produced).
    -   Streptococcus: Catalase Negative (no effervescence).

## 1. Staphylococcus Species Identification

-   **🧪 Coagulase Test:** Differentiates *Staphylococcus aureus* (Coagulase Positive) from Coagulase-Negative Staphylococci (CoNS).
-   **🧪 Novobiocin Sensitivity Test:** Differentiates CoNS species:
    -   Novobiocin Sensitive: *Staphylococcus epidermidis*
    -   Novobiocin Resistant: *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*

### 🦠 Staphylococcus aureus

#### 🧪 Culture Media Characteristics:

-   **Nutrient Agar:** Produces golden-yellow pigmented colonies displaying a classic "oil paint appearance".
-   **🩸 Blood Agar:** Exhibits complete β-hemolysis around colonies.
-   **Phenolphthalein Phosphate Agar (PPA):** Produces bright pink colonies upon exposure to ammonia fumes.
-   **Selective Media:**
    -   Ludlam's Media (Blood agar supplemented with lithium chloride and tellurite).
    -  <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;"> Mannitol Salt Agar (MSA) </mark>(Blood agar supplemented with mannitol and 7.5% sodium chloride NaCl).
    -   Columbia Polymyxin Medium.

#### ☣️ Virulence Factors and Toxins:
-   **Cytolytic Toxins:** α, β, γ, δ toxins and Panton-Valentine (PV) Leukocidin.
-   **Enterotoxins:** 8 distinct serotypes (A–E, G–I); Type A enterotoxin is the most common cause of staphylococcal food poisoning.
-   **Toxic Shock Syndrome Toxin-1 (TSST-1):** Superantigen causing <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">Toxic Shock Syndrome.</mark>
-   **Exfoliative / Epidermolytic Toxins:** Serotypes A and B cause Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS).

#### Enzymes:
-   **Free Coagulase:** Extracellular enzyme secreted into cytoplasm; detected via the Tube Coagulase Test by converting fibrinogen to fibrin clot.
-   **Bound Coagulase (Clumping Factor):** Cell-wall associated protein; detected via the Slide Coagulase Test.

#### 🏥 Clinical Pathogenesis and Diseases:
?
-   **Cutaneous Infections:** Most common cause of <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">suppurative skin lesions</mark> (folliculitis, furuncles/boils, carbuncles, abscesses, impetigo, and cellulitis).
-   **Deep/Invasive Infections:** Can infect any organ hematogenously (most common cause of acute osteomyelitis, acute bacterial endocarditis, and septic arthritis).
-   **Staphylococcal Food Poisoning:**
    -   **Incubation Period:** Rapid onset of 2–6 hours post-ingestion.
    -   **Contaminated Foods:** Dairy products (milk, cheese, paneer), meat, fish, and creamy pastries.
    -   **Predominant Symptom:** Severe vomiting (mediated central stimulation of vomiting center and local intestinal hyperperistalsis).
-   **Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS):**
    -   **Clinical Presentation:** High fever, vomiting, diarrhea, diffuse erythematous rash, hypotension/shock, multiorgan involvement, elevated WBC, and negative blood culture.
    -   **Classic Scenario:** Young menstruating females using high-absorbency tampons.
-   **Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS):**
    -   Exfoliative toxin splits desmoglein-1 in the stratum granulosum.
    -   Manifests as widespread intraepidermal flaccid bullae and Nikolsky sign positive (epidermis detaches easily upon light friction).

### 🦠 Coagulase-Negative Staphylococci (CoNS)
-   Produce non-pigmented white colonies; commensals of skin and mucous membranes.
-   **Staphylococcus epidermidis:**
    -   Novobiocin sensitive.
    -   Biofilm-forming organism on indwelling prosthetic devices.
    -   Most common cause of early prosthetic valve endocarditis (occurring within 1 year post-cardiac surgery).
-   **Staphylococcus saprophyticus:**
    -   Novobiocin resistant.
    -   Second most common cause of acute urinary tract infections (UTI) in young, sexually active females.

## 2. Streptococcus Classification
-   **🩸 Hemolytic Classification on Blood Agar:**
    -   **α-Hemolytic** (Partial hemolysis, greenish hue): *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Streptococcus viridans*.
    -   **β-Hemolytic** (Complete clear zone of hemolysis): Categorized into Lancefield groups.
    -   **γ-Hemolytic** (Non-hemolytic): *Enterococcus* species.

-   **Classification Systems for β-Hemolytic Streptococci:**
    -   **Lancefield Classification:** Based on :: cell-wall Group C Carbohydrate antigen. Divides strains into 20 groups designated A through V (excluding letters I and J).
<!--SR:!2026-08-31,2,230-->
    -   **Griffith Typing:** Based on :: surface M protein types. Subdivides Group A streptococci into ~100 M serotypes (1 to 100).

#### Hippurate Hydrolysis Test

##### Definition & Principle

Detects the activity of the enzyme hippuricase, which hydrolyzes sodium hippurate into glycine and benzoic acid.

Rapid Method (Ninhydrin): Glycine is converted using ninhydrin to deep purple / violet complex.

##### Procedure (Rapid Method)

1.  **Preparation:** Prepare a heavy suspension of the test organism (from a fresh 18-24 hour culture) in sterile water or sodium hippurate medium. Caution: Avoid picking up agar, as its protein content can cause interference.
2.  **Incubation:** Incubate the tube for 2 hours at 35-37°C (water bath preferred).
3.  **Development:** Add 0.2 mL of ninhydrin reagent to the mixture.
4.  **Observation:** Re-incubate for an additional 10–30 minutes and observe for the development of a deep purple or violet color.

##### Clinical Uses & Significance

The test is primarily used for the presumptive identification of:
?
-   *Streptococcus agalactiae*: Group B are hippurate positive.
-   *Campylobacter jejuni*: Distinguishes *C. jejuni* (positive) from *C. coli* (negative).
-   *Listeria monocytogenes* and *Gardnerella vaginalis*.
<!--SR:!2026-09-02,2,170-->

##### Interpretation & Limitations

-   **Positive:** Development of a deep purple/violet color within 30 minutes.
-   **Negative:** No color change, or a faint blue-gray color.

### Alpha-Hemolytic Streptococci: 🩸 S. pneumoniae vs. S. viridans
#### Steptococcus pneumoniae
- **Morphology:** Gram-positive, flame-shaped / lanceolate diplococci occurring in pairs with a single capsule enveloping each pair. 95 capsular serotypes exist.
    -   **Capsule Demonstration:** India Ink (negative staining) and Quellung Reaction (capsule swell).
    -   **Culture Appearance:** Blood agar displays appearence like :: ==Carom Coin appearance or Draughtsman appearance (colonies with central umbonation/depression and concentric ringed edges).==

### Gamma-Hemolytic Streptococci: 🩸 Enterococcus

-   **Morphology:** Gram-positive pairs, Spectacle / Goggle appearance.
-   **Culture:** Gamma-hemolytic (non-hemolytic) on blood agar.
-   **Biochemical Identification:** :: Bile Esculin Test Positive (hydrolyzes esculin in presence of 40% bile to produce black/brown precipitate).
<!--SR:!2026-09-03,5,210-->
-   **Survival in Extreme Environments:** Grows in 6.5% NaCl, 40% bile, temperatures up to 60°C, and high alkaline pH up to 9.6.
