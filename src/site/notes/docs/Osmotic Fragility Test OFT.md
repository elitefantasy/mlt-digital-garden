#haematology 

**Introduction to the Osmotic Fragility Test (OFT)** The **Osmotic Fragility Test (OFT)** is a hematological test used to evaluate the resistance or fragility of Red Blood Cells (RBCs) when they are exposed to varying concentrations of hypotonic solutions.

**Mechanism of RBC Hemolysis**

* **Isotonic Environment:** When RBCs are placed in **Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl)**, they are in an **isotonic solution**. Because the concentration of the fluid outside matches the inside of the cell, fluid easily passes back and forth without harming the cell, resulting in **no hemolysis**.  
* **Hypotonic Environment:** If RBCs are placed in a **hypotonic solution (such as distilled water)**, which lacks a sufficient sodium concentration, fluid will rapidly rush into the RBCs. This causes the cells to **swell and ultimately burst (hemolyze)**, making them fragile. The OFT measures exactly at what hypotonic concentration this bursting begins and ends.

**Normal Osmotic Fragility (Healthy RBCs)**

* **Cell Shape:** Healthy adult RBCs have a **biconcave disc-like structure**, which makes their cell membranes strong and resilient against immediate hemolysis.  
* **Hemolysis Range:** When healthy RBCs are exposed to gradually decreasing concentrations of hypotonic solutions, **hemolysis begins at 0.48% (or 0.45%)** and reaches **complete hemolysis at 0.36%**.

**Increased Osmotic Fragility** Osmotic fragility is considered "increased" when RBCs burst much earlier than normal (in slightly hypotonic solutions).

* **Associated Condition:** This is most prominently seen in **Spherocytosis**. Mainly is test ko isi ke liye krte he   
* **Mechanism:** In spherocytosis, the RBC loses its biconcave shape and becomes round or oval (a spherocyte). As a result, the **cell membrane becomes extremely weak**. Because it is already weak, introducing it to even a slightly hypotonic solution causes it to burst quickly.  
* **Hemolysis Range:** In a patient with spherocytosis, hemolysis **starts early at 0.70%** and reaches **complete hemolysis at 0.45%**.

**Decreased Osmotic Fragility** Osmotic fragility is considered "decreased" when RBCs are highly resistant to bursting and only hemolyze in severely hypotonic solutions.

* **Associated Conditions:** Decreased OFT is characteristically observed in patients suffering from **Sickle Cell Anemia, Iron Deficiency Anemia, and Thalassemia**.

* **Mechanism:** In these specific anemias, the RBCs take on abnormal shapes (like the crescent moon shape in sickle cell anemia or smaller sizes in iron deficiency). This abnormal shaping causes the cell membrane to **shrink and feel structurally stronger or more resistant** to fluid penetration. Because the membrane is shrunken and tough, the fluid struggles to enter and burst the cell.  
* **Hemolysis Range:** In these conditions, hemolysis **starts much later at 0.36%** and does not reach **complete hemolysis until 0.24%**.
