## Defination and agent
- vector-borne protozoal disease caused by _Leishmania_ species and transmitted by the bite of infected **female sandflies**.
- three major clinical forms are: visceral, cutaneous, and mucocutaneous leishmaniasis
- In India, **visceral leishmaniasis (VL)** is commonly called **kala-azar**, meaning “black disease,” referring to the darkening of skin that may occur in chronic illness



## Important Species

|Clinical form|Important species|Main geographical distribution|
|---|---|---|
|Visceral leishmaniasis|_L. donovani_, _L. infantum_|India, East Africa, Mediterranean region, Latin America|
|Cutaneous leishmaniasis|_L. tropica_, _L. major_, _L. mexicana_ complex|Africa, Middle East, Mediterranean region, Americas|
|Mucocutaneous leishmaniasis|_L. braziliensis_ complex|Mainly Central and South America|
|Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis|Usually _L. donovani_|Indian subcontinent and East Africa|

## Morphology
_Leishmania_ occurs in two important forms:

|Feature|Amastigote form|Promastigote form|
|---|---|---|
|Other name|Leishman-Donovan body|Flagellate form|
|Location|Humans and other vertebrate hosts|Sandfly and culture medium|
|Shape|Round or oval|Elongated, spindle-shaped|
|Size|About 1–5 µm|About 10–20 µm|
|Flagellum|No free flagellum|Single free anterior flagellum|
|Site in humans|Inside macrophages of reticuloendothelial organs|Not normally seen in clinical specimens|
|Structures|Nucleus and kinetoplast|Nucleus, kinetoplast, and flagellum|

The **amastigote** contains a nucleus and a small rod-like **kinetoplast**, a key feature for microscopy. 



---

## Life Cycle

1. An infected female sandfly bites a human and injects **metacyclic promastigotes** into the skin.
2. Promastigotes are phagocytosed by macrophages.
3. Inside macrophages, promastigotes transform into **amastigotes**.
4. Amastigotes multiply by binary fission within macrophages.
5. In visceral disease, infected macrophages carry parasites to the spleen, liver, bone marrow, and lymph nodes.
6. Another sandfly takes a blood meal and ingests macrophages containing amastigotes.
7. In the sandfly gut, amastigotes change into promastigotes, multiply, and migrate to the proboscis.
8. The next bite transmits infective promastigotes to another human.

# Clinical Forms

## Visceral Leishmaniasis

Visceral leishmaniasis, also called kala-azar, is the most severe form. Without treatment, it is fatal in more than 95% of cases.

**Incubation Period**
Usually weeks to months; it may occasionally be longer.

**Clinical Features**
- Prolonged, irregular fever.
- Progressive weight loss and weakness.
- Massive splenomegaly.
- Hepatomegaly.
- Pallor due to anemia.
- Pancytopenia.
- Hyperpigmentation of skin in some patients.
- Lymphadenopathy, more frequent in African cases.
- Recurrent infections and bleeding tendency in advanced disease.

**Laboratory Findings**

|Investigation|Finding|
|---|---|
|Hemoglobin|Decreased|
|Total leukocyte count|Leukopenia|
|Platelet count|Thrombocytopenia|
|Peripheral smear|May show pancytopenia; parasites usually not seen|
|ESR|Often raised|
|Serum albumin|Decreased|
|Serum globulins|Increased|
|Albumin:globulin ratio|Reversed|
|Bone marrow/splenic aspirate|LD bodies may be demonstrated|

**Why Pancytopenia Occurs**
- Hypersplenism causes increased removal of blood cells.
- Bone marrow may be infiltrated by infected macrophages.
- Chronic infection suppresses hematopoiesis.
- Nutritional deficiency may contribute.



## Cutaneous Leishmaniasis
Cutaneous leishmaniasis is the most common form and affects the skin at the site of sandfly bite.

### Clinical Features
- Begins as a papule at the bite site.
- Gradually develops into a nodule or plaque.
- May ulcerate, producing a painless ulcer.
- Ulcer classically has raised indurated borders and a depressed central area.
- Usually occurs on exposed body parts: face, arms, hands, or legs.
- Lesions often heal spontaneously but can leave permanent scars.

Common names include **Oriental sore**, **Delhi boil**, **Baghdad boil**, and **Aleppo boil**.

A typical lesion may be a round ulcer with an inflamed raised border and central tissue loss.

---
## Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis
Mucocutaneous disease occurs mainly in the Americas and is commonly associated with the _L. braziliensis_ complex.

### Clinical Features
- May occur months or years after a skin lesion.
- Involves nasal, oral, pharyngeal, or laryngeal mucosa.
- Nasal stuffiness, epistaxis, ulceration, and tissue destruction may occur.
- Severe cases can cause destruction of the nasal septum and facial disfigurement.

---

## Post-Kala-Azar Dermal Leishmaniasis
**Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL)** is a dermal condition occurring after apparent cure from visceral leishmaniasis, particularly in India and East Africa.

### Clinical Features
- Hypopigmented macules.
- Erythematous papules.
- Nodules or plaques.
- Lesions commonly appear on the face, trunk, and upper limbs.
- Patient may appear otherwise well.
- Skin lesions contain parasites and can serve as a reservoir for transmission.

# Laboratory Diagnosis
**which aldehyde is commonly used in aldehyde test to detect visceral leishmaniasis**
?
The aldehyde test for visceral leishmaniasis is **Napier’s formol-gel test**. It uses **formaldehyde**, usually added as **commercial formalin (40% formaldehyde solution)** to the patient’s serum.
.
Formaldehyde reacts with the markedly increased serum gamma globulins in kala-azar, causing **opacification and jelly-like coagulation** of serum in a positive test. This is a nonspecific test because gamma globulins may also be raised in other chronic diseases

## Specimens

|Clinical form|Preferred specimens|
|---|---|
|Visceral leishmaniasis|Splenic aspirate, bone marrow aspirate, lymph node aspirate, peripheral blood in selected cases|
|Cutaneous leishmaniasis|Scraping from active ulcer edge, slit-skin smear, lesion aspirate, skin biopsy|
|Mucocutaneous leishmaniasis|Scraping, aspirate, or biopsy from mucosal lesion|
|PKDL|Slit-skin smear, skin biopsy, lesion aspirate|

## Direct Microscopy

### Principle

Demonstration of **amastigotes (LD bodies)** in macrophages or tissue macrophages confirms leishmaniasis.

### Staining

- Giemsa stain.
- Leishman stain.
- Wright stain.
- Hematoxylin and eosin stain in tissue sections.

### Microscopic Appearance of LD Bodies

- Round or oval bodies measuring about 1–5 µm.
- A large round nucleus.
- A small rod-shaped kinetoplast.
- Located inside macrophages or occasionally extracellularly.

Microscopy rapidly detects amastigotes but cannot reliably identify the parasite species. In cutaneous lesions, Giemsa-stained scrapings or biopsy impression smears are commonly used.

### Diagnostic Value of Aspirates in VL

|Specimen|Relative sensitivity|Major concern|
|---|---|---|
|Splenic aspirate|Highest|Risk of serious hemorrhage; only trained personnel should perform it|
|Bone marrow aspirate|Moderate|Safer than splenic aspiration|
|Lymph node aspirate|Variable|Useful when nodes are enlarged, especially in some regions|

A bone marrow smear may show clusters of amastigotes in macrophages.

## Culture

Clinical material can be inoculated into culture media such as:

- Novy–MacNeal–Nicolle (NNN) medium.
- Schneider’s insect medium.
- RPMI-based media.

In culture, amastigotes transform into **promastigotes**.

### Advantages

- Helps in isolation of parasite.
- May allow further identification or drug-susceptibility work.

### Limitations

- Slow.
- Risk of contamination.
- Requires specialized facilities.
- Less useful for urgent diagnosis.

---

## Serological Tests

Serology is particularly helpful in **visceral leishmaniasis** but has limited value in cutaneous and mucocutaneous disease.

|Test|Use / principle|
|---|---|
|rK39 rapid diagnostic test|Detects antibodies against recombinant K39 antigen; widely used for VL|
|Direct agglutination test|Detects anti-_Leishmania_ antibodies|
|ELISA|Detects antibodies in serum|
|Indirect fluorescent antibody test|Antibody detection|
|Western blot|More specialized antibody testing|

### Limitations of Antibody Tests

- Antibodies may remain positive after successful treatment.
- Cannot always distinguish past infection from active disease.
- May be less sensitive in immunocompromised patients, especially HIV infection.
- Not reliable for diagnosing cutaneous leishmaniasis.

---

## Molecular Tests

- Conventional PCR.
- Real-time PCR.
- Species-specific PCR.
- DNA sequencing in reference laboratories.

Molecular testing improves sensitivity and can identify species, which is important because species influences the clinical form and treatment choice

---

## Montenegro Skin Test

Also called the **leishmanin skin test**.

### Principle

It detects delayed-type hypersensitivity to _Leishmania_ antigen.

### Interpretation

- Usually positive in cutaneous and mucocutaneous leishmaniasis.
- Often negative during active visceral leishmaniasis due to depressed cell-mediated immunity.
- May become positive after cure of VL.
- Indicates exposure or cellular immune response, not necessarily active disease.

# Differential Diagnosis

## Visceral Leishmaniasis

|Condition|Distinguishing feature|
|---|---|
|Malaria|Parasites detectable in peripheral blood smear|
|Typhoid fever|Blood culture may be positive; splenomegaly usually less marked|
|Tuberculosis|Chronic fever with suggestive clinical and radiological features|
|Leukemia/lymphoma|Abnormal cells in peripheral smear or marrow examination|
|Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis|Fever, splenomegaly, cytopenias, high ferritin; requires specialized work-up|
|Cirrhosis with portal hypertension|Splenomegaly but different liver-related findings|

## Cutaneous Leishmaniasis

- Pyoderma.
- Tuberculosis verrucosa cutis.
- Lupus vulgaris.
- Fungal skin infection.
- Syphilitic ulcer.
- Skin malignancy.
- Traumatic ulcer.

# Treatment

Treatment depends on the form of disease, species, geographical region, severity, pregnancy status, immune status, and drug availability. Confirmed VL requires complete treatment promptly.

|Drug|Important use / point|
|---|---|
|Liposomal amphotericin B|Preferred option for many VL settings; effective and less toxic than conventional amphotericin B|
|Amphotericin B deoxycholate|Effective but more nephrotoxic and infusion-related adverse effects are common|
|Miltefosine|Oral drug; used in selected VL and cutaneous cases; teratogenic, so avoid in pregnancy|
|Paromomycin|Injectable aminoglycoside; used in some VL treatment regimens|
|Pentavalent antimonials|Sodium stibogluconate and meglumine antimoniate; resistance and toxicity limit use in some areas|
|Local therapy|May be used for selected simple cutaneous lesions; includes local heat, cryotherapy, or intralesional therapy|

### Important Adverse Effects

|Drug|Important adverse effects|
|---|---|
|Amphotericin B|Fever, chills, nephrotoxicity, hypokalemia, anemia|
|Miltefosine|Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, teratogenicity|
|Paromomycin|Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity risk|
|Pentavalent antimonials|Pancreatitis, cardiotoxicity, hepatotoxicity|

**Exam caution:** Drug regimen and dose vary by national guidelines. Do not write one fixed dose for all forms of leishmaniasis.

# Prevention and Control

- Early detection and complete treatment of cases.
- Vector control through residual insecticide spraying in endemic areas.
- Use of insecticide-treated bed nets.
- Wearing full-sleeved clothing, especially from dusk to dawn.
- Use of repellents and screens.
- Improving housing by plastering cracks and reducing sandfly resting places.
- Environmental sanitation and removal of organic waste.
- Control of reservoir animals where zoonotic transmission occurs.
- Surveillance for relapse, HIV co-infection, and PKDL.

Leishmaniasis is linked with poverty, poor housing, malnutrition, population movement, and weakened immunity; therefore, control requires both medical and public-health measures.

---

# Epidemiology in India

Kala-azar has historically been endemic mainly in **Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, and Uttar Pradesh**, while cutaneous disease has been reported in north-western parts of India.

India reached the WHO-defined public-health elimination threshold for kala-azar in 2023: fewer than 1 case per 10,000 population at the subdistrict level. Continued surveillance remains essential to prevent resurgence.
