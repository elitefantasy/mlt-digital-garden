# **Chromatography**

## **I. Introduction and Definition**

* **Definition:** Chromatography is a **chemical analysis laboratory technique** used to separate a mixture into its individual components. Historically, this separation was often done with the help of colors.

## **II. Principle and Purpose**

* **Basic Principle:** The fundamental principle of chromatography is that **different compounds within a mixture move at different rates** or speeds depending on their specific nature.  
* **Purpose:** This technique is primarily used to **separate, identify, and quantify** the individual components of a mixture.

## **III. The Two Phases of Chromatography**

Every chromatographic system relies on the interaction between two distinct phases:

1. **Stationary Phase:**  
   2. This phase remains fixed in one place and **does not move** during the process.  
   3. It acts as a support for the sample.  
   4. It can be either a **solid or a liquid** (e.g., gels).  
   5. *Common examples:* Silica gel, alumina, filter paper, columns, and thin-layer plates.  
6. **Mobile Phase:**  
   7. This phase **moves through the stationary phase**, conveying the sample or mixture along with it to help separate the components.  
   8. It is **always a fluid**, meaning it can be either a **liquid or a gas**.  
   9. *Common examples:* Water, liquid solvents, and gases like helium or nitrogen. The choice of mobile phase depends entirely on the type of chromatography being used.

## **IV. Types of Chromatography**

There are many variations of chromatography, each with its own specific applications and requirements \[2, 4\]. The main types include:

* **Gas Chromatography (GC)** (Uses a gas mobile phase like helium or nitrogen)  
* **Liquid Chromatography (LC)** (Uses a liquid solvent mobile phase)  
* **Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC)**  
* **High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)**

## **V. Factors Influencing Separation**

The separation of components in chromatography can be affected by various parameters, including:

* The type of **stationary phase**  
* The type of **mobile phase**  
* The **flow rate** of the mobile phase  
* The **temperature**

# 

## **VI. Detection and Output (Chromatogram)**

* Chromatography systems utilize **detectors** that record the rate of movement, often referred to as the elution time or difference time.  
* These detectors generate a **chromatogram**, which is a graphical representation that correlates signal intensity with time.  
* This graph provides highly valuable data regarding the **composition and concentration** of the sample.


## **VIII. Common Applications**

Chromatography is widely used across medical and scientific fields for tasks such as:

* Separating colors from dyes  
* Extracting and separating **drugs from blood** samples  
* Separating specific colors from pigments
