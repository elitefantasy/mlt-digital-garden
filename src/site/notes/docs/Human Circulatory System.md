**Study Notes: Human Circulatory System**

**1\. Overview of the Circulatory System** The human circulatory system is a network through which blood flows, comprising various blood vessels that participate in this process. The primary components of this vascular network include:

* **Arteries and Arterioles:** Carry blood away from the heart.  
* **Veins and Venules:** Carry blood back to the heart.  
* **Capillaries:** A microscopic network of vessels where the exchange of nutrients and gases occurs.

**2\. Major Types of Circulation** The body utilizes different pathways for blood circulation, primarily categorized into Systemic, Portal, and Capillary circulation.

* **Systemic Circulation:**  
  * Oxygenated blood exits the heart through the **arch of the aorta** and is supplied to every organ in the body via arteries.  
  * After delivering oxygen and nutrients, deoxygenated blood is collected from all body parts.  
  * This blood returns to the **right atria** of the heart via the **superior and inferior vena cava**.

**3\. Specific Organ Circulation: The Liver (Dual Blood Supply)** The liver is unique because it receives a dual blood supply through both portal and systemic circulation.

* **Portal Circulation (Nutrient Supply \- \~80% of blood):**  
  * Blood from the gastrointestinal tract (stomach, small intestine, large intestine) and the spleen absorbs nutrients post-digestion.  
  * Capillaries from these organs merge into a large vessel called the **hepatic portal vein**, which transports this nutrient-rich blood directly to the liver for distribution.  
  * This blood contains a normal concentration of urea.  
* **Systemic Circulation (Oxygen Supply \- \~20% of blood):**  
  * Because the liver is a living tissue, it requires oxygenated blood.  
  * Oxygenated blood travels from the heart \-\> thoracic artery \-\> abdominal artery \-\> **hepatic artery** \-\> liver.  
* **Venous Drainage and Urea Production:**  
  * The liver performs the **urea cycle**, converting highly toxic ammonia into urea.  
  * After the liver extracts oxygen and produces urea, the deoxygenated blood leaves the liver via the right and left **hepatic veins**.  
  * Because it directly carries blood away from the site of urea production, the **hepatic vein contains the highest concentration of urea in the human body**.  
  * The hepatic veins empty this blood into the inferior vena cava, which carries it back to the heart.

**4\. Specific Organ Circulation: The Kidneys (Renal Circulation)** The kidneys are responsible for filtering waste products, such as urea, from the blood.

* **Blood Entry:** Oxygenated blood containing a normal concentration of urea enters the left and right kidneys via the **renal arteries**.  
* **Filtration:** Once inside the kidney, the blood reaches the glomerulus within the kidney pyramids, where it is filtered. The urea and other waste products are separated into urine and sent down the **ureter** to the urinary bladder.  
* **Blood Exit:** The newly filtered blood leaves the kidneys through the **renal vein**, which then merges with the inferior vena cava.  
* **Urea Concentration:** Because the blood has just been purged of waste, the **renal vein contains the lowest concentration of urea in the entire circulatory system**.
