## Introduction
**Plasmodium** is a **protozoan parasite** that causes **malaria**. It is an **obligate intracellular parasite** of humans.

- **Definitive host:** Female _Anopheles_ mosquito
- **Intermediate host:** Human
- **Infective stage to humans:** **Sporozoite**
- **Infective stage to mosquito:** **Gametocyte**
- **Site of sexual reproduction:** Mosquito
- **Site of asexual reproduction:** Human

## Life cycle
![Pasted image 20260907191006.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260907191006.png)
**Mosquito bite → Sporozoites → Liver → Merozoites → RBC → Trophozoite → Schizont → RBC rupture → Merozoites**

Some parasites differentiate into **gametocytes**, which are taken up by another female _Anopheles_ mosquito.

In mosquito:
**Gametocytes → Gametes → Zygote → Ookinete → Oocyst → Sporozoites → Salivary glands**

The life cycle of _Plasmodium_ is divided into **two main phases**:
1. **Human phase — asexual cycle**
2. **Mosquito phase — sexual cycle**

### A. HUMAN PHASE
The developmental stages in humans are:
1. **Pre-erythrocytic schizogony**
2. **Exo-erythrocytic schizogony**
3. **Erythrocytic schizogony**
4. **Gametogony**

#### 1. Pre-erythrocytic Schizogony
This is the **first stage in humans** and occurs in the **liver**.

**Entry of sporozoites** : mosquito bites -> injects sporozoite into bloodstream
- **Sporozoite is the infective stage for humans.**
- Sporozoites circulate in blood for a short period.
- They rapidly reach the liver.

**Invasion of liver cells**
Sporozoites -> **hepatocytes** -> enlarge , hepatic schizonts

**Multiplication**
The nucleus of the schizont divides repeatedly, followed by division of the cytoplasm.
This process is called **schizogony**.
Large numbers of **merozoites** are formed inside the hepatic schizont.

**Release of merozoites**
merozoite release from hepatocyte and invade rbc

Important point
In **_P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_**, some parasites may remain dormant in the liver as **hypnozoites**. Their later reactivation causes **relapse**.



#### 2. Erythrocytic Schizogony
This is the **most important stage responsible for clinical malaria**.
It occurs inside the **RBCs**.

![Pasted image 20260910111147.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260910111147.png)

**Step 1 — Merozoite enters RBC**
Merozoites invade rbcs
merozoite develops into a **young trophozoite**.
there maybe 6-24 merozoites in rbc

**Step 2 — Ring form**
The young trophozoite appears as a **ring-shaped structure** and is therefore called the:
**Ring form**
It contains:
- Cytoplasm
- Central vacuole
- Chromatin/nuclear material

**Step 3 — Mature trophozoite**
The ring form grows into a **mature trophozoite**.
The parasite feeds on hemoglobin and produces **malarial pigment (hemozoin)**.

**Step 4 — Schizont**
The trophozoite develops into a **schizont**.
The nucleus divides repeatedly.
The cytoplasm then divides around the nuclei, producing multiple **merozoites**.

Step 5 — RBC rupture
The infected RBC ruptures and releases:
- Merozoites
- Hemozoin pigment
- Cellular debris
The merozoites invade fresh RBCs and repeat the cycle.

Sequence to remember:
**Merozoite → Ring form → Trophozoite → Schizont → Merozoites**

Repeated RBC rupture is responsible for the characteristic **malarial fever, chills and rigors**.

#### 3. Exo-erythrocytic Schizogony
resembles the pre erythrocytic cycle
**Exo-erythrocytic** means development **outside the erythrocytes (RBCs)**.
It includes multiplication of _Plasmodium_ in **tissue cells other than RBCs**, particularly liver cells.


The term is especially important in relation to:
- **Liver/tissue stages**
- Persistent tissue forms
- Relapse in _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ due to <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">hypnozoites</span>


#### 4. Gametogony
Not all merozoites continue the asexual erythrocytic cycle.
Some differentiate into **sexual forms called gametocytes**.

There are two types:
Male gametocyte: **Microgametocyte**

Female gametocyte: **Macrogametocyte**

These gametocytes circulate in peripheral blood.
They **do not undergo sexual reproduction in humans**.

When a female _Anopheles_ mosquito feeds on an infected person, it ingests the gametocytes.

Thus: **Gametocyte = infective stage for mosquito.**


### B. MOSQUITO PHASE
mosquito takes blood from infected person containing gametocyte
mosquito is the **definitive host**(sexual reproduction)


#### 1. Gametogenesis
Inside the mosquito's **stomach/midgut**
gametocyte -> maturation into male and female gametophyte

**Male gametocyte** -> exflagellation -> microgametes

**Female gametocyte** -> macrogamete

#### 2. Fertilization
 **Microgamete + Macrogamete → Zygote**

#### 3. Ookinete Formation
The zygote elongates and becomes a motile worm-like structure called the: **Ookinete**
The ookinete penetrates the mosquito's **midgut wall**.

#### 4. Oocyst Formation
After penetrating the gut wall, the ookinete comes to lie on the outer surface of the mosquito's midgut and develops into an: **Oocyst**

The oocyst increases in size.

#### 5. Sporogony
Inside the oocyst, repeated nuclear and cytoplasmic divisions occur.

Large numbers of **sporozoites** are produced.

This process is called: **Sporogony**

The mature oocyst eventually ruptures and releases sporozoites.


#### 6. Migration to Salivary Glands
The sporozoites migrate through the mosquito's body and reach the: **Salivary glands**
During the mosquito's next bite, these sporozoites are injected into another human.
The cycle begins again.




## Laboratory diagnosis
**Specimen:** Peripheral blood

#### A. Peripheral blood smear — main method
1. **Thick blood film**
    - More sensitive
    - Used mainly for **detection** of parasites.
        
2. **Thin blood film**
    - Better for **species identification** and parasite morphology.

**Stains:** Giemsa, Leishman or Field stain.

**Fluorescent microscopy**
- blood smear is stained with acridine orange
- nuclear dna of malarial parasite is stained green and cytoplasmic rna red

#### B. Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs)
?
Detect _Plasmodium_ antigens such as:
- **HRP-2** → histidine rich protein 2 mainly _P. falciparum_
- **pLDH** → plasmodium lactate dehydrogenase : Plasmodium species


#### C. Molecular methods

**PCR** can detect and identify _Plasmodium_ DNA, especially when microscopy/RDT results are unclear.

#### D. Other Tests
**Quantitative Buffy coat**
- blood collected in QBC Tube coated with fluorescent dye and is subjected to centrifugation
- after centrifugation QBT tube is examined directly under fluorescent microscope

### 5. Microscopic identification 

**P. falciparum**
- Multiple delicate ring forms may occur in one RBC
- Mature trophozoites usually absent from peripheral blood
- **Crescent/banana-shaped gametocytes**
    

**P. vivax**
- RBC enlarged
- Amoeboid trophozoite
- **Schüffner's dots**
- Usually one parasite/RBC

**P. malariae**
- Normal or slightly smaller RBC
- **Band-form trophozoite**
- Rosette/daisy-head schizont

**P. ovale**
- Enlarged, oval RBC
- **Fimbriated/irregular RBC margins**
- Schüffner's dots

### 6. Clinical features
Typical malaria may present with:
- Fever
- Chills and rigors
- Sweating
- Headache
- Weakness
- Anemia
- Splenomegaly
    

Severe _P. falciparum_ infection can cause **cerebral malaria, severe anemia, renal failure, hypoglycemia and other complications**.

## Treatment
- chloroquine for acute malaria
- mefloquine for chloroquine resistant strains
- artemisinin are newer antimalarial drugs
