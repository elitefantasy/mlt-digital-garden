## Occult blood test

An **occult blood test**, usually referring to the **fecal occult blood test (FOBT)**, detects small amounts of blood in stool that are not visible to the naked eye. It is commonly used to screen for **gastrointestinal bleeding** and colorectal cancer

### Main methods

1. **Guaiac FOBT (gFOBT/Hemoccult)**
    
    - Detects the **peroxidase activity of hemoglobin**.
    - Stool is applied to guaiac-impregnated paper.
    - Developer containing **hydrogen peroxide is added.**
    - A **blue color** indicates a positive reaction.
    - It may produce false results because it can react with dietary meat, some vegetables, vitamin C, and certain medications.
2. **Fecal immunochemical test (FIT/iFOBT)**
    - Uses antibodies against **human hemoglobin**.
    - More specific for lower gastrointestinal bleeding than gFOBT.
    - Usually requires fewer dietary restrictions.
    - FIT has largely replaced traditional guaiac testing in many screening programs.

### Principle of guaiac test

The heme portion of hemoglobin has pseudoperoxidase activity. In the presence of hydrogen peroxide, it oxidizes guaiac to form a blue-colored compound:

$\text{Reduced guaiac} + H_2O_2 \xrightarrow{\text{heme}} \text{oxidized blue guaiac}$


### Specimen and procedure

- Collect stool in a clean, dry container without urine or water contamination.
- Using the applicator, smear small amounts from different areas of the stool onto the test card.
- For traditional gFOBT, samples are commonly collected from multiple bowel movements according to the kit instructions.
- Apply the developer reagent in the laboratory or as instructed by the kit.
- Interpret the color change within the stated time limit.

### Interpretation

|Result|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Negative|No detectable blood in the tested sample|
|Positive|Blood or hemoglobin detected; the source must be investigated|
