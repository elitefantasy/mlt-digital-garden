Here are some exam-ready notes on blood components:

Blood is a specialized bodily fluid that delivers necessary substances to the body's cells and transports waste products away from those same cells. It is composed of:

*   **Plasma**: The liquid matrix of blood, making up about 55% of its volume.
    *   Primarily water (92%).
    *   Contains proteins (e.g., albumin, globulins, fibrinogen), electrolytes, hormones, nutrients, and waste products.
    *   Functions in transport, maintaining osmotic pressure, and blood clotting.

*   **Formed Elements**: Cellular components suspended in plasma, making up about 45% of blood volume.
    *   **Erythrocytes (Red Blood Cells - RBCs)**:
        *   Biconcave discs, anucleated (in mammals).
        *   Contain hemoglobin, a protein that binds oxygen.
        *   Function: Oxygen transport from lungs to tissues, and carbon dioxide transport from tissues to lungs.
        *   Produced in red bone marrow.
    *   **Leukocytes (White Blood Cells - WBCs)**:
        *   Involved in the immune system.
        *   Larger than RBCs, contain a nucleus.
        *   Categorized into:
            *   **Granulocytes**: Neutrophils, Eosinophils, Basophils (contain granules in cytoplasm).
            *   **Agranulocytes**: Lymphocytes, Monocytes (lack visible granules).
        *   Function: Defend the body against pathogens, remove dead cells, and participate in allergic reactions.
    *   **Thrombocytes (Platelets)**:
        *   Small, irregular-shaped cell fragments.
        *   Lack a nucleus.
        *   Function: Essential for hemostasis (blood clotting) by forming a plug and releasing factors that promote coagulation.
        *   Derived from megakaryocytes in bone
