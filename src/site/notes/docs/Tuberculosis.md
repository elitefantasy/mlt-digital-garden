# Tuberculosis (TB): Study Notes
![Pasted image 20260928081228.png\|362](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260928081228.png)

## Definition and cause
- caused by mycobacterium tuberculosis a slow-growing, non-motile, slender acid-fast bacillus
- **Pulmonary TB:** Disease in the lungs; the main infectious form.
- **Extrapulmonary TB:** TB outside the lungs, such as lymph nodes, pleura, meninges, spine, bones, abdomen, genitourinary tract, or skin.
- **Latent TB infection (LTBI):** The bacilli are present but inactive; the person has no symptoms and does not transmit infection.
- **Active TB disease:** Bacilli multiply and produce illness; pulmonary/laryngeal TB can spread through the air.

## Organism characteristics

| Feature            | _M. tuberculosis_                                                                            |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shape              | Slender, straight or slightly curved bacillus                                                |
| Staining           | Acid-fast bacillus (AFB); retains carbol fuchsin after acid-alcohol decolorization           |
| Cell wall          | Rich in lipids and mycolic acids                                                             |
| Motility           | Non-motile                                                                                   |
| Spores/capsule     | Non-spore-forming; no true capsule                                                           |
| Oxygen requirement | Strict aerobe                                                                                |
| Growth             | Very slow; culture may take weeks                                                            |
| Important complex  | _M. tuberculosis_ complex includes _M. tuberculosis_ and _M. bovis_ among related organisms. |

**Why acid-fast staining works:** The waxy, mycolic-acid-rich cell wall resists ordinary staining and decolorization. Heating with carbol fuchsin helps dye enter the cell; acid-fast bacilli remain red after acid-alcohol treatment.

## Transmission and risk factors

TB is spread mainly by inhalation of airborne droplet nuclei produced when a person with infectious pulmonary or laryngeal TB coughs, speaks, sneezes, or sings. It is **not usually spread** by sharing utensils, food, clothing, or touching surfaces.

Risk of progression from infection to disease is higher in:
- HIV infection or other immunosuppression
- Diabetes mellitus
- Malnutrition
- Tobacco use
- Chronic kidney disease/dialysis
- Silicosis
- Steroid or anti-TNF treatment
- Close household contact with infectious TB
- Infants, older adults, and people living in crowded or poorly ventilated settings.

## Pathogenesis

1. **Inhalation:** Droplet nuclei reach the alveoli.
2. **Phagocytosis:** Alveolar macrophages ingest bacilli, but bacilli can survive and multiply within macrophages.
3. **Primary focus:** A small lesion develops in the lung, often in the lower part of the upper lobe or upper part of the lower lobe.
4. **Ghon complex:** Primary lung focus plus involved hilar lymph nodes.
5. **Cell-mediated immunity:** T lymphocytes activate macrophages, producing granulomas.
6. **Granuloma formation:** The body walls off bacilli; central caseous necrosis may occur.
7. **Outcome:** Infection may heal, remain latent, progress to primary disease, or reactivate later.

A typical TB granuloma contains epithelioid cells, Langhans giant cells, lymphocytes, and central caseous necrosis.

## Clinical features

### Pulmonary TB
- Cough lasting 2 weeks or longer
- Sputum production
- Hemoptysis
- Fever, often low grade
- Night sweats
- Weight loss and anorexia
- Fatigue
- Chest pain or breathlessness

### Extrapulmonary TB

|Site|Common presentation|
|---|---|
|Lymph nodes|Painless cervical lymphadenopathy, sometimes sinus formation|
|Pleura|Pleural effusion, chest pain, dyspnea|
|Meninges|Headache, fever, vomiting, neck stiffness, altered sensorium|
|Spine (Pott disease)|Back pain, vertebral destruction, deformity, neurological deficits|
|Genitourinary system|Dysuria, frequency, sterile pyuria, infertility|
|Abdomen|Pain, ascites, intestinal obstruction, abdominal mass|
|Bone/joints|Chronic pain, swelling, restricted movement|
|Miliary TB|Fever, weight loss, diffuse organ involvement due to hematogenous spread|

## Laboratory diagnosis

WHO recommends rapid molecular tests as the initial diagnostic test in people with symptoms suggestive of TB.

### Specimens
- **Pulmonary TB:** Sputum, induced sputum, bronchoalveolar lavage, gastric aspirate in children.
- **Extrapulmonary TB:** Aspirate, tissue biopsy, pleural fluid, CSF, urine, pus, or other site-specific sample.

### Important diagnostic methods

| Test                                | Principle/use                                                       | Important point                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Ziehl-Neelsen stain                 | Detects acid-fast bacilli in sputum smear                           | Rapid but less sensitive               |
| Fluorescent microscopy              | Auramine-stained bacilli fluoresce                                  | Faster screening of many smears        |
| NAAT/CBNAAT                         | Detects TB DNA and may identify rifampicin resistance               | Rapid molecular test                   |
| Culture on Lowenstein-Jensen medium | Grows mycobacteria for confirmation and drug susceptibility testing | Slow; colonies may take weeks          |
| Liquid culture                      | Faster culture-based detection                                      | Requires specialized system            |
| Drug susceptibility testing         | Detects resistance to anti-TB drugs                                 | Crucial in suspected drug-resistant TB |
| Chest X-ray                         | Supports diagnosis and assesses extent                              | Cannot alone confirm TB                |
| Histopathology                      | Caseating granulomas; AFB may be demonstrated                       | Useful for tissue TB                   |

### Sputum microscopy: Ziehl-Neelsen staining

1. Prepare a thin smear from purulent sputum.
2. Air dry and heat fix.
3. Flood with strong carbol fuchsin and gently heat.
4. Wash with water.
5. Decolorize with acid-alcohol.
6. Counterstain with methylene blue.
7. Acid-fast bacilli appear **bright red/pink** against a **blue** background.

### Culture media

- **Lowenstein-Jensen medium:** Egg-based solid medium containing malachite green, which inhibits contaminants.
- Typical colonies are rough, tough, buff-colored.

## Latent TB versus active TB

|Feature|Latent TB infection|Active TB disease|
|---|---|---|
|Symptoms|Absent|May be present|
|Infectiousness|Not infectious|Pulmonary/laryngeal disease may be infectious|
|TST/IGRA|Usually positive|May be positive|
|Chest X-ray|Usually normal|May show infiltrates, cavities, nodules, or other changes|
|Sputum AFB/culture|Negative|May be positive|
|Treatment goal|Prevent future disease|Cure disease and stop transmission|

A positive tuberculin skin test or IGRA indicates TB infection, but active disease must be excluded by clinical evaluation and appropriate investigations.

## Tuberculin skin test

The Mantoux test uses purified protein derivative injected intradermally. It detects delayed-type hypersensitivity due to prior sensitization to TB antigens.

- Read after **48 to 72 hours**.
- Measure **induration**, not redness.
- A positive result supports infection but does **not** differentiate latent infection from active TB.
- False-positive reactions may occur after BCG vaccination or exposure to non-tuberculous mycobacteria.
- False-negative reactions may occur in severe illness, HIV, malnutrition, recent TB infection, or immunosuppression.

IGRA is a blood test that measures interferon-gamma release by sensitized T cells; it is another first-line test for TB infection.

## Treatment principles

TB is curable when the correct multi-drug regimen is taken regularly and completed. Standard drug-susceptible TB treatment commonly uses a 6-month combination regimen containing isoniazid and rifampicin with additional drugs during the intensive phase.

### First-line anti-TB drugs

|Drug|Key action|Major adverse effect|
|---|---|---|
|Isoniazid (H)|Inhibits mycolic-acid synthesis|Hepatitis, peripheral neuropathy|
|Rifampicin (R)|Inhibits bacterial RNA synthesis|Hepatitis, orange-red body fluids, drug interactions|
|Pyrazinamide (Z)|Active in acidic intracellular environment|Hepatotoxicity, hyperuricemia|
|Ethambutol (E)|Inhibits arabinogalactan cell-wall synthesis|Optic neuritis; impaired red-green color vision|
|Streptomycin (S)|Inhibits protein synthesis|Ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity|

**Exam point:** Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) is often given with isoniazid to reduce peripheral neuropathy risk, particularly in patients with malnutrition, diabetes, HIV, pregnancy, alcoholism, renal failure, or neuropathy.

### Why combination therapy is necessary

- Prevents emergence of drug resistance.
- Attacks bacilli in different metabolic states.
- Improves cure rates.
- Reduces relapse and transmission.

### Drug-resistant TB

- **RR-TB:** Rifampicin-resistant TB.
- **MDR-TB:** TB resistant to at least isoniazid and rifampicin.
- Drug resistance commonly develops through inadequate treatment, irregular drug intake, inappropriate regimens, poor-quality medicines, or transmission of resistant strains.

Drug-resistant TB requires rapid resistance detection, specialist-guided regimens, and strict adherence.
