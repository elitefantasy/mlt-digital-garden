### Definition & Overview
- Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria (PNH) is a rare, life-threatening, acquired clonal stem cell disorder.
- It is characterized by complement-mediated intravascular hemolytic anemia, recurrent thrombosis, and bone marrow failure.

### Etiology & Genetics
- **Acquired Genetic Mutation:** Caused by a somatic mutation in the hematopoietic stem cells within the bone marrow.
- **Non-Inherited:** Unlike hereditary spherocytosis or G6PD deficiency, PNH is **acquired** and not inherited.

### Pathophysiology
1. **Complement Regulatory Protein Defect:** Mutations lead to the deficiency of glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored complement-regulatory proteins on the cell surface, specifically **CD55** (Decay Accelerating Factor / DAF) and **CD59** (Membrane Attack Complex Inhibitory Factor / MAC-IF).
2. **Alternative Complement Pathway Activation:** A stimulus triggers the activation of the alternative complement pathway.
3. **Enzyme Cascades:** Activation proceeds via **C3 convertase** and **C5 convertase**.
4. **Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) Formation:** The unregulated cascade leads to the assembly of the Membrane Attack Complex (MAC) directly on the defective PNH red blood cell membrane.
5. **Intravascular Hemolysis:** The MAC lyses the PNH erythrocytes, leading to severe intravascular hemolysis.
6. **Nocturnal Exacerbation:** Hemolysis is characteristically pronounced or worse at night, resulting in the classic presentation of dark urine (hemoglobinuria) in the morning.

### Clinical Presentation
- **Triad of PNH Manifestations:**
    - **Intravascular Hemolysis:** Leads to episodic chronic anemia and classic dark urine (hemoglobinuria), most prominent in the morning.
    - **Thrombosis (Blood Clots):** Markedly increased risk of venous/arterial thrombosis at unusual sites.
    - **Bone Marrow Failure:** Impaired production of blood cells resulting in cytopenias.
- **Symptoms:**
    - Fatigue and weakness
    - Shortness of breath (dyspnea)
    - Dark-colored morning urine
    - Abdominal pain and back pain
    - Symptoms of thrombotic events
- **Physical Findings:**
    - Splenomegaly is variable (unlike hereditary spherocytosis where it is consistently present).
### Laboratory Diagnosis
**1. Complete Blood Count (CBC) & Smear**
- **Hemoglobin (Hb):** Decreased (moderate to severe anemia).
- **RBC Count & PCV:** Decreased due to hemolysis.
- **Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS):** Shows **normal RBC morphology** (no specific structural red cell abnormalities like spherocytes or bite cells).
- **Reticulocyte Count:** Increased as the marrow compensates for hemolysis, unless concomitant bone marrow failure restricts response.

**2. Urine Analysis**
- **Hemoglobinuria:** Classic presence of free hemoglobin in urine, giving it a dark appearance (particularly first morning void).
- **Urinary Urobilinogen:** Increased due to elevated bilirubin clearance.

**3. Biochemical Hemolysis Markers**
- **Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH):** Characteristically **very high (markedly increased)** due to direct intravascular erythrocyte destruction.
- **Indirect (Unconjugated) Bilirubin:** Elevated due to hemoglobin breakdown.
- **Serum Haptoglobin:** Depleted/markedly decreased due to binding of free intravascular hemoglobin.
- **Plasma Free Hemoglobin:** Elevated in circulation.

**4. Special Diagnostic & Confirmatory Testing**
- **Flow Cytometry (Gold Standard / Diagnostic Test):**
    - Evaluates the expression of GPI-anchored surface antigens on blood cells.
    - **Result:** Demonstrates a marked **decrease or complete absence of CD55 and CD59** on red blood cells, granulocytes, or monocytes.
