- Fungi include yeasts , mold , and dimorphic fungi   
- Eukaryotic organism    
- Medical mycology is the branch of medical microbiology that deals with   Study of disease causing fungi 
- Reproduce by spores

**Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic** 
* **Nucleus and Organelles**: 
	* Eukaryotic : nucleus bound nuclear membrane , membrane bound organelle ( ER, Golgi Aparatus , 80S Ribosome)
* **Cell Wall Composition**: 
	* Eukaryotic : rigi cell wall of **chitin (polymer of N-Acetylglucosamine) , glucans and mannans**
	* Prokaryotic : peptidoglycan
* **Cell Membrane Sterols**: 
	* Eukaryotic :  fungal cell membranes contain sterols (specifically **ergosterol**)
	* Prokarytic : sterol absent in bacterial cell membranes (with the rare exception of *Mycoplasma*).

### 4 Dimorphic fungi

## Taxonomical Classification

### 1 zygomycetes
![Pasted image 20260905114211.png\|314](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260905114211.png)
Zygomycetes (also historically referred to as Phycomycetes) 

* **Hyphal Structure**: only one with **non-septate (aseptate)** hyphae. resulting in a continuous, multinucleated cytoplasmic stream.  
* **Reproduction**:  
	* **Sexual Spores (Teleomorph)**: Zygospores and oospores.  
	* **Asexual Spores (Anamorph)**: Sporangiospores, which are produced endogenously inside a sac-like structure called a sporangium.  
* **Representative Genera** :: *Rhizopus*, *Mucor*, and *Absidia*.
<!--SR:!2026-10-08,33,278-->
* **Specialized Structures**: They are noted for having **rhizoids**, which are root-like, highly branched vegetative structures used to anchor the fungus and absorb nutrients.

### 2 Ascomycets
![Pasted image 20260905114329.png\|431](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260905114329.png)
* **Hyphal Structure**: narrow septate hyphae
* Include both yeast and filamentous fungi
* **Reproduction**:
	* **Sexual Spores (Teleomorph)**: They produce **ascospores**, which are generated endogenously within a specialized sac-like structure called an **ascus**.
	* **Asexual Spores (Anamorph)**: They reproduce asexually by producing **conidia**, which are formed exogenously.
* **Dikaryon Phase**: Similar to Basidiomycetes, Ascomycetes can exhibit a "dikaryon phase" (n+n state). This is a transitional condition where plasmogamy (fusion of cytoplasm) occurs, but karyogamy (fusion of nuclei) is delayed, resulting in cells containing two separate haploid nuclei.
* **Representative Genera**:  ==Trichophyton, Microsporum, and *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*==
<!--SR:!2026-09-10,3,250-->

### 3 Basidiomycetes 

* **Hyphal Structure**: They have septate hyphae.  
* **Reproduction**:  
  * **Sexual Spores** : They produce basidiospores, which are generated exogenously on a specialized structure called a basidium.  
* **Dikaryon Phase**: Similar to Ascomycetes, they can exhibit a "dikaryon phase" (n+n state).   
* **Representative Examples**: This class primarily includes mushrooms., cryptococcus neoformans.

### 4 Dueteromycetes 
- fungi imperfecti
- no sexual reproductive stage

**Key Characteristics:**
* **Hyphal Structure**:  septate hyphae.  
* **Reproduction**: asexually, primarily via conidia or budding. 
* **Clinical Importance**:   *Candida*, *Cryptococcus*, and Dermatophytes.

**Sub-classification:\[less important \]**  
Because they lack a known sexual stage, they are artificially grouped into three classes:

* **Blastomycetes**: Yeast-like budding forms, such as *Cryptococcus* and *Rhodotorula*.  
* **Hyphomycetes**: Mycelial molds with septate hyphae that reproduce asexually via conidia. This is the largest group of medically relevant fungi and is split into:  
  * *Dematiaceous Hyphomycetes*: Produce dark pigments (causing phaeohyphomycosis).  
  * *Hyaline Hyphomycetes*: Produce colorless or bright pigments (includes *Aspergillus* and Dermatophytes).  
* **Coelomycetes**: Produce conidia within specialized hyphal structures known as acervuli.

## Classification Based on Reproduction 

### I. Sexual Reproduction

Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of nuclei and the formation of specialized spores.

* **Class Zygomycetes**: Sexual spore is the **zygospore**; examples include *Rhizopus* and *Mucor*.  
* **Class Ascomycetes**: Sexual spore is the **ascospore**; examples include *Aspergillus* and *Candida*.  
* **Class Basidiomycetes**: Sexual spore is the **basidiospore**; example includes *Cryptococcus*.  
* **Class Deuteromycetes**: Sexual stage is not known (Fungi Imperfecti); examples include *Candida* and *Aspergillus*.

#### Steps of Sexual Reproduction
?
* **Plasmogamy**: Fusion of cytoplasm between two haploid cells. The cytoplasm merges but nuclei remain separate, resulting in the **dikaryotic stage (n+n)**.
* **Dikaryotic Stage**: Occurs between plasmogamy and karyogamy. Common in Ascomycetes and Basidiomycetes; can persist for long periods before nuclear fusion.
* **Karyogamy**: Fusion of two haploid nuclei to form a **diploid nucleus (2n)**. Occurs in specialized reproductive structures and marks the completion of nuclear fusion.
* **Meiosis**: Reduction division that restores the haploid state. The diploid nucleus undergoes division to produce haploid sexual spores, ensuring genetic variation. These spores germinate to form new fungal mycelium.
<!--SR:!2026-09-10,5,238-->

### II. Asexual Reproduction
* **Conidia**: Produced externally on conidiophores. Examples: *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*.  
* **Sporangiospores**: Produced inside a sporangium. Example: *Rhizopus*.  
* **Blastoconidia**: Budding spores. Example: *Candida*.  
* **Arthroconidia**: Formed by fragmentation of hyphae. Example: *Coccidioides*.  
* **Chlamydospores**: Thick-walled survival spores. Example: *Candida albicans*.


## Classification based on hyphal structure 
* **Septate Hyphae**  
	* **Definition**: These hyphae contain cross walls, which are known as septa.  
	* **Characteristics**: They are characterized as thin, branching hyphae.  
	* **Examples**: Found in *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, and Dermatophytes.  
* **Aseptate (Coenocytic) Hyphae**  
	* **Definition**: These hyphae lack septa and contain many nuclei within the cell structure.  
	* **Characteristics**: They are characterized as broad, ribbon-like, and non-septate hyphae.  
	* **Examples**: Found in *Rhizopus* and *Mucor*.  
* **Pseudohyphae**  
	* **Definition**: These are structured as chains of elongated, newly budded yeast cells that remain attached end-to-end without detaching.  
	* **Characteristics**: They form thread-like structures that feature clear, constricted junctions between the cells.  
	* **Example**: Found in *Candida albicans*.

2\. **KOH Preparation (10-20%)**  
* **Overview**: A simple method utilized for the detection of fungi.  
* **Principle**: The potassium hydroxide (KOH) dissolves keratin, leaving the fungal elements visible for microscopic analysis.  
* **Used for**: Skin scrapings, hair, and nail samples.
Procedure
1.  Place the clinical specimen on a clean glass slide.
2.  Add 1–2 drops of **10–20% KOH** (which digests background host keratin while leaving fungal cell walls intact).
3.  Apply a coverslip, gently warm/heat (optional), and allow to stand for 10–15 minutes.
4.  Examine systematically under 10× and 40× objectives for hyaline branching septate hyphae, arthroconidia, and hair invasion patterns

# Structure of Fungal Cells

![433](/img/user/Attachments/Structure%20of%20funal%20cell.png)

## 1\. Fungal Cell Wall 

The fungal cell wall is a rigid outer layer that typically ranges from 100 to 200 nm in thickness.

* **Primary Functions**:
	*  shape and structural integrity to the cell.
	* Protects from environmental stress and prevents osmotic lysis.
	* pathogenicity and immune recognition.
* **Major Structural Components**:
	?
	* **Chitin**: polysaccharide ;;; composed of a polymer of N-acetylglucosamine. It provides rigidity and strength  and serves as a common target for diagnostic studies and antifungal drugs.
	* **Glucans**: Primarily consisting of beta-1,3-glucan and beta-1,6-glucan. They are major wall components that maintain cell wall integrity and provide **elasticity**. Clinically, beta-glucan detection tests are utilized for diagnosing fungal infections.
	* **Mannoproteins**: Glycoproteins containing **mannose sugars** located on the **outer layer** of the cell wall. They function in host **cell adhesion**, act as antigenic determinants, and interact with the host's immune system.
	* **Other Components**: Glycoproteins, lipids, and minor amounts of proteins and pigments (such as melanin in certain fungi).
<!--SR:!2026-09-09,3,235-->

## 2\. Fungal Cell Membrane 

Located beneath the cell wall, the cell membrane manages internal cellular boundaries and external interactions.

* **Structure**: Composed of a phospholipid bilayer embedded with proteins and sterols.  
* **Functions**: Regulates the transport of nutrients, maintains cell integrity, and serves as a site for various metabolic activities.  
* **Ergosterol**: Unlike animal cell membranes that contain cholesterol, the primary sterol in the fungal membrane is ergosterol. It is essential for survival by maintaining membrane **fluidity and permeability**. Clinically, ergosterol is a primary **target for antifungal medications** such as Amphotericin B, azoles, and polyenes.

## 4\. Cytoplasm and Internal Organelles 

The cytoplasm is a semi-fluid matrix that houses organelles and serves as the primary site for metabolic reactions.

* **Nucleus**: Contains DNA and chromosomes; controls the growth and reproduction of the cell.  
* **Mitochondria**: The site of ATP production, providing energy for cellular activities.  
* **Ribosomes**: Fungal cells possess 80S ribosomes which are responsible for protein synthesis.  
* **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) & Golgi Apparatus**: Responsible for protein synthesis and transport.  
* **Vacuoles**: Serve as storage centers for nutrients, ions, and waste products.

## 5\. Structural Comparison: Fungi vs. Bacteria 

Fungal structures differ drastically from bacterial cell structures across several key features:

| Feature | Fungi | Bacteria |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Cell Type** | Eukaryotic | Prokaryotic |
| **Cell Wall** | Composed of Chitin and glucan | Composed of Peptidoglycan |
| **Cell Membrane Sterol** | Ergosterol present | Usually absent |
| **Nucleus** | Present | Absent |
| **Size** | Larger | Smaller |



# Fungal Growth Media 
name few media in which fungus can grow :: Sabouraud dextrose agar, Potato dextrose agar, Corn Meal agar , czapek dox agar , brain heart infusion
<!--SR:!2026-09-11,3,230-->

**1\. Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA)**
* **Overview:** The most commonly used primary isolation medium for fungi.  
* **Developed By:** Raymond Sabouraud.  
* **Composition:** Contains Peptone, Dextrose, and Agar.  
* **Characteristics:**  
	* Has an **acidic pH** of approximately 5.6, which helps inhibit bacterial growth while supporting fungi.  
	* Often supplemented with antibiotics (e.g., chloramphenicol, gentamicin) to further minimize bacterial contamination.  
* **Uses:** Primary isolation of yeasts and molds from clinical specimens such as skin, hair, and nails.

**2\. Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA)**
* **Composition:** Contains potato infusion and dextrose.  
* **Characteristics:** Supports rapid fungal growth and is specifically noted for enhancing **sporulation and pigmentation**.  
* **Uses:** Cultivation of molds and fungal identification in laboratory settings.

**3\. Corn Meal Agar (CMA)**
* **Characteristics:** A nutrient-poor medium.  
* **Uses:** Encourages the formation of characteristic fungal structures. It is particularly used for the identification of *Candida* species and the observation of chlamydospores.

**4\. Czapek-Dox Agar**
* **Composition:** Contains sodium nitrate as the nitrogen source and has a defined chemical composition.  
* **Uses:** Cultivation of *Aspergillus* and *Penicillium* species.

**5\. Brain Heart Infusion (BHI) Agar**
* **Composition:** An enriched medium prepared from brain and heart extracts.  
* **Characteristics:** Supports the growth of fastidious fungi.  
* **Uses:** Culturing pathogenic fungi from clinical specimens.
