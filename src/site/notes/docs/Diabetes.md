## **Diabetes Mellitus**

Diabetes Mellitus (DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistently elevated blood glucose levels (hyperglycemia). It arises either from a deficiency in insulin production by the pancreas or from cellular resistance to the action of insulin. 

**Diabetes Confirmatory ?**

* When fasting blood sugar exceeds **125 mg/dL** its diabetes
* Normal FBS is ==**70 to 110== mg/dL**

Note: myocardial infarctions (heart attacks) triggered by complications of diabetes are uniquely **painless (silent)**.

### **The Classic Symptom Triad (The 3 P's)**

When blood glucose levels rise significantly

1. **Polyuria:** Frequents or excessive urination.  
2. **Polydipsia:** Excessive, intense thirst.  
3. **Polyphagia:** Excessive hunger or increased appetite.

*Other associated constitutional symptoms include unexplained weight loss, generalized fatigue, and blurred vision.*

### **Core Classifications of Diabetes**

Diabetes manifests in several distinct clinical variations based on its underlying etiology:

* **Type 1 Diabetes:** An autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells. Leads to insulin deficiency or resistance.   
* **Type 2 Diabetes:**  
  * Insulin resistance or does not produce enough insulin. It is tightly linked to lifestyle factors, including obesity and sedentary habits, and is diagnosed predominantly in adults.  
  * NIDDM stands for **Non-Insulin-Dependent Diabetes Mellitus**, an older medical term now commonly called **Type 2 Diabetes**   
* **Gestational Diabetes:** during pregnancy and typically resolves spontaneously post-delivery.

### **Diabetes Mellitus vs. Diabetes Insipidus**

While both conditions present with profound fluid imbalances, their underlying pathophysiology and urinalysis profiles are entirely distinct:

| Feature | Diabetes Mellitus (DM) | Diabetes Insipidus (DI) |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Primary Definition** | Metabolic disorder with high blood glucose. | Disorder of water balance. |
| **Pathophysiology** | Insulin deficiency or insulin resistance. | Anti-Diuretic Hormone (ADH) deficiency (Central) or kidney unresponsiveness to ADH (Nephrogenic). |
| **Anatomical Origin** | Pancreatic islet dysfunction. | Posterior pituitary or renal tubular dysfunction. |
| **Urinary Changes** | **Polyuria & Glycosuria** (glucose present in urine). | **Severe polyuria** with **no glucose** present in the urine. |
| **Specific Gravity** | **High** specific gravity due to solute load. | **Low** specific gravity (highly dilute urine). |

### **Laboratory Diagnostics: Glycosuria and Ketonuria**

#### **1\. Glucose in Urine (Glycosuria)**

Normally, the kidneys completely reabsorb filtered glucose via the Proximal Convoluted Tubules (PCT), resulting in glucose-free urine. However, the renal threshold for glucose rests between **160 to 180 mg/dL**. When blood glucose breaches this threshold, the PCT becomes saturated, causing glucose to spill over into the urine.

* **Benedict's Qualitative Test:** Utilizes copper sulfate. Glucose acts as a reducing agent, converting blue copper sulfate into a red-brown cuprous oxide precipitate upon boiling.  
* **Reagent Strip Method:** Leverages a specific enzymatic reaction cascade utilizing glucose oxidase, peroxidase, and a color-changing chromogen pad.

#### **2\. Ketone Bodies (Ketonuria)**

When cellular glucose utilization is compromised due to insulin deficiency, the body accelerates fat metabolism for energy, yielding ketone bodies. Severe accumulation can culminate in life-threatening **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)**.

* **Rothera's Test:** Specifically detects acetone and acetoacetic acid. The addition of sodium nitroprusside and liquid ammonia yields a distinct purple ring at the liquid interface.  
* **Reagent Strip Method:** Employs an alkaline pH pad that shifts to a purple or magenta hue in the presence of ketones.
