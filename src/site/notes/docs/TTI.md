# Transfusion-Transmissible Infections

## Definition

**Transfusion-transmissible infections (TTIs)** are infections that may be transmitted from a donor to a recipient through transfusion of whole blood or blood components.



A donor may be infectious during the **window period**, when the infection is present but the selected screening marker is not yet detectable. Generally, nucleic acid appears first, followed by ==antigen and then antibody==

## TTI Detection Markers

### 1. Nucleic acid

Nucleic-acid testing detects the genetic material of the pathogen:

- RNA, for example HIV RNA and HCV RNA.
- DNA, for example HBV DNA.
- Usually performed by NAT, including PCR-based methods.

**Advantages:**

- Detects infection early.
- Shortens the window period.
- Can detect infection before antibody or antigen becomes measurable.

**Limitations:**

- More expensive than routine serology.
- Requires specialized equipment and trained staff.
- May detect very low-level contamination unless quality control is excellent.

### 2. Antigen

An antigen is a component or product of the infectious organism.

Examples include:

- HIV p24 antigen.
- Hepatitis B surface antigen, or HBsAg.
- HCV core antigen.
- Malaria parasite antigens such as HRP-2 and pLDH.

Antigen testing usually becomes positive before antibody testing but after nucleic acid becomes detectable.

### 3. Antibody

Antibody testing detects the host immune response against the pathogen.

Examples include:

- Anti-HIV-1/2.
- Anti-HCV.
- Anti-HBc.
- Anti-_Treponema pallidum_.
- Anti-malaria antibodies.
- Anti-HTLV-I/II.

Antibody may remain positive after the infection has resolved, depending on the infection and the individual test. Therefore, an antibody-positive result does not always prove that active infection is present.

### Window period

The **window period** is the time between infection and the first reliable detection of a particular marker. A person may be infectious during this period despite a non-reactive test.

|Order of appearance|Marker|General meaning|
|---|---|---|
|First|Nucleic acid|Direct evidence of pathogen genetic material|
|Second|Antigen|Direct evidence of pathogen components|
|Third|Antibody|Immune response to infection|
