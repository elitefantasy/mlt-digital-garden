# Transfusion-Transmissible Infections

## Definition

**Transfusion-transmissible infections (TTIs)** are infections that may be transmitted from a donor to a recipient through transfusion of whole blood or blood components.



A donor may be infectious during the **window period**, when the infection is present but the selected screening marker is not yet detectable. Generally, nucleic acid appears first, followed by ==antigen and then antibody==

## TTI Detection Markers

### 1. Nucleic acid

Nucleic-acid testing detects the genetic material of the pathogen:

- RNA, for example HIV RNA and HCV RNA.
- DNA, for example HBV DNA.
- Usually performed by NAT, including PCR-based methods.

**Advantages:**

- Detects infection early.
- Shortens the window period.
- Can detect infection before antibody or antigen becomes measurable.

**Limitations:**

- More expensive than routine serology.
- Requires specialized equipment and trained staff.
- May detect very low-level contamination unless quality control is excellent.

### 2. Antigen

An antigen is a component or product of the infectious organism.

Examples include:

- HIV p24 antigen.
- Hepatitis B surface antigen, or HBsAg.
- HCV core antigen.
- Malaria parasite antigens such as HRP-2 and pLDH.

Antigen testing usually becomes positive before antibody testing but after nucleic acid becomes detectable.

### 3. Antibody

Antibody testing detects the host immune response against the pathogen.

Examples include:

- Anti-HIV-1/2.
- Anti-HCV.
- Anti-HBc.
- Anti-_Treponema pallidum_.
- Anti-malaria antibodies.
- Anti-HTLV-I/II.

Antibody may remain positive after the infection has resolved, depending on the infection and the individual test. Therefore, an antibody-positive result does not always prove that active infection is present.

### Window period

The **window period** is the time between infection and the first reliable detection of a particular marker. A person may be infectious during this period despite a non-reactive test.

|Order of appearance|Marker|General meaning|
|---|---|---|
|First|Nucleic acid|Direct evidence of pathogen genetic material|
|Second|Antigen|Direct evidence of pathogen components|
|Third|Antibody|Immune response to infection|

# Syphilis

## Overview

Syphilis is a sexually transmitted infection caused by the spirochete _Treponema pallidum_ subsp. _pallidum_.

It can also be transmitted through:

- Transfusion of infected blood.
- Direct contact with infectious lesions.
- Mother-to-child transmission.

The organism may be present in blood, especially during early infection. It is relatively fragile and sensitive to heat; storage below 20°C for more than 72 hours markedly reduces its infectivity. However, screening remains important because some blood components, particularly platelets, are stored at higher temperatures.

## Treponemal and Non Treponemal Tests
?
### Specific assays

Specific treponemal tests detect antibodies directed against _T. pallidum_.

Examples:

- TPHA: _Treponema pallidum_ haemagglutination assay.
- TPPA: _Treponema pallidum_ particle agglutination assay.
- FTA-ABS: fluorescent treponemal antibody absorption test.
- Treponemal EIA or CIA.
- Rapid treponemal antibody tests.

These tests are more specific than non-treponemal tests but may remain positive for life after infection. They cannot reliably distinguish active infection from previously treated infection.

For blood screening, WHO identifies **TPHA or treponemal EIA** as suitable specific approaches.

### Non-specific assays

Non-treponemal tests detect antibodies against lipid antigens, particularly cardiolipin-containing antigens released during cellular injury.

Examples:

- VDRL: Venereal Disease Research Laboratory test.
- RPR: Rapid plasma reagin test.

Characteristics:

- Usually correlate better with active disease.
- Titres often decline after successful treatment.
- Useful for disease activity and treatment follow-up.
- May be negative during very early infection.
- May produce biological false-positive results.
- Less sensitive than treponemal tests in some stages.

### Comparison

| Feature                                 | Treponemal tests                     | Non-treponemal tests                                  |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Target                                  | Specific anti-_T. pallidum_ antibody | Reagin against lipoidal/cardiolipin antigens          |
| Examples                                | TPHA, TPPA, FTA-ABS, EIA             | VDRL, RPR                                             |
| Main use                                | Confirmation and blood screening     | Activity assessment and treatment monitoring          |
| Positivity after treatment              | May remain lifelong                  | Usually declines                                      |
| False positives                         | Less common                          | More common                                           |
| Recommended for routine blood screening | Yes                                  | Mainly where incidence is high or according to policy |
