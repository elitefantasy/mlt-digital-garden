# FNAC
FNAC stands for Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology. It is a minimally invasive diagnostic procedure in which a thin needle is used to collect cells from a lump, swelling, cyst, or mass for microscopic examination.

  

It is widely used because it is quick, simple, inexpensive, and usually causes minimal discomfort.

  

## Very short revision points

  

- FNAC = Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology.
    
- Used for cytological diagnosis of masses.
    
- Needle size is usually 22–25 gauge.
    
- Common sites: thyroid, breast, lymph node.
    
- Advantages: simple, quick, minimally invasive.
    
- Limitation: does not give tissue architecture.
    

  

## 1. Definition

  

FNAC is a technique in which cells are aspirated from a lesion using a fine needle and syringe and then examined cytologically to determine whether the lesion is benign, inflammatory, infective, or malignant.

  
  

## 2. Aim / Purpose

  

FNAC is performed to:

- Diagnose the nature of a swelling or mass.
    
- Distinguish benign vs malignant lesions.
    
- Detect infection, inflammation, cysts, and metastatic disease.
    
- Obtain material for additional tests such as special stains or molecular studies in selected cases.
    

  
  

## 3. Common sites for FNAC

  

FNAC is commonly done for:

- Thyroid nodules
    
- Breast lumps
    
- Lymph nodes
    
- Salivary gland swellings
    
- Soft tissue masses
    
- Superficial cysts
    
- Deep lesions under imaging guidance
    

  
  

## 4. Principle

  

A thin needle is inserted into the lesion, and suction is applied to collect cells. The aspirated cells are spread on glass slides, stained, and examined under a microscope by a cytopathologist.

  
  

## 5. Equipment required

  

- Fine needle, usually 22–25 gauge
    
- Syringe
    
- Syringe holder/aspiration gun
    
- Clean glass slides
    
- Antiseptic solution
    
- Gloves and sterile drape
    
- Local anesthetic, if needed
    
- Fixatives and stains
    

  
  

## 6. Procedure of FNAC

  

### Step 1: Patient preparation

- Explain the procedure and obtain consent.
    
- Check history of bleeding tendency or anticoagulant use.
    
- Position the patient properly depending on the lesion site.
    

  

### Step 2: Site preparation

- Clean the skin with antiseptic solution.
    
- Use sterile draping.
    
- Local anesthesia may be given, though it is not always necessary.
    

  

### Step 3: Aspiration

- Insert a fine needle into the lesion.
    
- Apply negative pressure with the syringe.
    
- Move the needle to and fro within the mass to collect cells.
    
- Release suction before withdrawing the needle.
    

  

### Step 4: Smear preparation

- Expel the sample onto slides.
    
- Make thin smears.
    
- Fix or air-dry the smears depending on the stain to be used.
    

  

### Step 5: Staining and examination

- Slides are stained and examined under microscope.
    
- The pathologist reports the lesion as benign, suspicious, malignant, inflammatory, or inadequate.
    

  
  

## 7. Interpretation of FNAC

  

FNAC report may show:

- Benign
    
- Inflammatory/infective
    
- Suspicious
    
- Malignant
    
- Inadequate / unsatisfactory sample
    

  

The interpretation depends on cell pattern, nuclear features, cytoplasm, background material, and presence of atypia. [drlogy](https://drlogy.com/test/fnac)

  
  

## 8. Advantages of FNAC

  

- Simple and rapid
    
- Minimally invasive
    
- Low cost
    
- Outpatient procedure
    
- Little scarring
    
- Useful for preliminary diagnosis
    
- Can be repeated if needed
    

  
  

## 9. Limitations

  

- Only cells are obtained, not full tissue architecture.
    
- May not be sufficient in some cases such as lymphoma where histology is needed.
    
- False negative or inadequate samples may occur.
    

  
  

## 10. Contraindications

  

FNAC should be avoided or done carefully in:

- Severe bleeding tendency / coagulopathy
    
- Unsafe access window
    
- Suspected hydatid cyst
    
- Vascular lesions
    
- Infected field or lesions where sampling may worsen the condition
    
- Patients who cannot cooperate
    

  
  

## 11. Complications

  

FNAC is generally safe, but possible complications include:

- Mild pain
    
- Minor bleeding
    
- Bruising
    
- Infection
    
- Rare injury to nearby structures
    
- Rare vasovagal reaction
    

  
  

## 12. Uses in diagnosis

  

FNAC helps in diagnosing:

- Thyroid diseases
    
- Breast lumps
    
- Enlarged lymph nodes
    
- Salivary gland lesions
    
- Soft tissue tumors
    
- Tuberculous lymphadenitis
    
- Metastatic malignancy
