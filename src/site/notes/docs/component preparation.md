# Soft spin vs hard spin

**Soft spin (light spin)**

* **Definition**: Centrifugation at **lower speed and/or shorter time** to separate RBCs from **platelet‑rich plasma (PRP)** without pelleting platelets.  
* Typical ranges (conceptual exam level): about **2000 g (or RPM) for \~3–10 minutes at 20–22°C**  
* **Purpose**:  
  * From whole blood → **RBC layer \+ PRP** (used for platelet preparation).  
  * In buffy coat method → separate supernatant containing platelets from buffy coat.

**Hard spin (heavy spin)**

* **Definition**: Centrifugation at **higher speed and/or longer time**   
*  **5000 rpm for 5–7 minutes at 20–22°C** (platelets)  
* **Purpose**:  
  * PRP → hard spin → **platelet pellet \+ platelet‑poor plasma (PPP)** (platelet concentrate).  
  * Whole blood hard spin → **PRBC \+ plasma \+ buffy coat** (buffy coat method).  
  * Hard spin at cold temperature is also used in cryoprecipitate/plasma processing.

**COMPONENTS**

1. PRBC  
2. FFP  
3. Platelate concentrate  
4. Granulocyte concentrate  
5. cryoprecipitate

## 1\. Packed Red Blood Cells (PRBC)

**Preparation (manual whole‑blood–derived)**

* Whole blood collected in anticoagulant bag.  
* Either:  
  * **Single hard spin** → RBC bottom layer \+ plasma (if platelets not needed).  
  * Or **two‑step PRP method**:  
    * Soft spin: WB → **PRP \+ RBCs**.  
    * Remove PRP into satellite bag (for platelets/plasma).  
    * Add **additive solution** to RBCs (optional) → **PRBC**.

PRBC are stored at **1–6°C** in preservative solution (CPDA‑1 or additive) for up to about **==35–42 daysNote that cpda shelf life is 35 days and sagm along with cpda is 42 days.==**

**Main uses**

* **Anemia**  
* **hemorrhage**

## 2\. Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)

**Preparation**

* Plasma separated from whole blood by centrifugation (often with platelets removed).  
* Conditions: centrifuge at **5–7°C, 3000–4000 g for \~15–20 minutes** to obtain platelet‑poor plasma and avoid RBC damage.  
* Plasma is expressed into satellite bag and **frozen within 8 hours** of collection at **≤ –18°C or lower (often –30°C)**.

**Main uses**

* **coagulation factor deficiencies** (DIC, liver failure, massive transfusion coagulopathy).  
* **Rare isolated factor deficiencies** (e.g., factors V, VII, X, XI, XIII, AT III) when specific factor concentrates not available.  
* **Bleeding due to clotting disorders** where specific tests or factors are unavailable; temporary replacement.

Shelf life is :: 1 year when stored at –18°C or colder, but it can last up to 3 years if kept at –25°C or lower.

## 3\. Platelet concentrate

**Preparation (PRP )**

* Whole blood processed within \~6 hours.  
* **Step 1 – soft spin**: WB → **PRP \+ RBCs** (soft spin at \~2000 g, few minutes).  
* Express PRP into satellite bag; RBCs retained as PRBC.  
* **Step 2 – hard spin of PRP**: PRP → **platelet pellet \+ PPP** using higher speed (\~5000 g for 5–10 minutes at 20–22°C).  
* Remove PPP, leaving \~50–60 mL plasma suspending platelets → **platelet concentrate**.  
* Platelets stored at **20–24°C with continuous agitation**, shelf‑life is :: \~5–7 days.
<!--SR:!2026-09-15,17,250-->

(You may also mention **apheresis platelets** as machine‑collected single‑donor units. )

**Main uses**

* **Thrombocytopenia with bleeding** or high risk of bleeding (e.g., \<10–20 × 10⁹/L; higher thresholds pre‑surgery).  
* Platelet dysfunction (e.g., some anti‑platelet drugs, inherited platelet function defects) when bleeding.  
* Prophylaxis before invasive procedures in severely thrombocytopenic patients.

## 4\. Granulocyte concentrate

(Granulocytes are less common but important to know.)

**Preparation**

* Usually prepared by **apheresis** using cell separator after donor stimulation (e.g., G‑CSF ± steroids) to raise circulating neutrophil count.  
* Machine collects granulocyte‑rich fraction; other components returned to donor.

**Main uses**

* **Severe neutropenia** with life‑threatening infections unresponsive to antibiotics, especially in hematology/oncology patients.  
* Use is limited and specialized; not routine like PRBC/platelets.

## 6\. Cryoprecipitate

**Preparation**

* Start with **FFP**.  
* Thaw FFP slowly at **1–6°C**; during cold thawing, cold‑insoluble proteins precipitate (fibrinogen, factor VIII, vWF, factor XIII, fibronectin).  
* Cold‑insoluble precipitate is collected by **hard spin at low temperature** (e.g., \~5000 g for a few minutes).  
* Supernatant plasma removed; precipitate resuspended in small volume of plasma → **cryoprecipitate** unit; refrozen at :: ≤ –18°C.
<!--SR:!2026-09-04,15,290-->

**Main uses**

* **Hypofibrinogenemia or afibrinogenemia** (low fibrinogen) with bleeding.  
* Some **congenital factor deficiencies** (factor XIII, fibrinogen) when specific concentrates unavailable.  
* Historically used for **hemophilia A** (factor VIII), now mostly replaced by specific factor concentrates.

## Quick exam table to remember

| Component | Prepared by (basic) | Stored at | Main use (short) |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| PRBC | WB centrifugation (soft or hard spin) \+ additive  | 1–6°C | Anemia, blood loss  |
| FFP | Plasma separated, frozen within 8 h  | ≤ –18/–30°C | Coagulation factor deficiency  |
| Platelet conc. | WB → PRP (soft spin) → platelets (hard spin)  | 20–24°C, agitated | Thrombocytopenia/platelet dysfunction  |
| Granulocytes | Apheresis from stimulated donor  | 20–24°C, short shelf life | Severe neutropenic sepsis  |
| Cryoprecipitate | Cold thaw FFP → precipitate \+ hard spin  | ≤ –18°C | Low fibrinogen, factor XIII deficiency  |
