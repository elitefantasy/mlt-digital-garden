# Sites of Hematopoiesis by Age

* **Mesoblastic phase (yolk sac):** Starts early in embryonic life (around the 3rd week) and is the primary site of blood cell formation.  
* **Hepatic phase (liver and spleen):** The liver becomes the primary site of hematopoiesis from the 2nd to 5th month of gestation, with the spleen also contributing.  
* **Myeloid phase (bone marrow):** Bone marrow becomes the primary site of hematopoiesis starting from the 5th month of gestation and remains the principal site throughout adult life. In adults, active marrow is found primarily in the axial skeleton (vertebrae, ribs, sternum, pelvis, skull) and the proximal ends of long bones (femur, humerus).


# . General Properties of Blood

* **Volume:** A healthy adult typically has approximately 5 litres of blood, which accounts for roughly 8% of total body weight.  
* **Gender Variations:** Females generally have a slightly lower blood volume, averaging about 4.5 litres.  
* **Reaction and pH:** Blood is mildly alkaline with a consistent normal pH level of 7.4.  
* **Viscosity:** Blood is roughly five times more viscous than water, a property primarily attributed to the presence of red blood cells and plasma proteins.  
* **Specific Gravity:** The specific gravity for whole blood ranges from 1.052 to 1.061.

# 2\. Blood Components (Centrifugation Layers)

When blood is centrifuged with an anticoagulant, it separates into three distinct layers based on density:

* **Plasma (55%):** The top, clear, straw-colored liquid layer. It is composed of 91%–92% water and 7%–9% solids, including proteins (albumin, globulins, fibrinogen), electrolytes (sodium, potassium), nutrients, and metabolic waste.  
* **Buffy Coat (\<1%):** A thin, whitish-grey layer situated between the plasma and red cells. This layer contains the concentrated White Blood Cells (leukocytes) and platelets (thrombocytes).  
* **Erythrocytes/RBCs (44%):** The bottom red layer consisting of Red Blood Cells. The percentage of total blood volume occupied by these cells is known as the **Hematocrit** or **Packed Cell Volume (PCV)**.

###  **Artificial Blood & Oxygen Carriers**

**Perfluorocarbons (PFCs):** Perfluorocarbons are most commonly used as a **blood substitute**. Their primary function is to **help deliver oxygen to the tissues** in the absence of oxygen

## **Regulation of Erythropoiesis**

The production of red blood cells is a tightly controlled process to ensure oxygen delivery meets metabolic demands.

* **Primary Hormonal Regulator (Erythropoietin/EPO):**  
  * **Source:** Synthesized primarily by renal interstitial fibroblasts in the kidneys.  
  * **Trigger:** Stimulated by tissue hypoxia (low oxygen levels) via oxygen-sensing pathways.  
  * **Action:** Binds to erythropoietin receptors (EPOR) on erythroid progenitor cells, promoting their survival, proliferation, and differentiation.  
  * Erythropoietin (EPO) is a **glycoprotein hormone** with a peptide backbone and multiple carbohydrate side chains attached (N-linked and O-linked glycans)  
  * These covalently attached carbohydrate groups make it a **glycoprotein**, not a lipoprotein.  
  * “Conjugated protein” is a broad category that includes glycoproteins, lipoproteins, metalloproteins, etc., but in exams they specifically classify EPO as a **glycoprotein**.  
  *   
* **Iron Metabolism:**  
  * **Hepcidin:** Produced by the liver; acts as the master regulator of iron homeostasis by inhibiting iron absorption and release from storage (degrades ferroportin).  
* **Other**  
  * **Vitamin B12, and Folic Acid:** Maturation Factor  
  * **VitaminC**: helps in absorption  
  * **Iron and copper** : for heme synthesis

# **Leukopoiesis**

Leukopoiesis is divided into two main types:

1) **Myelopoiesis:** Includes granulopoiesis (basophils, eosinophils, neutrophils) and monopoiesis (monocytes).  
2) **Lymphopoiesis:** Includes the production of lymphocytes (T cells, B cells, NK cells).

## **Stages of Granulopoiesis**

1. **Myeloblast:** Large cell (≈14–20 µm), high N:C ratio, round/oval nucleus with fine chromatin and 2–4 nucleoli, scant light blue cytoplasm, no visible granules.  
2. **Promyelocyte:** Largest cell of neutrophil series, more cytoplasm, round nucleus with relatively immature chromatin and possible nucleoli, basophilic cytoplasm with abundant primary (azurophilic, non-specific) granules.  
3. **Myelocyte:** Medium cell (≈12–18 µm), eccentric round/oval nucleus with more condensed chromatin, nucleoli absent; cytoplasm less basophilic with appearance of secondary (specific) granules and reduction of primary granules.  
4. **Metamyelocyte:** Smaller cell with kidney/indented nucleus, condensed chromatin; abundant cytoplasm filled mainly with secondary granules, primary granules markedly reduced; no further division.  
5. **Band cell:** Horseshoe-shaped, non-segmented nucleus with condensed chromatin; abundant pink cytoplasm with secondary granules (immature neutrophil).  
6. **Segmented neutrophil:** Mature granulocyte with 3–5 nuclear lobes connected by thin filaments, very condensed chromatin, abundant pink cytoplasm rich in secondary (specific) granules.

## **Stages of Monopoiesis**

Monopoiesis (monocytopoiesis) progresses through three morphologically recognizable stages: monoblast → promonocyte → monocyte → tissue macrophage, each with characteristic nuclear and cytoplasmic changes.

Overview of stages  
1\. **Monoblast**  
   \- Earliest identifiable precursor of the monocyte series.  
   \- Large cell (about 12–20 µm), high nuclear–cytoplasmic ratio.  
   \- Nucleus: round/oval, fine chromatin, 1–4 nucleoli, central or slightly eccentric.  
   \- Cytoplasm: scant, light blue to slightly gray, agranular on routine smear.  
2\. **Promonocyte**  
   \- Next stage, slightly smaller than monoblast (≈15–20 µm).  
   \- Nucleus: irregular or indented/**kidney-shaped**, chromatin more condensed but still immature.  
   \- Cytoplasm: gray to pale blue, more abundant than monoblast; may contain **azurophilic (non-specific) granules** and occasional vacuoles.  
3\. **Monocyte (circulating mature cell)**  
   \- Large cell (≈15–30 µm), the largest normal leukocyte in peripheral blood.  
   \- Nucleus: round, oval, or kidney/horse-shoe shaped; more condensed chromatin, often eccentric.  
   \- Cytoplasm: abundant, dull gray-blue (“ground glass” appearance), variable azurophilic granules and vacuoles.  
   \- Functional features: phagocytic, part of the mononuclear phagocyte system; remains in blood \~20–40 hours before entering tissues.  
4\. **Macrophage / Histocyte (tissue form)**  
   \- Derived from monocytes after they migrate into tissues.  
   \- Larger than blood monocytes, with abundant cytoplasm rich in lysosomes and other inclusions; nucleus typically round to oval.  
   \- Highly phagocytic, long-lived, key in antigen presentation and tissue cleanup.

## **Core stages of lymphopoiesis**

1\. **Lymphoblast**  
    \- Earliest recognizable precursor in the lymphoid series.  
    \- Large cell, high nucleus–cytoplasm ratio.  
    \- Nucleus: round/oval, fine chromatin, 1–2 (sometimes more) nucleoli.  
    \- Cytoplasm: scant, deeply basophilic, usually agranular on routine smear.  
2\. **Prolymphocyte**  
    \- Intermediate stage between lymphoblast and mature lymphocyte.  
    \- Slightly smaller than lymphoblast, with more cytoplasm.  
    \- Nucleus: round, chromatin more condensed than lymphoblast, typically **single prominent nucleolus**  
    \- Cytoplasm: basophilic, more abundant than in lymphoblast, generally agranular or with very few non-specific granules.  
3\. **Mature lymphocyte**  
(Seen in peripheral blood as small and large lymphocytes; functionally includes B, T, and NK cells.)  
    \- **Small lymphocyte:**  
        \- Size: slightly larger than a red cell; high N:C ratio.  
        \- Nucleus: round, coarse clumped chromatin, no visible nucleoli.  
        \- Cytoplasm: scant, pale to sky-blue, clear, usually without visible granules.  
    \- **Large lymphocyte:**  
        \- Larger cell with more abundant pale blue cytoplasm.  
        \- Nucleus: may be slightly irregular; chromatin less coarse than small lymphocyte.  
        \- Cytoplasm: may contain a few azurophilic (primary) granules, especially in activated or NK cells.  
4\. **Plasma cell (terminal B-cell stage)**  
    \- Derived from activated B lymphocytes; responsible for antibody secretion.  
    \- Larger than small lymphocyte, abundant **deeply basophilic** cytoplasm.  
    \- Nucleus: eccentric, round/oval, with coarse “clock-face” chromatin; characteristic **paranuclear halo (Golgi zone)** adjacent to nucleus.

## **Developmental pathway context (brief)**

\- All lymphocytes arise from **hematopoietic stem cells → common lymphoid progenitor (CLP)** in bone marrow.  
\- **B cells** complete antigen-independent development (pro-B, pre-B, immature B, mature B) in bone marrow; **T cells** develop from CLP in thymus (double-negative, double-positive, single-positive stages).  
\- The simple morphological series *lymphoblast → prolymphocyte → mature lymphocyte → plasma cell* is what is usually required in classical hematology/practical exams.
