**\#hematology**

**Leukemia** 

**Introduction to Leukemia** Leukemia is fundamentally known as **blood cancer**. It is a hematological condition characterized by the uncoordinated, excessive formation and accumulation of abnormal White Blood Cells (WBCs) in the bone marrow and the bloodstream.

**Leukemia vs. Leukocytosis** While both conditions result in an abnormally high white blood cell count, there is a critical clinical distinction:

* **Leukocytosis:** Involves an increased count of completely **mature** WBCs.  
* **Leukemia:** Involves the uninhibited proliferation of **premature or immature** WBCs, which are clinically referred to as **"blast cells"**.

**Pathophysiology (Mechanism of Disease)** To understand leukemia, one must look at normal blood cell production (hematopoiesis). Normally, the bone marrow contains Pluripotent Hematopoietic Stem Cells (PHSC) that divide into two main lineages:

1. **Colony Forming Blastocytes (Myeloid Series):** These mature into Red Blood Cells (RBCs), Granulocytes (Neutrophils, Eosinophils, Basophils), Monocytes, and Platelets.  
2. **Lymphoid Stem Cells (Lymphoid Series):** These mature into Lymphocytes.

In a leukemic patient, an abnormality causes these stem cells to multiply excessively without undergoing the normal maturation process. As a result, huge amounts of immature "blast cells" accumulate in the bone marrow, disrupting normal cellular production, and eventually spill out in massive numbers into the systemic blood circulation.

**Classification of Leukemia** Leukemia is classified into four primary types based on two factors: the *series of cells affected* (Myeloid or Lymphoid) and the *rate of disease progression* (Acute or Chronic).

1. **Acute Lymphoid Leukemia (ALL):** Rapidly progressing, affecting the lymphoid series.  
2. **Chronic Lymphoid Leukemia (CLL):** Slowly progressing, affecting the lymphoid series.  
3. **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML):** Rapidly progressing, affecting the myeloid series.  
4. **Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML):** Slowly progressing, affecting the myeloid series.

**Age-Specific Prevalence** According to epidemiological trends noted in the text, specific types of leukemia predominantly affect different age groups:

* **Children:** The most common leukemia in the pediatric population is **Acute Lymphoid Leukemia (ALL)**.  
* **Adults:** The most prominent leukemia in adults (particularly noted in India) is **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**.  
* **Old Age (Elderly):** The most frequently diagnosed leukemia among the older population is **Chronic Lymphoid Leukemia (CLL)**.

**Key Diagnostic Differences: AML vs. CML** When evaluating the myeloid series, acute and chronic forms present differently in laboratory findings:

* **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML):** Because it is an acute and aggressive condition, the blood circulation will show a massive influx of entirely immature blast cells, typically constituting **20% or more** of the observed cells.  
* **Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML):** Because it progresses slowly, the initial blast cell count is lower (less than 10%). However, a key laboratory finding during the **accelerated phase of CML** is the presence of *differentiated* blast cells across the myeloid series. Specifically, a patient's blood smear will characteristically show an **increase in basophils alongside the blast cells**.
