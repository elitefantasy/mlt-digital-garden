## Introduction
- **Trypanosoma** is a **blood and tissue flagellate**.
- possess a special mitochondrial DNA-containing structure called the **kinetoplast**.

Two medically important groups are:
- **Salivaria:** Parasites transmitted in the saliva of a biting insect, e.g., _T. brucei_.
- **Stercoraria:** Parasites transmitted through insect feces contaminating the bite wound or mucosa, e.g., _T. cruzi_.

The major human pathogens are _Trypanosoma brucei gambiense_, _T. brucei rhodesiense_, and _Trypanosoma cruzi_.

### Morphology
- shape: Elongated, spindle-shaped, curved organism
- flagellum: Single flagellum arising near the kinetoplast
- undulating membrane: Wavy membrane formed by the flagellum attached to the body
- kinetoplast: Dark-staining mitochondrial DNA mass, usually posterior to the nucleus
- movement: Active, twisting and lashing movement

### Important forms:
- **Trypomastigote:** bloodstream form ; infective or diagnostic form depending on species.
- **Epimastigote:** Multiplying form in insect vector.
- **Amastigote:** without flagellum ; characteristic of _T. cruzi_ in human tissues.
- **Metacyclic trypomastigote:** Infective stage transmitted to humans.

## African trypanosomiasis | sleeping sickness | brucei
- africal sleeping sickness
- bite of tsetse fly (glossina)

| Feature                        | _T. b. gambiense_              | _T. b. rhodesiense_            |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Disease type                   | West African sleeping sickness | East African sleeping sickness |
| Reservoir                      | Mainly humans                  | Wild animals and cattle        |
| Course                         | Chronic, slow progression      | Acute, rapid progression       |
| Parasitemia                    | Usually low                    | Usually high                   |
| Posterior cervical lymph nodes | Common; **Winterbottom sign**  | Less prominent                 |
| CNS involvement                | Late                           | Early and severe               |

### T brucei life cycle
- tsetse bite -> injects **metacyclic trypomastigotes**
- enters blood and lymph nodes and become **bloodstream trypomastigote**
- multiply by binary fission(blood, lymph and later cerebrospinal fluid)
- another tsetse fly ingests **bloodstream trypomastigotes**
- multiple in midgut , migrate to salivary gland
- become **infective metacyclic trypomastigote**

### Clinical features
Stage 1
- fever, headache, weakness
- pruritis
- anemia
- edema
- hepatosplenomegaly
- **winterbottom sign**: Enlarged posterior cervical lymph nodes

Stage2
- CNS involve
- sleep cycle distrub
- confusion
- tremors

### Lab diagnosis
|Specimen|Use|
|---|---|
|Blood|Wet mount, thick film, thin smear, buffy coat examination|
|Lymph node aspirate|Demonstration of motile trypomastigotes|
|Chancre fluid|Useful in early rhodesiense infection|
|Bone marrow aspirate|May demonstrate parasites|
|CSF|Detects CNS involvement and helps stage disease|
|Serology|Screening, especially for gambiense disease|
|PCR|Sensitive confirmation and species identification|
**Microscopy:** Examine a fresh wet mount for actively motile parasites. Giemsa-stained thin or thick blood smears may demonstrate trypomastigotes.

## American Trypanosomiasis: Chagas Disease : cruzi
- transmited by triatomine bug(reduviid / kissing bug)
- this bug defecates near bite site, infective parasites in feces enter through broken skin, the bite wound

### life cycle of T.cruzi
- bug bite -> deposit feces -> **metacyclic trypomastigote**
- invade host cell especially macrophages
- transform into **amastigote** -> binary fission
- convert into **trypomastigote**, rupture cell , enters blood
- **bloodstream trypomastigotes** infect new cell , or **ingested by** bug
- in insect gut develop into **epimastigote** and later **metacyclic trypomastigotes**

The intracellular amastigote stage is the key difference between _T. cruzi_ and _T. brucei_ infection.

### clinical features
- fever, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegally, edema
- eye involvement
- myocarditis

### Lab diagnosis
- PBS, buffy coat, PCR
- demonstration of amastigote in biopsy
- In blood films, _T. cruzi_ trypomastigotes are typically C-shaped or U-shaped with a large posterior kinetoplast.

## Brucei vs cruzi

|Feature|_T. brucei_|_T. cruzi_|
|---|---|---|
|Disease|African sleeping sickness|Chagas disease|
|Vector|Tsetse fly (_Glossina_)|Triatomine/kissing bug|
|Transmission|Parasite injected in fly saliva|Parasite enters through bug feces|
|Main location in humans|Extracellular blood, lymph, CSF|Intracellular tissues plus bloodstream|
|Tissue form|No important amastigote stage in humans|Amastigote present in tissues|
|Main organ affected|CNS in late disease|Heart, GI tract, autonomic nervous system|
|Important sign|Winterbottom sign|Romaña sign|
|Blood parasite shape|Slender trypomastigote with undulating membrane|Often C- or U-shaped trypomastigote|



## Antigenic Variation
Very important mechanism of _Trypanosoma_.
- Trypanosomes can **change their surface glycoproteins**.
- These are called **Variant Surface Glycoproteins (VSGs)**.
- The immune system produces antibodies against one VSG.
- The parasite changes its VSG → **escapes immune response**.
- New antigenic variants appear approximately every **7–10 days**.
- A single parasite may possess **~1,000 or more VSG genes**.
