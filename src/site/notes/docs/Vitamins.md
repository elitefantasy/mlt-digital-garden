## I. Definition and General Characteristics

- **Definition:** Vitamins are essential organic compounds found in small quantities in natural foods. They do not undergo catabolism or direct metabolism to yield energy like carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids.

- **Primary Function:** Their major biological role is to act as organic catalysts that balance and regulate vital reactions (the biochemical processes occurring within our body's cells). If these vital reactions become unbalanced due to vitamin deficiencies, various pathological disorders arise.

- **Classification Basis:** They are categorized as **micromolecules (micronutrients)** due to the minute quantities required by the body daily.

- **Vitamers:** Organic compounds that are chemically and quantitatively similar to a specific vitamin, sharing the same biological activity and functions.  
  - Example: Retinol, retinal, and retinoic acid are all vitamers of Vitamin A.

## II. Historical Background & Nomenclature

### 1. Accessory Factors — Hopkins

Early biological experiments showed that a purified diet consisting solely of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, and minerals could not sustain animal growth in the way natural foods such as milk do.

Scientist **Hopkins** concluded that natural foods contained unknown essential trace substances, which he termed **"Accessory Factors"** or **"External Elements."**

### 2. Lunin's Discovery

Scientist **Lunin** (often referenced as Luming) is credited with early research establishing that these trace dietary compounds are vital to preventing disease states.

### 3. The Term "Vitamine" — Casimir Funk, 1913

In **1913**, scientist **Casimir Funk** isolated these accessory factors.

Believing that all such vital substances contained an amine chemical group, he merged:

- **Vital** — necessary for life
- **Amine** — chemical group

This resulted in the term **"Vitamine."**

### 4. Transition to "Vitamin"

Subsequent chemical analyses revealed that only a small portion of these compounds possessed amine groups. Consequently, the final letter **"e"** was dropped, establishing the term **"Vitamin."**

# III. Soluble Classification: Fat-Soluble vs. Water-Soluble

There are **13 essential vitamins** required by the human body. They are classified based on their **solubility** rather than their chemical structure.

| Feature | Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K) | Water-Soluble Vitamins (B-Complex & C) |
|---|---|---|
| **Solubility** | Soluble in fats, oils, and organic fat solvents (alcohol, ether, acetone, ethanol). | Soluble in water. |
| **Dietary Source** | Found in lipid-rich/fatty foods. | Found in grains, fruits, vegetables, and yeasts. |
| **Absorption & Transport** | Requires lipids and bile salts for absorption; requires specific carrier proteins. | Absorbed simply without lipids or carrier proteins (except for B12). |
| **Storage** | Stored heavily in the liver and adipose tissue. | Not stored in the body (major exception: Vitamin B12, which is stored in the liver). |
| **Excretion** | Not excreted in urine. | Readily excreted in urine when in excess. |
| **Toxicity** | Excess intake leads to accumulation and toxicity (Hypervitaminosis), especially with Vitamins A & D. | Very low toxicity risk due to regular renal excretion. |
| **Dietary Frequency** | Does not require daily replenishment; large single doses can maintain levels. | Requires continuous, regular supply in the daily diet. |

# IV. Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

## 1. Vitamin A — Retinoids / Retinol

### Chemical Name

- **Retinoids:** Class name.
- **Retinol:** Most common active vitamer.

### Sources

#### Animal Sources — Preformed Vitamin A

- Fish liver oil — richest source, specifically **Halibut liver oil**
- Liver
- Kidney
- Egg yolk
- Milk
- Cheese

#### Plant Sources — Pro-vitamin A / Carotenoids

- **Beta-carotene** is a pigment found in yellow and dark green vegetables and fruits.
- Sources include:
- Carrots
- Spinach
- Pumpkin
- Mango
- Papaya
- **Carrots** are the richest plant source.

### Golden Rice

- Genetically engineered rice rich in **beta-carotene** to combat visual impairment in malnutrition-prone countries.

### Functions

- **Vision:** Vitamin A/beta-carotene is processed by the liver to synthesize **rhodopsin**, a light-sensitive pigment in the eye's rod cells responsible for dim-light and night vision.
  - Cone cells handle color vision and are structurally distinct.

- **Immunity:** Promotes immune health. Often integrated into infant vaccinations since maternal breast milk is deficient in Vitamin A (unlike cow/buffalo milk).

- **Antioxidant:** Neutralizes free oxygen radicals.

- **Eye Moisture:** Crucial for the normal production of tears and keeping eye membranes moist.

### Deficiency Diseases

- **Night Blindness (Nyctalopia):** Failure of rod cells to adapt to dark/dim light.
- **Xerophthalmia:** Severe dryness and scaling of conjunctival membranes due to lack of tears.
- **Keratomalacia:** Softening and erosion of the cornea, leading to blindness.
- **Corneal Xerosis:** Cellular damage to the cornea.

## 2. Vitamin D — Calciferol / Cholecalciferol

### Chemical Name / Forms

- **Vitamin D2 (Ergocalciferol):** Commercial form derived from fungi/yeasts.
- **Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol):** Endogenous animal form.

### Endogenous Synthesis

Vitamin D is known as the **"Sunshine Vitamin."**

It is endogenously synthesized in the skin from **7-dehydrocholesterol** using cholesterol and the metal selenite/selenium as catalysts upon exposure to solar UV radiation.

Synthesis:

**Skin → Liver → Kidneys**

### Sources

- Fish liver oil, especially Halibut liver oil
- Fatty fish
- Egg yolk
- Meat
- Butter

> **Note:** Milk is a poor source of Vitamin D.

### Functions

- Acts as a **hormone** and is often called the **Hormone Vitamin**.
- Coordinates closely with **Parathyroid Hormone (PTH)**.
- Directly facilitates the absorption of **calcium and phosphorus** from the intestine into the bones and teeth, giving them strength and rigidity.

### Deficiency Diseases

- **Rickets:** Softening, thinning, and deformities of bones in children, delayed teething, and soft skull (cranium).
- **Osteomalacia:** Bone softening and generalized skeletal pain in adults.
- **Bowlegs (Bowlux):** Bending of weak femur bones into a rainbow shape under skeletal weight.
- **Knock-Knees (No-Knee Disease):** Inward collapse of weak leg bones causing knees to collide when walking.
- **Osteoporosis:** Progressive loss of calcium causing bones to develop large pores and become fragile.

---

## 3. Vitamin E — Tocopherol

### Chemical Name

- **Tocopherol**

### Nicknames

- **Beauty Vitamin**
- **Anti-sterility Vitamin**

### Sources

- Seed oils:
  - Wheat germ oil
  - Cotton seed oil
  - Peanut oil
  - Soybean oil
- Fish liver oil
- Coconut water
- Watermelon
- Pomegranate
- Leafy green vegetables

### Functions

- **Antioxidant:** Neutralizes toxic free oxygen radicals generated by cellular reactions, protecting cell membranes in the skin, heart, and eyes.
- Maintains skin glow and elasticity while preventing early wrinkles and premature aging.
- Protects **erythrocyte (RBC) membranes** from hemolysis.
- Essential for maintaining functional ovaries, egg cell (ova) maturation, and overall fertility.

### Deficiency Diseases

- **Infertility / Sterility**
- **Hemolytic/Megaloblastic Anemia:** Due to increased fragility of red blood cells.
- **Cataracts:** Accumulation of dead cells in the eyes.
- **Premature Aging / Heart Diseases**

---

## 4. Vitamin K — Phylloquinone / Menaquinone

### Chemical Name / Forms

- **Vitamin K1 (Phylloquinone):** Dietary form obtained from plant sources.
- **Vitamin K2 (Menaquinone):** Synthesized endogenously by bacterial microflora of the colon/large intestine.
- **Vitamin K3 (Menadione):** Synthetic, water-soluble variant of Vitamin K.

### Sources

- Cabbage
- Cauliflower
- Tomato
- Green leafy vegetables
- Spinach
- Egg yolk
- Meat
- Liver

### Functions

- Acts as a **co-enzyme** to initiate and accelerate blood clotting.
- Functions as a physiological catalyst to speed up the chemical reaction cascade that stops bleeding.

### Deficiency

- Severe impairment of clot formation, resulting in:
  - Prolonged bleeding
  - Elevated **Prothrombin Time (PT)**

# V. Water-Soluble Vitamins (B-Complex & C)

## 1. Vitamin C — Ascorbic Acid

### Chemical Name

- **Ascorbic Acid**

### Characteristics

- Highly heat-sensitive acid-form vitamin.
- Excessive cooking, prolonged heat exposure, or solar exposure easily destroys it.
- **Note:** Milk is a poor source of Vitamin C.

### Sources

- Citrus fruits:
  - Amla / Indian gooseberry — richest source
  - Guava
  - Orange
  - Sweet lime
  - Raw mango
  - Lemon
  - Grapes
  - Tamarind
- Green chillies
- Green vegetables

High levels are stored in the **adrenal glands and gonads**.

### Functions

- **Wound Healing:** Promotes recovery by supporting the synthesis of collagen fibers/protein in skin and connective tissue.
- **Antioxidant & Anti-carcinogenic:** Directly neutralizes carcinogenic elements and free radicals.
- **Immune Booster:** Serves as the most effective vitamin for boosting the immune system.
- **Iron Absorption:** Enhances the absorption of dietary iron in the bloodstream.

### Deficiency Disease

#### Scurvy

Marked by:

- Weakened capillary walls
- Loose teeth
- Bleeding gums
- Delayed wound healing
- Impaired immune responses

# 2. Vitamin B-Complex Group

### Mnemonic

> **Thoda Roj Nimbu Pani Pyo Bahut Fayada Karega**

| Mnemonic | Vitamin |
|---|---|
| **Thoda** | Thiamine (B1) |
| **Roj** | Riboflavin (B2) |
| **Nimbu** | Niacin (B3) |
| **Pani** | Pantothenic acid (B5) |
| **Pyo** | Pyridoxine (B6) |
| **Bahut** | Biotin (B7) |
| **Fayada** | Folic Acid / Folate (B9) |
| **Karega** | Cobalamin (B12) |

The B-vitamins primarily exist in **grains and cereals**.

> **Important:** Chopping vegetables before washing or over-washing grains such as rice can wash away these water-soluble vitamins.

# VI. Vitamins and Their Coenzymes

Most **B-complex vitamins** function as precursors to coenzymes, which are essential organic molecules that assist enzymes in catalyzing biochemical reactions.

**Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)** and **Vitamin C** generally do not function as coenzymes.

| Vitamin | Primary Coenzyme(s) | Biochemical Functions | Principle / Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1 (Thiamine)** | Thiamine Pyrophosphate (TPP) | α-ketoacid decarboxylation (e.g., pyruvate to acetyl-CoA), transketolation | Erythrocyte Transketolase Activity Assay |
| **B2 (Riboflavin)** | Flavin Adenine Dinucleotide (FAD) / Flavin Mononucleotide (FMN) | Electron transfer (redox) reactions, oxidative phosphorylation | Glutathione Reductase Assay |
| **B3 (Niacin)** | Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide (NAD⁺) / NADP⁺ | Essential electron carriers in catabolic/anabolic reactions (glycolysis, TCA cycle, lipid synthesis) | Urinary N-methylnicotinamide measurement |
| **B5 (Pantothenic Acid)** | Coenzyme A | Acyl-group transfer (fatty acid metabolism, TCA cycle, cholesterol synthesis) | Urinary pantothenate excretion measurement |
| **B6 (Pyridoxine)** | Pyridoxal Phosphate (PLP) | Amino acid metabolism (transamination, decarboxylation, deamination), neurotransmitter synthesis | Erythrocyte Aspartate Aminotransferase activation or Urinary Xanthurenic acid test |
| **B7 (Biotin)** | Biotin | Carboxylation reactions (CO₂ carrier), fatty acid synthesis, gluconeogenesis | Plasma biotin or urinary biotin/metabolite analysis |
| **B9 (Folic Acid)** | Tetrahydrofolate | One-carbon unit transfer, nucleotide and DNA synthesis | Histidine Load Test (FIGLU excretion) or Serum Folate Assay |
| **B12 (Cobalamin)** | Methylcobalamin / 5-Deoxyadenosylcobalamin | Methyl group transfer, odd-chain fatty acid metabolism, DNA synthesis | Methylmalonic Acid (MMA) test or Serum B12 Assay |
