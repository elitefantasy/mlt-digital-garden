## Purpose of cell blocking 

The main purpose of **cell blocking** in cytology is **to concentrate loose cells from fluid samples or needle aspirations into a paraffin-embedded pellet**. This allows pathologists to analyze liquid specimens using standard histological and molecular testing methods.

**Core Benefits**

* **Tissue Architecture:** Preserves cell clusters so doctors can see structural patterns similar to traditional biopsy sections.  
* **Advanced Testing:** Yields multiple sections for **immunohistochemistry (IHC)** and **molecular/genetic testing**.  
* **Better Accuracy:** Reduces false-negative results by capturing and utilizing all available cellular material from fine-needle aspirations (FNAs) and body fluids.  
* **Sample Archiving:** Stores the paraffin block safely for future re-testing or additional biomarker analysis.

# **Cytology fixatives**

| Fixative | Main use | Special point |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 95% Ethyl alcohol | Routine cytology smears | Most commonly used; in histology act as dehydration but in cytology act as fixative agent. |
| Methanol | Cytology, blood films | Good nuclear preservation |
| Carnoy’s fixative | Bloody cytology fluids | Lysing RBCs, good nuclear detail |
| Formalin / buffered formalin | Some cytology/histology work | More common in histopathology |
| Schaudinn’s fixative | Body fluids/cytology | Good morphology |

## **1\. Definition**

A fixative is a chemical agent that **stops autolysis and putrefaction**, preserves cell morphology, and prepares the specimen for staining and microscopic examination.

In cytology, the main goal is preservation of **individual cells and nuclear detail** rather than tissue architecture.

\*\*\*

## **2\. Qualities of a good cytology fixative**

A good fixative should:

* Preserve cell shape and nuclear detail.  
* Prevent drying artifact.  
* Rapidly penetrate the specimen.  
* Produce minimal distortion or shrinkage.  
* Allow good staining.  
* Be stable and easy to use.

\*\*\*

## **3\. Types of cytology fixation**

### **A. Wet fixation**

* The smear is placed immediately into fixative.  
* Common in **Papanicolaou (Pap) smears** and many non-gynecological cytology samples.

### **B. Spray fixation**

* Alcohol-based spray is used directly on the smear.  
* Helps prevent air-drying artifact.

### **C. Immediate immersion fixation**

* Slides are dipped into fixative solution at once.  
* Common in routine cytology labs.

***

## **4\. Common cytology fixatives**

### **1\. 95% Ethyl alcohol**

* **Most commonly used fixative in cytology.**  
* Excellent for preserving nuclear details.  
* Used for Pap smears and many cytology smears.

**Advantages**

* Rapid action  
* Good nuclear preservation  
* Easy to use

**Disadvantages**

* May cause some shrinkage  
* Needs proper wet fixation to avoid air-drying artifacts

\*\*\*

### **2\. Methanol**

Another alcohol fixative used for cytology smears and blood films. Preserves nuclear detail well.

\*\*\*

### **3\. Formalin / buffered formalin**

More commonly used in histopathology than routine cytology. Sometimes used for cytology specimens when tissue preservation is needed.

\*\*\*

### **4\. Carnoy’s fixative**

A special cytology fixative, especially useful for **blood-stained cytology fluids** because it **lyses RBCs**.  
Composition: **absolute ethanol \+ chloroform \+ glacial acetic acid** in the ratio **6:3:1**.

**Uses**

* Bloody smears  
* Body fluids  
* Nuclear preservation  
* Cytological preparations needing RBC removal

**Advantages**

* Rapid fixation  
* Good nuclear detail  
* Clears blood background by hemolyzing RBCs

\*\*\*

### **5\. Schaudinn’s fixative**

Used in some cytology/body fluid preparations. Good morphology for cellular detail.

\*\*\*

### **6\. Giemsa / May-Grünwald fixation-related methods**

In some air-dried cytology smears, methanol fixation is used before Romanowsky staining. Common in hematology and FNAC.

## **5\. Important fixative classification**

### **A. Nuclear fixatives**

Preserve nuclear detail well.

* Carnoy’s fixative  
* Clarke’s fluid  
* Formalin alcohol

### **B. Cytoplasmic fixatives**

Preserve cytoplasmic structures.

* Champy’s fluid

### **C. Histochemical fixatives**

Used when enzyme or special chemical reactions must be preserved.

* Buffered formalin  
* Cold acetone  
* Some special fixatives

\*\*\*

## **6\. Why fixation is important in cytology**

Without proper fixation:

* Cells dry out.  
* Nuclear detail becomes blurred.  
* Staining quality becomes poor.  
* False interpretation may occur.

Good fixation ensures:

* Better diagnosis  
* Better nuclear-cytoplasmic detail  
* Better background clarity
