### Definition & Overview
- inherited hemolytics anemia , small , spherical RBC

### Etiology & Genetics
- **Mode of Inheritance:**
    - **Autosomal Dominant:** Most common mode of inheritance, affecting males and females equally. "Autosomal" means the gene is on a regular, numbered chromosome (not a sex chromosome). "Dominant" means a person only needs one copy of changed gene
    - **Autosomal Recessive:** Rare cases, typically associated with more severe clinical disease.
- **Molecular Defect:**
    - Mutations affecting five key cytoskeletal and membrane proteins: **Spectrin** , **Ankyrin**, **Band 3**, **Protein 4.2**, (Associated linkage proteins)

### Pathophysiology
1. membrane protein disrupt
2. rbc lose membrane over time thus reduces surface area
3. spherocyte formation
4. splenic sequestration(rbc trap inside spleen)
5. premature destruction

### Laboratory Diagnosis
**1. Complete Blood Count (CBC)**
- Hb and RBC, PCV decreases
- **MCV & MCH:** Normal.
- MCHC increased due to cell surface decrease
- wbc and platelate normal
- **Reticulocyte Count:** increased
**2. Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**
- **Spherocytes:** 
- **Other Features:** Variable anisocytosis, poikilocytosis, and polychromasia (reticulocytosis).
- **Differential Considerations for Spherocytes:**
	- Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)
    - ABO Hemolytic Disease of the Newborn
    - Bacterial toxin exposure (e.g., _Clostridium_ infections)
**3. Osmotic Fragility Test (Key Diagnostic Screen)**
- Spherocytes have a reduced surface area-to-volume ratio, causing them to rupture at higher (less hypotonic) saline concentrations compared to normal discocytes.
- **Result:** **Increased osmotic fragility** (positive).
- (Note: Also increased in hereditary elliptocytosis and stomatocytosis; decreased in iron deficiency anemia and thalassemia).
**4. Direct Coombs Test (DAT / Direct Antiglobulin Test)**
- **Result:** **Negative**.
- **Purpose:** Essential to rule out autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA), which also produces spherocytes on blood film.
**5. Biochemical Markers of Hemolysis**
- **Serum Bilirubin:** Elevated indirect (unconjugated) bilirubin.
- **Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH):** Elevated due to continuous RBC destruction.
- **Urine/Fecal Urobilinogen:** Increased urinary urobilinogen and stercobilinogen excretion.
- **Serum Haptoglobin:** Decreased (consumed by binding free hemoglobin during hemolysis).
**6. Special & Confirmatory Tests**
- **Flow Cytometry (EMA Dye Binding Test):** Measures fluorescence intensity of eosin-5-maleimide bound to band 3/membrane proteins; detects decreased fluorescence in HS.
- **SDS-PAGE (Sodium Dodecyl Sulfate-Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis):** Analyzes RBC membrane cytoskeletal proteins to identify the specific defective protein (spectrin, ankyrin, band 3, or protein 4.2).

Note: aplastic crisis in hereditary spherocytosis is caused by parvovirus B19 infection

### Bone Marrow Examination
(Not routinely required for clinical diagnosis)
- Hypercellular marrow with prominent **erythroid hyperplasia**.
- Normoblastic maturation with a **decreased myeloid-to-erythroid (M:E) ratio**.
- Preserved, normal maturation of myeloid and megakaryocyte lineages.
