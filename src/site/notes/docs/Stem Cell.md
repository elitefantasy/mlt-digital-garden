# **Stem cells: exam note**

Stem cells are **undifferentiated cells** that can **self-renew** and **differentiate** into specialized cells.  

## **1\. Definition**

A stem cell is a cell with two key abilities:

1. 1\. **Self-renewal** — it can divide repeatedly and produce more stem cells.  
2. 2\. **Differentiation** — it can mature into specialized cell types.

Hematopoietic stem cells are stem cells that produce **all blood cell lineages** throughout life.  

## **2\. Properties of stem cells**

### **Main properties**

* **Self-renewal**: ability to maintain the stem cell pool over long periods.  
* **Potency**: ability to form one or more specialized cell types.  
* **Unspecialized nature**: they do not perform highly specialized functions like mature cells.  
* **Differentiation**: ability to become mature cells in response to signals.  
* **Clonogenicity**: one stem cell can generate a colony of cells.  
* **Quiescence**: many adult stem cells remain dormant until activated by injury or stress.

---

## **3\. Potency of stem cells**

Potency means the **range of cells** a stem cell can produce.

| Potency | Meaning | Example |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Totipotent** | Can form all embryonic and extraembryonic tissues | Zygote, early blastomeres |
| **Pluripotent** | Can form nearly all body cell types from the three germ layers | Embryonic stem cells, iPS cells |
| **Multipotent** | Can form multiple related cell types within one lineage | Hematopoietic stem cells |
| **Unipotent** | Can form only one mature cell type but still self-renew | Tissue-specific stem/progenitor cells |

### **Easy memory trick**

* **Toti** \= total  
* **Pluri** \= many  
* **Multi** \= several within one family  
* **Uni** \= one

---

## **4\. Classification of stem cells**

### **A. Embryonic stem cells**

* Derived from the **inner cell mass of blastocyst**.  
* They are **pluripotent** and can form tissues from all three germ layers.  
* High research value, but ethical issues and tumor risk are important.

### **B. Adult / somatic stem cells**

* Present in adult tissues such as bone marrow, skin, intestine, and other organs.  
* Usually **multipotent**, with more limited differentiation than embryonic cells.  
* Important for tissue repair and regeneration.

### **C. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)**

* Mature adult cells reprogrammed back to a pluripotent state.  
* Behave like embryonic stem cells in many respects.  
* Very important in regenerative medicine and research.

---

## **5\. Sources of hematopoietic stem cells**

The major clinical sources are:

* **Bone marrow** — classic and major source for transplantation.  
* **Peripheral blood** after mobilization — widely used now.  
* **Umbilical cord blood** — rich source, especially useful in pediatric transplantation.

---

## **6\. Location of hematopoietic stem cells**

### **In embryo/fetus**

* Yolk sac  
* Liver  
* Spleen  
* Later, bone marrow becomes dominant.

### **In adult**

* **Bone marrow** is the main site of hematopoietic stem cells.  
* They are mainly found in the marrow niche, especially in flat bones and axial skeleton.

---

## **7\. Stem cell banking process**

Stem cell banking means **collecting, processing, testing, freezing, and storing stem cells** for future therapeutic use.

### **Main steps**

#### **1\. Collection**

* Most commonly **umbilical cord blood** is collected after birth.  
* Collection is done in a sterile blood bag from the clamped cord/placenta.

#### **2\. Processing**

* The unit is transported to the laboratory.  
* It is checked for volume, cell count, contamination, and quality.  
* Red cells and plasma may be reduced depending on the method used.

#### **3\. Testing**

* Screening for infectious diseases in mother/donor.  
* Cell viability testing.  
* HLA typing may be performed depending on banking policy.  
* Sterility and nucleated cell count are important.

#### **4\. Cryopreservation**

* Cells are mixed with a cryoprotectant, commonly **DMSO**, to prevent ice-crystal damage.  
* The sample is cooled in a controlled way before freezing.

#### **5\. Storage**

* Stored in liquid nitrogen tanks at very low temperature, usually **below \-150°C**.  
* At this temperature, cells can be preserved for long-term use.

---

## **8\. Types of stem cell banking**

### **1\. Public banking**

* Donated cord blood is stored in a public bank.  
* Units are available for **any patient** who is a match.  
* Similar to blood donation for community use.

### **2\. Private banking**

* Cord blood is stored for **personal/family future use**.  
* The family pays storage charges.  
* Also called **family banking**.

### **3\. Hybrid banking**

* A model combining public and private features.  
* Some portion may be stored for family use and some for public donation, depending on policy.

---

## **9\. Uses of stem cells**

### **Hematopoietic stem cells are used for:**

* Leukemia  
* Lymphoma  
* Multiple myeloma  
* Aplastic anemia  
* Bone marrow failure syndromes  
* Thalassemia and other inherited blood disorders  
* Immune deficiency and some autoimmune disorders  
* Recovery after high-dose chemotherapy or radiotherapy

### **General stem cell uses**

* Regenerative medicine  
* Tissue repair  
* Disease modeling  
* Drug testing  
* Gene therapy support

## **10\. Bone marrow transplant / hematopoietic stem cell transplant**

Bone marrow transplant is the transplantation of **hematopoietic stem cells** to restore normal blood cell production after marrow damage or disease.

### **Types**

**A. Autologous bone marrow transplant**

* The patient is both **donor and recipient**.  
* Cells are collected before treatment, stored, and then reinfused after high-dose chemotherapy/radiation.  
* Advantages: no graft-versus-host disease, no need for donor match.  
* Limitation: no graft-versus-tumor effect.

**B. Allogeneic bone marrow transplant**

* Stem cells come from **another person**.  
* Donor may be sibling, unrelated matched donor, or sometimes cord blood source.  
* Advantages: graft-versus-tumor effect.  
* Limitation: risk of graft-versus-host disease, rejection, need for HLA matching.

### **Procedure**

Bone marrow transplant, now more commonly called **hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT)**, is a procedure in which healthy stem cells are given to replace diseased or damaged marrow. The transplant is usually done after **conditioning therapy** with chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy.

**1\. Pre-transplant evaluation**

* Patient is assessed for diagnosis, organ function, infection status, and fitness for transplant.  
* HLA typing is done when a donor transplant is planned.  
* A **central venous catheter** is inserted into a large vein for infusion of cells, medicines, and blood products

**2\. Stem cell collection**

There are two main ways to collect stem cells:

**A. Peripheral blood stem cell collection**

* Donor or patient receives injections for several days to mobilize stem cells from bone marrow into blood.  
* Blood is then collected by **apheresis/leukapheresis**.  
* The machine separates stem cells and returns the remaining blood to the donor.

**B. Bone marrow harvest**

* Done in the operating room under **general anesthesia** or sometimes regional anesthesia/sedation.  
* A needle is inserted into the **posterior iliac crest** (back of hip bone).  
* Liquid marrow is aspirated from multiple punctures.  
* The collected marrow is filtered and may be frozen until use.

**3\. Processing and storage**

* Collected stem cells are filtered, tested, and processed.  
* If not used immediately, they may be **cryopreserved** and stored frozen.2

**4\. Conditioning regimen**

* Before transplant, the recipient receives **high-dose chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy**.  
* This destroys diseased marrow and suppresses the immune system to allow engraftment.

**5\. Infusion of stem cells**

* On transplant day, stem cells are infused through the central venous catheter.  
* The procedure is similar to a blood transfusion and usually takes 1 to 3 hours or longer depending on the product and type of transplant.

**6\. Engraftment and recovery**

* Transplanted cells travel through the bloodstream and settle in the bone marrow.  
* They begin producing new blood cells over the following days to weeks.  
* Patient is monitored for fever, infection, graft-versus-host disease, bleeding, and organ toxicity

## **12\. Very short revision points**

* Stem cells are **self-renewing and differentiating** cells.  
* Hematopoietic stem cells produce **all blood cells**.  
* Main sources: **bone marrow, peripheral blood, cord blood**.  
* Banking steps: **collection → processing → testing → cryopreservation → storage**.  
* Banking types: **public, private, hybrid**.  
* Bone marrow transplant types: **autologous and allogeneic**.
