### Definition & Overview
- G6PD deficiency is an inherited hemolytic anemia caused by an enzyme defect within erythrocytes.
- It impairs the red blood cell's ability to protect itself against oxidative stress, leading to episodic acute hemolysis.

### Genetic Basis & Variants
- **Inheritance:** **X-linked recessive** disorder (predominantly affects males, while females act as carriers or express variable severity due to lyonization).
- **Classification:** Classified by the World Health Organization into **five variants (Class I to Class V)** based on the level of enzyme activity and clinical severity.

### Biochemical Role & Pathophysiology
1. **HMP Shunt Role:** G6PD is the rate-limiting enzyme in the hexose monophosphate (HMP) shunt (pentose phosphate pathway).
2. **NADPH Generation:** It catalyzes the conversion of Glucose-6-Phosphate to 6-Phosphogluconate, reducing $\text{NADP}^+$ to $\text{NADPH}$.
3. **Glutathione Maintenance:** $\text{NADPH}$ is essential to maintain glutathione in its reduced form ($\text{GSH}$). Reduced glutathione neutralizes reactive oxygen species and free radicals inside the RBC.
4. **Oxidative Damage & Heinz Body Formation:** In G6PD deficiency, oxidative stress (e.g., infections, oxidant drugs, fava beans) depletes $\text{GSH}$. Hemoglobin undergoes oxidation and denatures into insoluble intracellular aggregates called **Heinz bodies**.
5. **Hemolysis:**
    - As RBCs containing Heinz bodies pass through splenic sinusoids, splenic macrophages "bite out" these precipitates, forming **bite cells** and **blister cells**.
    - The damaged RBCs suffer membrane compromise and are rapidly hemolyzed (mostly intravascular and extravascular hemolysis).


### Clinical Features
- **Episodic Hemolytic Anemia:** Hemolysis is typically triggered by exposure to oxidative agents, infections, or ingestion of fava beans.
- **Splenomegaly:** Usually **absent** (unlike membrane disorders like hereditary spherocytosis).
- **Hemoglobinuria:** May be present during acute, severe intravascular hemolytic episodes.
- **Neonatal Jaundice:** Can occur in affected newborns.
### Laboratory Diagnosis
**1. Complete Blood Count (CBC)**
- **Hemoglobin & Packed Cell Volume (PCV):** Moderate to severe drop in hemoglobin during an acute hemolytic episode.
- **RBC Count:** Decreased secondary to hemolysis.
- **Reticulocyte Count:** Markedly **increased** due to robust compensatory bone marrow erythropoiesis.

**2. Peripheral Blood Smear (PBS)**
- **Anisocytosis and Poikilocytosis:** Marked variation in RBC size and shape.
- **Polychromasia:** Bluish immature RBCs corresponding to high reticulocyte output.
- **Bite Cells / Blister Cells:** Characteristic RBCs where hemoglobin appears pushed to one side or has a margin "bitten" out by splenic macrophages.
- **Spherocytes:** Irregularly contracted cells and spherocytes may also appear during active hemolysis.

**3. Heinz Body Demonstration (Special Supravital Stain)**
- **Stain:** **Brilliant Cresyl Blue** (supravital stain).
- **Finding:** Identifies round, dark inclusions (Heinz bodies) adhering to the inner RBC membrane, composed of denatured hemoglobin.

**4. Screening Tests**
- **Fluorescent Spot Test:**
    - **Principle:** Blood is mixed with Glucose-6-Phosphate ($\text{G6P}$), $\text{NADP}^+$, and saponin, placed onto filter paper, and inspected under UV light.
    - $\text{G6P} + \text{NADP}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{G6PD}} \text{6-Phosphogluconate} + \text{NADPH}$.
    - $\text{NADPH}$ exhibits bright fluorescence under UV light.
    - **Result:** **Lack of fluorescence** indicates G6PD deficiency.
- **Methemoglobin Reduction Test:**
    - Evaluates the ability of RBCs to generate $\text{NADPH}$ and reduce methemoglobin back to hemoglobin.
    - _Important limitation:_ False-negative results can occur during acute hemolysis because older, enzyme-deficient RBCs are destroyed first, leaving a young reticulocyte-rich population with higher residual G6PD activity.
**5. Quantitative / Confirmatory Assay**
- **Quantitative G6PD Enzyme Assay:** Spectrophotometric measurement of $\text{NADPH}$ formation rate; serves as the **definitive test** to quantify exact G6PD enzyme activity levels.
**6. General Biochemical Hemolysis Markers**
- **Serum Indirect Bilirubin:** Elevated.
- **Serum Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH):** Elevated due to erythrocyte lysis.
- **Serum Haptoglobin:** Decreased / depleted.
- **Urinary Urobilinogen:** Elevated excretion.
