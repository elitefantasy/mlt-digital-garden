# Syphilis

## Overview

Syphilis is a sexually transmitted infection caused by the spirochete _Treponema pallidum_ subsp. _pallidum_.

It can also be transmitted through:

- Transfusion of infected blood.
- Direct contact with infectious lesions.
- Mother-to-child transmission.

The organism may be present in blood, especially during early infection. It is relatively fragile and sensitive to heat; storage below 20°C for more than 72 hours markedly reduces its infectivity. However, screening remains important because some blood components, particularly platelets, are stored at higher temperatures.

## Treponemal and Non Treponemal Tests
?
### Specific assays

Specific treponemal tests detect antibodies directed against _T. pallidum_.

Examples:

- TPHA: _Treponema pallidum_ haemagglutination assay.
- TPPA: _Treponema pallidum_ particle agglutination assay.
- FTA-ABS: fluorescent treponemal antibody absorption test.
- Treponemal EIA or CIA.
- Rapid treponemal antibody tests.

These tests are more specific than non-treponemal tests but may remain positive for life after infection. They cannot reliably distinguish active infection from previously treated infection.

For blood screening, WHO identifies **TPHA or treponemal EIA** as suitable specific approaches.

### Non-specific assays

Non-treponemal tests detect antibodies against lipid antigens, particularly cardiolipin-containing antigens released during cellular injury.

Examples:

- VDRL: Venereal Disease Research Laboratory test.
- RPR: Rapid plasma reagin test.

Characteristics:

- Usually correlate better with active disease.
- Titres often decline after successful treatment.
- Useful for disease activity and treatment follow-up.
- May be negative during very early infection.
- May produce biological false-positive results.
- Less sensitive than treponemal tests in some stages.

### Comparison

| Feature                                 | Treponemal tests                     | Non-treponemal tests                                  |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Target                                  | Specific anti-_T. pallidum_ antibody | Reagin against lipoidal/cardiolipin antigens          |
| Examples                                | TPHA, TPPA, FTA-ABS, EIA             | VDRL, RPR                                             |
| Main use                                | Confirmation and blood screening     | Activity assessment and treatment monitoring          |
| Positivity after treatment              | May remain lifelong                  | Usually declines                                      |
| False positives                         | Less common                          | More common                                           |
| Recommended for routine blood screening | Yes                                  | Mainly where incidence is high or according to policy |

## Wasserman Reaction
what is wassermann reaction and what it is used for
?
- The **Wassermann reaction** (Wassermann test) is a classic serological diagnostic test used to screen for **Syphilis** (caused by *Treponema pallidum*).
- based on **Complement Fixation (CFT)**. 
- detects non-specific antilipoidal antibodies (**reagin antibodies**) present in the serum of infected patients.
- **Antigen Source:** The test utilizes a non-treponemal lipoidal antigen (cardiolipin extracted from beef heart muscle) rather than the actual spirochete. When reagin antibodies bind to cardiolipin, they fix and consume complement in the test system.
- **Modern Non-Treponemal Tests:** In routine medical laboratory practice, the Wassermann test has been replaced by faster, simpler non-treponemal flocculation assays, specifically **VDRL** (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory) and **RPR** (Rapid Plasma Reagin).
- **Confirmatory testing**: A positive Wassermann, VDRL, or RPR screening test must be confirmed using a specific treponemal antibody test, such as the **FTA-ABS** (**Fluorescent Treponemal Antibody Absorption**) test.
