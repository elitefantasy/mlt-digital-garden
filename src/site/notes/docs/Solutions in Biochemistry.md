# **Solutions in Clinical Biochemistry**

## **Solution Fundamentals & Definitions**

* **Solution Definition**: A homogeneous physical mixture composed of two primary components: a solute and a solvent Solution \= Solute \+ Solvent  
* **Solute**: The solid, liquid, or gaseous substance that is dissolved in a solvent (e.g., glucose, sodium chloride, or sucrose).  
* **Solvent**: The liquid medium in which the solute is dissolved.  
  * **Distilled Water (DW)**: The primary and most essential solvent utilized in clinical biochemistry laboratories to avoid mineral and ionic interference.  
* **Illustrative Example**: In a sucrose (sugar) and water solution, sugar serves as the solute and distilled water serves as the solvent, resulting in complete dissolution.

## **Five Core Solution Types in Biochemistry**

The video outlines five basic solution categories widely tested in laboratory examinations:

1. **Normal Solution (N)**: A solution containing 1 gram equivalent weight of solute per liter of solution.  
2. **Molar Solution (M)**: A solution containing 1 gram molecular weight (1 mole) of solute per liter of solution.  
3. **Saturated Solution**: A solution holding the maximum possible concentration of dissolved solute at a specific temperature and pressure, where additional solute cannot dissolve.  
4. **Unsaturated Solution**: A solution containing less than the maximum equilibrium amount of solute, allowing additional solute to be dissolved without difficulty.

### **Molarity (M)**

* **Definition**: The concentration of a solution expressed as the total number of moles of solute dissolved per 1 liter (1 L) of solution.  
* **Formula**:  
  Molarity (M)= Moles of Solute / Volume of Solution in Liters (L)  
* **Gram Molecular Weight**: A 1 Molar (1 M) solution is prepared by dissolving 1 gram-molecular weight (1 mole) of solute in distilled water brought to a total volume of 1 Liter.

### **Molality**

The number of moles of solute present per kilogram (kg) of solvent.

### **Equivalent Weight**

Calculated by dividing the molecular weight of a compound by its valency.

### **Mole:** 

The standard SI unit for measuring the amount of a substance.

### **pH**

The logarithmic measure of hydrogen ion power/concentration in a solution, indicating acidity or alkalinity.

## **Practical/Lab Tips**

* **Solvent Choice**: Always use Distilled Water (DW) rather than tap water for preparing laboratory reagents, calibrators, and standards to prevent unwanted ion interactions and contamination.  
* **Solute vs. Solvent Identification**: Remember that the component present in a smaller proportion that dissolves is the solute, whereas the liquid present in a larger proportion that dissolves it is the solvent.

## **Buffer Systems & Laboratory Preparation Protocols**

### **Buffer Systems Mechanics**

Buffer solutions utilize weak acids and weak bases due to their lower concentration of ions. When extra acid or base is introduced, these ions conjugate (forming covalent bonds) to neutralize the addition, thereby maintaining pH stability. In human physiology:

> * **Bicarbonate System:** The primary and most common blood buffer system responsible for maintaining slightly alkaline blood pH.  
> * **Phosphate Buffer System:** Secondary physiological buffer system working in intracellular and renal fluid environments.

### **Step-by-Step Solution Preparation Protocol**

Basic formula : amount of solute \= desired concentration x volume of solution   
Eg: 500g \= 10% x 1000ml

> 1. Calculate the required mass or volume of solute for the target concentration and volume.  
> 2. Accurately measure solid solute using an analytical balance.  
> 3. Transfer the solute into a volumetric flask (designed specifically for accurate volume containment/delivery).  
> 4. Add a portion of solvent slowly and mix to dissolve (never pour water rapidly into concentrated aggressive acids).  
> 5. Dilute slowly up to the final volume graduation mark.

## **pH Measurement & Physiological Fluid Values**

The pH scale ranges from 0 to 14 (7 is neutral, \<7 is acidic, \>7 is alkaline). Because pH is logarithmic, a solution with a pH of 2 is **100 times more acidic** than a solution with a pH of 4\. Measurement tools include litmus paper, indicator strips, pH meters, and Blood Gas Analyzers (BGA).

## **Water Purification Techniques**

> * **Distillation Mechanism:** Boils water to produce steam, leaving non-volatile contaminants behind, then condenses steam into pure liquid water. Types include Simple, Fractional, and Double Distillation.  
> * **Distilled vs. Deionized Water:** Distilled water is free of both dissolved ions and microorganisms due to heat sterilization. Deionized water removes ions via exchange resins but may still contain microbial contaminants. Both serve as solvent bases for reagents.

### **Distillation**

Distillation is a **physical separation technique** used to separate liquids based on differences in boiling points. It works by heating a mixture to form vapor and then cooling the vapor to obtain the liquid again.

#### **Principle**

The principle of distillation is selective **vaporization and condensation**. The component with the lower boiling point evaporates first, and the vapor is then condensed to collect the purified liquid, called the distillate.britannica+2

#### **Working**

1. The mixture is heated in the distillation flask.  
2. The component with the lower boiling point vaporizes first.  
3. The vapor passes into the condenser.  
4. Cold water in the condenser cools the vapor.  
5. The vapor condenses into liquid and is collected as distillate.

#### **Types of Distillation**

* **Simple distillation:** Used when boiling points differ greatly or to separate a liquid from non-volatile impurities.  
* **Fractional distillation:** Used when liquids have close boiling points.  
* **Steam distillation:** Used for heat-sensitive substances like essential oils.  
* **Vacuum distillation:** Used for substances with very high boiling points.  
* **Destructive distillation:** Heating in absence of air to decompose material.courseware.cutm.ac+2

#### **Applications**

Distillation is used in:

* Preparation of distilled water.  
* Separation of alcohol from mixtures.  
* Petroleum refining.  
* Extraction of essential oils.  
* Purification of laboratory chemicals.byjus+2

#### **Advantages**

* Simple and effective method.  
* Useful for purification.  
* Can separate volatile liquids from non-volatile impurities.chem.libretexts+1

#### **Limitations**

> * Not suitable for liquids with very close boiling points unless fractional distillation is used.  
> * Some substances may decompose on heating.  
> * Not useful for separating all types of mixtures
