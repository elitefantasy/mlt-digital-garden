An **Electrocardiogram (ECG or EKG)** is a non-invasive graphic recording of the electrical potentials generated by the heart muscle during each cardiac cycle, detected via surface electrodes placed on the limbs and chest. It was invented by **Willem Einthoven** in **1903** using a string galvanometer.
## 1. Physiological Basis & Conduction Pathway

- **Conduction Sequence:** $\text{SA Node (70–80 bpm)} \rightarrow \text{Internodal Pathways} \rightarrow \text{AV Node (0.1 s physiological delay)} \rightarrow \text{Bundle of His} \rightarrow \text{Right \& Left Bundle Branches} \rightarrow \text{Purkinje Fibers}$
- **Vector Deflection Rules:**
	- Depolarization wave moving **toward** a positive electrode produces an **upward (positive)** deflection.
	- Depolarization wave moving **away** from a positive electrode produces a **downward (negative)** deflection.
	- Absence of net electrical activity produces a flat **isoelectric baseline**.
- **Terminology Distinction:**
	- **Wave:** Any positive or negative deflection from the baseline.
	- **Segment:** A flat isoelectric line *between* two waves (contains no waves).
	- **Interval:** A time duration that includes *at least one wave* plus a segment.

## 2. Standard 12-Lead ECG System

- **Electrode vs. Lead Count:** Uses **10 physical electrodes** (4 limb + 6 chest) to generate **12 electrical views** across two anatomical planes.
- **Frontal (Coronal) Plane — 6 Limb Leads:**
  - **Bipolar Standard Leads (Einthoven's Triangle):**
    - **Lead I:** Left Arm $(+)$ vs. Right Arm $(-)$
    - **Lead II:** Left Leg $(+)$ vs. Right Arm $(-)$
    - **Lead III:** Left Leg $(+)$ vs. Left Arm $(-)$
    - **Right Leg (RL):** Acts solely as a neutral ground electrode to reduce electrical interference.
    - **Einthoven's Law:** $\text{Lead II} = \text{Lead I} + \text{Lead III}$
  - **Augmented Unipolar Limb Leads:**
    - **aVR:** Right Arm $(+)$ — normally records inverted P, QRS, and T waves.
    - **aVL:** Left Arm $(+)$
    - **aVF:** Left Leg / Foot $(+)$
- **Horizontal (Transverse) Plane — 6 Precordial (Chest) Leads:**
  - $V_1$: 4th intercostal space (ICS), right sternal border.
  - $V_2$: 4th ICS, left sternal border.
  - $V_3$: Midway between $V_2$ and $V_4$.
  - $V_4$: 5th ICS, left mid-clavicular line.
  - $V_5$: Horizontal to $V_4$, left anterior axillary line.
  - $V_6$: Horizontal to $V_4$, left mid-axillary line.

## 3. ECG Graph Paper Calibration

- **Standard Paper Speed:** **25 mm/sec** (horizontal axis measures **time**).
- **Standard Voltage Calibration:** **10 mm/mV** (vertical axis measures **amplitude/voltage**).
- **1 Small Box ($1\text{ mm} \times 1\text{ mm}$):**
  - Horizontal time = **0.04 seconds** (40 ms).
  - Vertical voltage = **0.1 mV**.
- **1 Large Box ($5\text{ mm} \times 5\text{ mm} = 5\text{ small boxes}$):**
  - Horizontal time = **0.20 seconds** (200 ms).
  - Vertical voltage = **0.5 mV**.
- **5 Large Boxes ($25\text{ mm}$ horizontal):** Equals **1.00 second**.

## 4. Normal ECG Waves, Segments, and Intervals

- **P Wave:**
	- **Event:** Atrial depolarization initiated by the SA node.
	- **Normal Value:** Duration $\le 0.12\text{ s}$ ($\le 3$ small boxes); amplitude $\le 2.5\text{ mm}$ in Lead II.
	- **Abnormalities:** Tall peaked P wave ($> 2.5\text{ mm}$, *P pulmonale*) indicates right atrial enlargement; wide notched P wave (*P mitrale*) indicates left atrial enlargement; absent P waves occur in atrial fibrillation.
- **PR Interval:**
	- **Event:** Onset of P wave to onset of QRS complex (atrial depolarization plus AV nodal conduction delay).
	- **Normal Value:** **0.12 – 0.20 seconds** (3–5 small boxes).
	- **Abnormalities:** Prolonged ($> 0.20\text{ s}$) indicates 1st-degree AV block; shortened ($< 0.12\text{ s}$) with a slurred upstroke (*delta wave*) indicates Wolff–Parkinson–White (WPW) syndrome.
- **QRS Complex:**
	- **Event:** Rapid ventricular depolarization (atrial repolarization occurs simultaneously and is masked within it).
	- **Normal Value:** Duration **0.06 – 0.10 seconds** ($< 0.12\text{ s}$); normal Q wave is $< 0.04\text{ s}$ wide and $< 25\%$ of R-wave height.
	- **Abnormalities:** Wide QRS ($> 0.12\text{ s}$) indicates bundle branch blocks (RBBB/LBBB), ventricular tachycardia, or severe hyperkalemia; deep, wide pathological Q waves indicate old transmural myocardial infarction.
- **ST Segment:**
	- **Event:** Isoelectric period between the end of ventricular depolarization (J point) and onset of ventricular repolarization.
	- **Normal Value:** Flat at the isoelectric baseline.
	- **Abnormalities:** **ST elevation** occurs in acute transmural myocardial infarction (STEMI) and acute pericarditis; **ST depression** occurs in subendocardial ischemia and digoxin effect.
- **T Wave:**
	- **Event:** Ventricular repolarization.
	- **Normal Value:** Upright in leads I, II, and $V_3$–$V_6$; inverted in aVR.
	- **Abnormalities:** Tall, narrow, peaked ("tented") T waves are classic for **hyperkalemia**; flattened or inverted T waves indicate myocardial ischemia or **hypokalemia**.
- **QT Interval:**
	- **Event:** Total duration of ventricular depolarization and repolarization (onset of Q wave to end of T wave).
	- **Normal Value:** Corrected $QT_c$ (Bazett's formula: $QT_c = \frac{QT}{\sqrt{RR}}$) is $< 0.44\text{ s}$ in males and $< 0.46\text{ s}$ in females.
	- **Abnormalities:** Prolonged $QT_c$ (hypocalcemia, hypokalemia),  shortened $QT_c$ occurs in hypercalcemia and digoxin toxicity.
- **U Wave:**
  - **Event:** Late repolarization of Purkinje fibers or mid-myocardial cells.
  - **Normal Value:** Small positive deflection following the T wave (frequently absent).
  - **Abnormalities:** Prominent U wave is a classic sign of **hypokalemia**.
