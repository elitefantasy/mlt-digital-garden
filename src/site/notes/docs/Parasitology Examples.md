# 1. Protozoa
## Entamoeba Histolytics
![Entamoeba Histolytica.png\|358](/img/user/Attachments/Entamoeba%20Histolytica.png)
- **Trophozoite:** Unstable, constantly changing shape  . It displays **active**, growing , motile . 
- The cytoplasm contains ingested RBCs (erythrophagocytosis). 
	- pseudopodia formed by the extension of ectoplasm
	- cytoplasm is granular 
	- Trophozoite reproduce by binary fission every 8 hour 
- **Cyst:** Round  , containing **1 to 4 nuclei** with a small, **central dot-like karyosome** It contains characteristic **oblong chromatoid bodies with rounded ends** (cigar-shaped) and a large glycogen vacuole that stains reddish-brown.
### Introduction
 * **Entamoeba histolytica** is a **pathogenic intestinal protozoan parasite** that causes **amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery)**.
### Morphology & Stages
Entamoeba histolytica exists in three distinct morphological forms:
1. **Trophozoite** (Vegetative / Invasive Stage)
	* Size:  Larger forms are seen in freshly passed dysenteric stool; smaller forms appear in carriers;
	* **Motility & Structure**: **Irregular shape**; exhibits unidirectional crawling/gliding amoeboid **motility** via finger-like pseudopodia formed by ectoplasm extension and endoplasm streaming. Requires attachment to a surface (crawls up glass tubes in culture). Motility is inhibited at low temperatures.
	* **Nucleus**: Spherical , invisible in living forms but visible upon staining (e.g., iron-hematoxylin). Features a central karyosome surrounded by a clear halo, evenly distributed peripheral chromatin, and a linin network giving a **"cartwheel" appearance**.
	* **Cytoplasm & Inclusions**: Finely **granular** ("ground-glass" appearance). Contains food vacuoles with **ingested red blood** cells (RBCs), leukocytes, and tissue debris. 
	* **Reproduction & Viability:** Multiplies by **binary fission** every 8 hours. Survives up to 5 hours at 37°C but is destroyed by heat, drying, chemical sterilization, and gastric acid. Ingested trophozoites cannot cause infection.
2. **Precystic Stage**
	* **Characteristics**: Occurs only in the **intestinal lumen** prior to encystment (never in tissues or outside the body).
	* **Size & Structure**:  rounded/oval form with extruded food vacuoles, a large glycogen vacuole, and two chromatid bars.
3. **Cystic Stage** (Dormant / Infective Stage)
	* Size & Shape: Spherical.
	* Cyst Wall: Highly refractile; **resistant** to gastric acid and adverse external conditions, enabling environmental survival and transmission.
	* Maturation: Immature cysts contain chromatoid bars and a glycogen mass. As the cyst matures, these structures disappear, and the nucleus undergoes two mitotic divisions to form the infective **quadrinucleate cyst**.
### Life Cycle & Transmission
 * **Mode of Infection:** Ingestion of mature quadrinucleate cysts via contaminated food or water.
 * **Excystation**: Cysts pass unaffected through the stomach to the ileocecal region/ileum/cecum. Alkaline pH and trypsin action weaken the wall, and amoeboid movement releases a quadrinucleate amoeba (metacyst).
 * **Development**: The metacyst divides to form 8 small metacystic trophozoites (amoebulae), which colonize the large intestine/glandular crypts of the caecum and colon and reproduce by binary fission.
 * **Course of Colonization**:
	* Asymptomatic (90%): Trophozoites remain in the lumen without mucosal invasion, encyst under unfavorable conditions, and pass into feces as cysts (carriers).
	* Symptomatic/Invasive (10%): Trophozoites invade the mucosa, producing ulcerations, dysentery, and potential hematogenous spread.
### Pathogenesis & Clinical Features
1. **Intestinal Amoebiasis**
	* **Ulcer Formation**: Starts as raised nodules with pouting edges that rupture to form classic flask-shaped ulcers (narrow mouth, large rounded base) filled with necrotic material and trophozoites. Lesions undermine surrounding tissues in the submucosa and can merge.
	* **Symptoms**: Variable incubation period (1–4 months). Ranges from mild gastrointestinal symptoms (diarrhea, flatulence, cramps) to classic amoebic dysentery (large, dark, foul-smelling, semi-liquid stool streaked with blood and mucus). Patients are typically afebrile. Cecal disease can mimic appendicitis.
	* **Intestinal Complications:** Fulminant amoebic colitis, toxic megacolon, perianal ulceration, ameboma (inflammatory mass mimicking a tumor), perforation, peritonitis, and hemorrhage.
2. **Extraintestinal Amoebiasis**
	* **Hepatic Amoebiasis**: Most common extraintestinal complication (2–10% in tropics). Presents with liver tenderness, hepatomegaly, fever, rigors, leukocytosis, and right upper quadrant pain. Forms liver abscesses (primarily in the right lobe) filled with sterile, thick "anchovy sauce" pus.
	* **Pulmonary Amoebiasis:** Direct extension of hepatic abscess through the diaphragm into the lower right lung; causes pleuritic chest pain, cough, dyspnea, and expectoration of "anchovy sauce" sputum via a hepatobronchial fistula.
 * **Metastatic & Other Forms**:
	* **Cerebral**: Brain abscesses (often fatal).
	* Cutaneous: Destructive ulcerations resembling condyloma/malignancy around perianal areas, colostomy sites, or sinus tracts.
	* **Genitourinary**: Penile amoebiasis (glans/prepuce) or female genital spread (vulva, vagina, cervix) mimicking carcinoma.
	* **Splenic**: Splenic abscesses.
### Laboratory Diagnosis
**Microscopy & Staining**
	* **Direct Stool Examination**: Stool microscopy is the direct examination method. Saline mount shows active, motile trophozoites (often with ingested RBCs). Iodine wet mounts demonstrate cyst nuclei and golden-brown glycogen masses.
	* **Microscopic Exudate:** Few inflammatory pus cells, clustered yellowish/reddish-brown RBCs, and diamond-shaped, refractile Charcot-Leyden crystals.
	* **Sample Protocol:** Examination of 3 consecutive stool samples due to intermittent cyst shedding; formal-ether concentration for low parasite loads.
 * **Staining Properties:**
	* Trichrome stain: Permanent stool staining for cyst and trophozoite morphology.
	* Iron hematoxylin: Nuclei and chromatoid bodies stain dark blue/black; glycogen remains clear.
	* Iodine wet mount: Glycogen turns golden brown, nuclei/karyosome bright yellow, chromatoid bodies remain unstained.
	* PAS (Periodic Acid-Schiff) stain: Demonstrates amoebae in tissue sections.
**Hepatic Abscess Diagnosis**
	* Microscopic examination of aspirated liver pus or edge biopsies for trophozoites.
	* Cysts are found in stool in ~15% of hepatic cases.
	* Imaging (Ultrasound, CT, MRI, X-ray) detects abscesses but cannot differentiate amoebic from pyogenic causes.
**Culture, Serology & Molecular Tests**
	* Culture Media (Polyxenic): Boeck and Drbohlav, Balamuth's, and Jones media.
	* Antigen Detection: ELISA on stool, serum, or aspirates.
	* Antibody Detection: IHA, IFA, and ELISA (IHA \ge 1:256 or IFA \ge 1:200 suggest hepatic amoebiasis).
	* Molecular: PCR and DNA probes for detecting amoebic DNA in feces or aspirated pus.
### Treatment & Prevention
 * **Tissue & Intestinal Disease**: Metronidazole and Nitroimidazoles.
 * **Luminal Agents:** Diiodohydroxyquin and Diloxanide furoate.
 * **Supportive Care:** Fluid and electrolyte therapy.
 * **Prophylaxis**: Safe disposal of human feces, avoiding fecal contamination of food/water, and treatment of asymptomatic carriers.

## Entamoeba coli (Non-Pathogenic)
- _Crucial Exam Distinction:_ Must be differentiated from _E. histolytica_ to prevent unnecessary treatment
- **Trophozoite:** Large (20-40 um), sluggish, and **generally non-motile** . Both endoplasm and ectoplasm are heavily granular and filled with vacuoles and ingested bacteria (never contains RBCs)
- **Cyst:** Large (12-20 um), containing **1 to 8 nuclei** with an **eccentric, large karyosome** . Chromatoid bodies have **sharp, splintered, or needle-shaped ends**


## Giardia Lambila
- First identified by Antonie van Leeuwenhoek
- Only Parasite which Affects Small Intestine
- Leading cause of traveler's' diarrhea
- Primary Habitat: Human small intestine
- Specific Location: Duodenum and upper jejunum
![Pasted image 20260831194155.png\|423](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260831194155.png)

Exist in Two Forms
- **Trophozoite:** Pear-shaped or **tennis-racket-shaped** (10-18 um) with a ventral sucking disc used to adhere to the duodenal mucosa Contains **2 nuclei** (giving it a face-like appearance), 2 axostyles, and **4 pairs of flagella**. It moves forward in rapid, falling-leaf jerks 
- **Cyst:** Oval (8-12 um), containing **2 to 4 nuclei clustered at one end** and refractile, S-shaped fibrils (flagellar remnants) placed lengthwise 

### Life Cycle
has a **simple, direct life cycle** with **two main stages**: the **infective cyst** and the **multiplying trophozoite**. No intermediate host is required. 

1. Infective stage and transmission
	- **Mature cysts** are passed in the **feces** of an infected person or animal. 
	- Cysts are **environmentally resistant** and can survive for **weeks to months** in moist conditions (water, soil, surfaces). 
	- Infection occurs by **ingestion of cysts** via:
	    - Contaminated **drinking water** or **food**
	    - **Fecal–oral** person‑to‑person spread (e.g., poor hand hygiene, diaper changes)
	    - **Zoonotic** exposure (animals)
	    - Certain **sexual practices** involving fecal–oral contact 
	- As few as **10 cysts** can cause infection. 
2. Excystation (in the host)
	- After ingestion, cysts pass through the **stomach** and reach the **duodenum/proximal small intestine**. 
	- In response to **gastric acid, bile, and pancreatic enzymes**, each cyst undergoes **excystation**, releasing **two trophozoites** (sometimes described as “excyzoites” that quickly become trophozoites). 
3. Trophozoite stage (pathogenic, replicative)
	- **Trophozoites** are **pear‑shaped, motile, flagellated** organisms with **two nuclei** and a characteristic **ventral sucking disc**. 
	- They **attach** to the **duodenal and proximal jejunal mucosa** via the ventral disc, and may also exist free in the lumen. 
	- They **multiply by longitudinal binary fission**, rapidly colonizing the upper small intestine (doubling time ~9–12 hours). 
	- Trophozoites are responsible for **clinical disease** (malabsorption, diarrhea, bloating, etc.) but **do not survive** well outside the host. 
4. Encystation (formation of cysts)
	- As trophozoites move **distally** toward the **colon**, changes in **pH, bile salts, and other luminal factors** trigger **encystation**. 
	- During encystation:
	    - Trophozoites **round up**, retract flagella/axonemes, condense cytoplasm, and secrete a **tough hyaline cyst wall**. 
	    - Nuclear division occurs, producing a **mature quadrinucleate cyst**. 
	- **Cysts** are the form passed in **stool** (especially formed stool) and are the only stage capable of surviving in the environment.

### Clinical Features
- Asymptomatic Cases: Common in many infections. 
- Symptomatic Cases: Mucus-heavy diarrhea, fat malabsorption, epigastric discomfort, and flatulence. 
- Effects in Children: Chronic diarrhea, nutrient malabsorption, weight loss, and sprue-like syndrome. 
- Complications: Gallbladder colonization leading to biliary colic and jaundice. 
- Incubation Period: Typically, around two weeks, but variable.

### Laboratory Diagnosis of Giardiasis 
- Stool Examination: Identifies Giardia lamblia in stool samples. 
- Forms Detected: Cysts in formed stools; cysts & trophozoites in diarrheal stools. 
- Macroscopic Examination: Stool may have an offensive odor, a pale fatty appearance, and float in water. 
- Microscopic Examination: Uses wet preparations and concentration techniques for detection. 
- Asymptomatic Carriers: Typically show only cysts in stool samples.
- Concentration Technique : 
	- Flotation Preparation Zinc Sulphate Concentration Technique 
	- Sedimentation Technique Formalin Ethyl Acetate

## Balantidium Coli
![Pasted image 20260901191138.png\|394](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260901191138.png)
### 1. Introduction & History
- **Taxonomy:** Belongs to the Phylum _Ciliophora_ and Family _Balantiididae_.
- **Classification:** It is the only ciliate protozoan parasite of humans.
- **Size:** It is the largest protozoan parasite of humans.
- **Location:** Largest protozoan parasite residing in the large intestine of man.
- **History:** First described by Malmsten in 1857 in the feces of dysenteric patients.
### 2. Habitat & Morphology
- **Habitat:** Resides in the large intestine of humans, pigs, and monkeys.
- **Morphological Stages:** Occurs in two distinct stages:
    1. Trophozoite
    2. Cyst
#### A. Trophozoite
- **Habitat/Feeding:** Lives in the large intestine, feeding on cell debris, bacteria, starch grains, and other particles.
- **Function/Characteristics:** Actively motile; serves as the invasive stage of the parasite; found in dysenteric stool.
- **Size:** Large ovoid cell, about 60–70 µm in length and 40–50 µm in breadth (very large cells measuring up to 200 µm are sometimes seen).
- **Motility:** Covered with short, delicate cilia over the entire surface of the body.
![Pasted image 20260901191243.png\|356](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260901191243.png)
- **Structure:** 
    - Anterior end is narrow; posterior end is broad.
    - **Anterior End:** Features a groove (_peristome_) leading to the mouth (_cytostome_) and a short funnel-shaped gullet (_cytopharynx_).
    - **Posterior End:** Has a small anal pore (_cytopyge_).
    - **Adoral Cilia:** Cilia surrounding the mouth are larger.
- **Nuclear Apparatus (Two Nuclei):**
    1. A large kidney-shaped **macronucleus**.
    2. A small **micronucleus** lying in the concavity of the macronucleus.
- **Cytoplasmic Contents:** Contains 1–2 contractile vacuoles and several food vacuoles.
#### B. Cyst
- **Shape & Size:** Spherical in shape, measuring 40–60 µm in diameter.
- **Cyst Wall:** Thick and transparent double-layered wall.
- **Contents:** Granular cytoplasm containing a macronucleus, micronucleus, and vacuoles.
- **Significance:** The **infective stage** of _B. coli_; found in chronic cases and healthy carriers.
### 3. Life Cycle
- **Host Requirement:** Completes its life cycle in **one host only** (single-host life cycle).
    - **Natural Host:** Pig
    - **Accidental Host:** Man
    - **Reservoirs:** Pig, monkey, and rat
    - **Infective Form:** Cyst
- **Transmission:** Balantidiasis is a zoonosis. Humans acquire infection by ingesting food or water contaminated with feces containing cysts from pigs, animal reservoirs, or human carriers.
- **Excystation:** Occurs in the small intestine following cyst ingestion.
- **Development & Colonization:**
    - From each cyst, a **single trophozoite** is liberated, which migrates to the large intestine.
    - Trophozoites multiply in the large intestine by **transverse binary fission**.
    - Sexual reproduction occurs infrequently by **conjugation**, during which reciprocal exchange of nuclear material happens between two trophozoites enclosed within a single cyst wall.
- **Encystation:**
    - Occurs as trophozoites pass down the colon or in evacuated stool.
    - The cell rounds up and secretes a tough cyst wall.
    - Cysts remain viable in feces for **1 to 2 days** to contaminate food/water and spread to new hosts.
### 4. Pathogenesis & Clinical Feature
- **Commensal Phase:** In healthy individuals, _B. coli_ lives as a lumen commensal and remains asymptomatic.
- **Predisposing Factors:** Disease develops when host resistance is lowered by:
    - Malnourishment
    - Alcoholism
    - Achlorhydria (absence of stomach acid)
    - Concurrent infection by _Trichuris trichiura_ or bacterial infections
- **Pathology:**
    - Trophozoites burrow into the intestinal mucosa, establish colonies, and cause an inflammatory reaction.
    - Forms **mucosal ulcers** and **submucosal abscesses** (resembling lesions in amebiasis).
    - **Key Distinction:** Unlike _Entamoeba histolytica_, _B. coli_ **does not** invade the liver or any other extraintestinal sites.
- **Clinical Features:**
    - Most cases are asymptomatic.
    - Symptomatic disease (balantidiasis) resembles amebiasis.
    - Presents with diarrhea or frank dysentery, abdominal colic, tenesmus, nausea, and vomiting.
### 5. Laboratory Diagnosis
- **Stool Examination:**
    - Demonstration of trophozoites and cysts in feces.
    - **Diarrheic Feces:** Motile trophozoites (recognized by large size, kidney-shaped macronucleus, and rapid-revolving motility).
    - **Formed Stool:** Cysts (recognized by round shape and large macronucleus).
- **Biopsy:** Biopsy specimens and scrapings from intestinal ulcers can be examined if stool tests are negative.
- **Culture:** Can be cultured in vitro in Locke's egg albumin medium or IH polygenic medium (similar to _E. histolytica_), though rarely necessary.
### 6. Treatment & Prophylaxis
- **Treatment:** **Tetracycline** is the drug of choice.
- **Prophylaxis:** Avoid food/water contamination; treat infected pigs and humans.

# 2. Helminths 
## 2.1 Nematodes (roundworms)
### **A. _Ascaris lumbricoides_ (Giant Roundworm)**

- **Diagnostic Stage in Stool:** Bile-stained, yellowish-brown eggs ``.
- **Fertilized Egg:** Round or oval (45-75 um), surrounded by a thick, transparent inner shell and a **rough, bumpy, albuminous outer coat** (called a **corticated egg**) ``.
- **Unfertilized Egg:** Elongated or elliptical (80-90 \(\mu\text{m}\)) with a thin inner shell and a highly irregular, **jagged, corticated outer coat** filled with refractile granules ``.
- **Decorticated Egg:** Sometimes eggs are shed **without the rough outer coat** (decorticated), presenting with only a smooth, thick, colorless shell

### **B. _Trichuris trichiura_ (Whipworm)**
- **Diagnostic Stage in Stool:** Distinctive **barrel-shaped or lemon-shaped** eggs (50 um)
- **Key Characteristics:** Features a thick, smooth, brown, double-layered shell The key diagnostic hallmark is the presence of a **rounded, transparent mucoid plug at each pole (bipolar plugs)**

### **C. _Enterobius vermicularis_ (Pinworm / Threadworm)**

- _Crucial Exam Tip:_ Eggs **rarely** appear in routine stool specimens because gravid females migrate out of the anus at night to lay eggs on the perianal skin 
- **Egg Morphology:** Asymmetrical, **D-shaped (planoconvex)** eggs  long) with one flat side and one curved side  They feature a smooth, thin, colorless, double-lined shell containing a curled-up larva`.
- **Special Collection Technique (The Perianal Cotton Swab/Wipe):** Since eggs are deposited perianally, they must be collected by wiping the skin around the anus using a **cotton-wool swab** The swab is dipped into **0.5 mL of normal saline** inside a test tube, and the fluid is evaluated microscopically as a wet mount

### **D. Hookworms (_Ancylostoma duodenale_ & _Necator americanus_)**

- **Diagnostic Stage in Stool:** Colorless, transparent, non-bile-stained eggs 
- **Key Characteristics:** Oval-shaped with flattened poles, surrounded by an **extremely thin, single-lined hyaline shell membrane** The internal content is segmented and typically contains **4 to 8 cells (blastomeres)** separated by a clear space from the shell

### **E. _Strongyloides stercoralis_ (Threadworm)**

- _Crucial Exam Tip:_ Unlike hookworms, the eggs of _Strongyloides_ hatch rapidly within the intestinal mucosa, meaning **eggs are almost never seen in stool** ``.
- **Diagnostic Stage in Stool:** Active **Rhabditidiform Larvae** (200-300 \(\mu\text{m}\) long, 15 \(\mu\text{m}\) thick) ``.
- **Key Characteristics:** Possesses a characteristically **short mouth/buccal cavity**, a double-bulb esophagus, a tapered tail, and a visible genital primordium near the middle of its body ``.

## 2.2 Cestodes (Tapeworms)

#### A. Taenia saginata_(Beef) & Taenia solium (Pork)

- _Crucial Exam Tip:_ The eggs of _T. saginata_ and _T. solium_ are **morphologically identical** and cannot be differentiated under the microscope ``.
- **Egg Morphology:** Spherical, yellowish-brown, bile-stained eggs (30-40 \(\mu\text{m}\)) `. They feature a **thick, smooth, double-layered shell with prominent radial striations** (transverse lines)`. The center contains a hexacanth embryo (oncosphere) with **3 pairs of refractile, lancet-shaped hooklets** ``.

#### B. Hymenolepis nana (Dwarf Tapeworm)

- **Egg Morphology:** Oval or spherical (30-47 \(\mu\text{m}\)) with a thin, transparent, hyaline outer membrane and a thick inner membrane `. The inner membrane has **polar thickenings that bear 4 to 8 long hair-like filaments** extending into the space between the two membranes`.

#### C. Hymenolepis diminuta (Rat Tapeworm)

- **Egg Morphology:** Round or slightly oval, significantly larger than _H. nana_  The outer shell is thick and yellowish, but it **completely lacks polar filaments**`

#### D. Diphyllobothrium latum ⭐
**Larval Stages**
	1. **Coracidium** — First stage
	2. **Procercoid larva** — Second stage
	3. **Plerocercoid larva** — Third stage

**Life Cycle**
1. Humans pass eggs through stool.
2. Eggs reach water.
3. A larva is released from the egg — the **coracidium** (Stage 1).
4. The coracidium is consumed by a **Cyclops** in water.
5. The Stage 1 larva develops inside the Cyclops into the **procercoid larva** (Stage 2).
6. The infected Cyclops is consumed by fish.
7. The larva develops into the **plerocercoid larva** (Stage 3) inside the fish.
8. The plerocercoid larva is the **infective stage for humans**.

## 2.3 Trematodes (Flukes)

#### A. Schistosomes (Blood Flukes)

- **_Schistosoma haematobium_ (Bladder Fluke):** Large, spindle-shaped eggs  with a **prominent, sharp terminal spine** at one pole Excreted primarily in **urine**.  also known as ==vesicle fluke and bilharzia==
- **_Schistosoma mansoni_:** Large, oval eggs (150 um) with a **prominent, sharp lateral spine** pointing backward Excreted primarily in **stool**
- **_Schistosoma japonicum_:** Round or oval eggs (70-80 um) with a very **small, inconspicuous lateral knob/spine**  Excreted primarily in **stool**
<!--SR:!2026-09-09,1,150-->

#### B. Hermaphroditic Flukes

- _Fasciola hepatica_ (Sheep Liver Fluke) & _Fasciolopsis buski_ (Giant Intestinal Fluke): 
	- Large, ovoid, bile-stained, yellowish-brown eggs (140 um) long, 80um wide) .They feature a smooth shell with a distinct cap-like lid called an **operculum** at one end. (Note: The eggs of these two flukes are morphologically identical and cannot be differentiated from each other
- _Paragonimus westermani_ (Lung Fluke): 
	- Oval, thick-shelled, golden-brown eggs (80 um by 55 um)  with a flat **operculum** at one end . Excreted primarily in **sputum**, but can be swallowed and detected in **stool**
- _Clonorchis sinensis_ (Chinese Liver Fluke): Small, distinctive **flask-shaped** eggs (20-35 um) with a convex operculum at the top and a **small, pointed knob at the posterior end** opposite the operculum ``.
