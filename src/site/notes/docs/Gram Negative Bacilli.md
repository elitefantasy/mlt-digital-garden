Gram-negative bacilli are a large and clinically important group of bacteria characterized by their **pink/red stain on Gram stain**. This coloration is due to their **thin peptidoglycan layer** and the presence of an **outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**. They are significant pathogens affecting the **gut, urinary tract, respiratory tract, bloodstream, wounds, and central nervous system (CNS)**. ncbi.nlm.nih+2

## 1. Definition and General Features

Gram-negative bacilli are rod-shaped bacteria that do not retain crystal violet during Gram staining. Instead, they are decolorized and subsequently counterstained red/pink by safranin. Their outer membrane provides additional protection, contributing to their increased resistance to antibiotics and host defenses. ncbi.nlm.nih+1

### Key Structural Features

-   **Outer Membrane:** Contains **LPS**, porins, and various proteins. It acts as a crucial permeability barrier. msdmanuals+1
-   **LPS Endotoxin:** The **lipid A** portion of LPS is responsible for triggering systemic inflammatory responses, including fever, inflammation, shock, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) in severe infections. msdmanuals+1
-   **Periplasmic Space:** Located between the inner and outer membranes, this space contains enzymes that can contribute to antibiotic resistance.
-   **Capsule:** Present in some species, the capsule helps bacteria evade phagocytosis by host immune cells.
-   **Pili/Fimbriae:** Hair-like appendages that facilitate adhesion to host tissues, playing a vital role in virulence.
-   **Flagella:** Present in many species, providing motility. Many coliforms are flagellated, with *Klebsiella* being a notable exception.

## 2. Classification

Gram-negative bacilli can be grouped in several practical ways for laboratory identification and clinical understanding.

### 2.1 Enteric Gram-Negative Bacilli (Enterobacterales)

These are commonly associated with the intestinal tract.
-   **Enterobacteriaceae:** Includes genera such as *Escherichia, Klebsiella, Enterobacter, Serratia, Citrobacter, Proteus, Salmonella, Shigella, and Yersinia* (e.g., causing bubonic plague).
-   **Coliforms:** Many enteric bacilli are referred to as coliforms, particularly those that are **lactose fermenters**, such as *E. coli, Klebsiella, Enterobacter,* and *Citrobacter*.


-   **Escherichia coli:** A normal gut flora member; causes UTIs, neonatal meningitis, sepsis, and various forms of gastroenteritis.
-   **Klebsiella pneumoniae:** Characterized by its capsule, forms mucoid colonies, and causes pneumonia, UTIs, and liver abscesses.
-   **Proteus mirabilis:** Urease positive, exhibits swarming motility, and is associated with UTIs and kidney stones.
-   **Salmonella typhi / Non-typhoidal Salmonella:** Causes enteric fever (typhoid), gastroenteritis, and bacteremia.
-   **Shigella:** Responsible for dysentery, with a very low infective dose.
-   **Enterobacter, Citrobacter, Serratia:** Opportunistic nosocomial pathogens. 

### 2.2 Non-Fermenting Gram-Negative Bacilli

These are aerobic bacilli that **do not ferment carbohydrates**. They are often opportunistic pathogens, particularly in hospital settings.
-   *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*
-   *Acinetobacter* spp.


-   **Pseudomonas aeruginosa:** An opportunistic pathogen causing infections in burn patients, ICU patients, pneumonia, UTIs, and sepsis.
-   **Acinetobacter baumannii:** A significant cause of hospital-acquired pneumonia, bloodstream, and wound infections.
-   **Stenotrophomonas maltophilia:** An opportunistic, multi-drug resistant hospital pathogen. 

### 2.3 Other Clinically Important Gram-Negative Bacilli

-   *Vibrio cholerae*: The causative agent of cholera.
-   *Haemophilus influenzae*: Causes respiratory infections and, historically, meningitis.
-   *Bordetella pertussis* :  Responsible for <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">whooping cough.</mark>
-   *Legionella pneumophila*
-   *Neisseria* spp. (Note: *Neisseria* are Gram-negative cocci, not bacilli).
-   **Anaerobic Gram-Negative Bacilli:** Includes *Bacteroides, Prevotella, and Fusobacterium*.

## 3. Important Laboratory Identification Points

Laboratory identification of Gram-negative bacilli relies on culture characteristics, biochemical tests, and sometimes antigen or molecular assays.

### Common Screening Points

-   **Gram Stain:** Observation of pink/red bacilli.
-   **MacConkey Agar:** Used to differentiate **lactose fermenters** from **non-lactose fermenters**.
    -   **Lactose Fermenters:** Produce pink colonies due to acid production from lactose fermentation, which lowers the pH and causes the neutral red indicator to turn pink. sciencedirect+1
    -   **Non-Lactose Fermenters:** Produce pale/colorless colonies as they do not ferment lactose. sciencedirect+1
-   **Oxidase Test:** Helps distinguish many enteric bacilli (often oxidase-negative) from non-fermenters and other groups (often oxidase-positive).
-   **Capsule Detection:** Important for identifying encapsulated organisms like *Klebsiella* and some *E. coli* strains.
-   **Antigenic Structure:** O, H, and K antigens are used in the serological classification of many enteric bacilli.

### Important Exam Point

It is crucial not to confuse **Gram-negative bacilli** with **all gut bacteria**. The gut harbors a diverse microbiota, but medically important Gram-negative bacilli represent only a subset, some of which are lactose fermenters while others are not.

## 4. Pathogenesis and Virulence Factors

Gram-negative bacilli employ a variety of virulence factors to cause disease. The major ones include **LPS endotoxin, capsule, pili, adhesins, exotoxins, enzymes, motility, and biofilm formation**. msdmanuals+2

### Major Virulence Factors

-   **Endotoxin (LPS/Lipid A):** Triggers the release of cytokines, leading to fever, vasodilation, capillary leak, shock, and DIC. msdmanuals+1
-   **Capsule:** Acts as a protective layer, preventing phagocytosis by immune cells. msdmanuals+1
-   **Pili/Adhesins:** Enable bacteria to attach to host mucosal surfaces and medical devices like catheters. ncbi.nlm.nih+1
-   **Exotoxins:** Secreted toxins that can cause direct tissue damage or induce secretory diarrhea, as seen with certain *E. coli* strains and *Vibrio cholerae*.
-   **Biofilm Formation:** Particularly significant in hospital-acquired infections, biofilms provide protection against antibiotics and host immunity. journals.lww+1
-   **Secretion Systems and Enzymes:** Facilitate bacterial invasion into host cells and evasion of immune responses.

## 5. Diseases Caused

Gram-negative bacilli are capable of infecting nearly every organ system in the human body.

### Common Infections

-   **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs):** Primarily caused by *E. coli, Klebsiella,* and *Proteus*. msdmanuals+1
-   **Gastroenteritis and Diarrhea:** Common culprits include *E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella,* and *Vibrio cholerae*. merckmanuals+1
-   **Pneumonia:** Especially hospital-acquired pneumonia, often due to *Klebsiella* and *Pseudomonas*. msdmanuals+1
-   **Bloodstream Infection / Bacteremia / Sepsis:** Severe infections that can be rapidly fatal. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih+1
-   **Wound and Surgical Site Infections:** Frequently encountered in healthcare settings. msdmanuals+1
-   **Meningitis:** Notably *E. coli* in neonates, historically *H. influenzae*, and other species in immunocompromised patients. ncbi.nlm.nih+1
-   **Biliary Tract and Intra-abdominal Infections:** Common with enteric bacilli.
-   **Anaerobic Infections:** *Bacteroides* and related organisms are often involved in abscesses and mixed infections.




## 6. Antibiotic Resistance

A major clinical challenge with Gram-negative bacilli is their **antimicrobial resistance**. Their outer membrane inherently reduces drug entry, and many species produce enzymes like **beta-lactamases**, making infections increasingly difficult to treat. ncbi.nlm.nih+2

## 8. Exam Points to Remember

-   Gram-negative bacilli stain **pink/red** on Gram stain.
-   They possess an **outer membrane with LPS**. msdmanuals+1
-   **Lipid A is the endotoxin** component. msdmanuals+1
-   Important groups include **Enterobacterales**, **non-fermenters**, and **other Gram-negative bacilli** such as *Vibrio*, *Haemophilus*, and anaerobes. ncbi.nlm.nih+2
-   Key laboratory tools for identification are **MacConkey agar**, the **oxidase test**, **biochemical tests**, and sometimes **serotyping**.
