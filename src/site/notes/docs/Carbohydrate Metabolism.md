# Glycolysis

Glycolysis, also known as the Embden–Meyerhof–Parnas (EMP) pathway, is a fundamental metabolic process that converts one molecule of glucose (C6H12O6) into two molecules of pyruvate. It serves as the primary stage of cellular respiration and provides energy under both aerobic and anaerobic conditions.


Location

Glycolysis occurs exclusively in the cytosol (cytoplasm) of the cell.

**1\. Energy investment phase (steps 1–5)**

This phase uses 2 ATP to phosphorylate and rearrange glucose into two 3‑carbon intermediates.

1. **Glucose → Glucose‑6‑phosphate (G6P)**  
   * Enzyme: **Hexokinase** (muscle/most tissues) or **Glucokinase** (liver, pancreas)  this enzyme adds phosphate group from atp to glucose, <mark style="background: #FF5582A6;">requires magnesium Mg2+  </mark>
   * Uses 1 ATP → ADP  
   * Irreversible; key regulatory step in many tissues.  
2. **Glucose‑6‑phosphate → Fructose‑6‑phosphate (F6P)**  
   * Enzyme: **Phosphoglucose isomerase,** ;   
   * Isomerization (aldose ↔ ketose).  
3. **Fructose‑6‑phosphate → Fructose‑1,6‑bisphosphate (F1,6BP)**  
   * Enzyme: **Phosphofructokinase‑1 (PFK‑1)** ; Regulatory Enzyme  
   * Uses 1 ATP → ADP  
   * Major rate‑limiting, highly regulated, irreversible step.  
4. **Fructose‑1,6‑bisphosphate → Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP) \+ Glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate (G3P)**  
   * Enzyme: **Aldolase**  
   * Cleavage of 6C sugar into two 3C sugars.  
5. **DHAP ↔ Glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate (G3P)**  
   * Enzyme: **Triose phosphate isomerase**  
   * DHAP is converted to G3P, so **2 G3P** proceed per glucose.

   ## **2\. Energy payoff phase (steps 6–10)**

Each G3P goes through these steps, so everything here happens **twice per glucose**.

6. **Glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate → 1,3‑Bisphosphoglycerate (1,3‑BPG)**  
   * Enzyme: **Glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH)**  
   * NAD⁺ → NADH \+ H⁺ (reduction)  
   * Inorganic phosphate (Pi) is incorporated.  
7. **1,3‑Bisphosphoglycerate → 3‑Phosphoglycerate (3‑PG)**  
   * Enzyme: **Phosphoglycerate kinase**  
   * Substrate‑level phosphorylation: 1,3‑BPG donates phosphate to ADP → ATP  
   * Produces **1 ATP per G3P** (2 per glucose).  
8. **3‑Phosphoglycerate → 2‑Phosphoglycerate (2‑PG)**  
   * Enzyme: **Phosphoglycerate mutase**  
   * Phosphate shift from C3 to C2.  
9. **2‑Phosphoglycerate → Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)**  
   * Enzyme: **Enolase**  
   * Dehydration (removal of H₂O), creates high‑energy PEP.  
10. **Phosphoenolpyruvate → Pyruvate**  
    * Enzyme: **Pyruvate kinase**  
    * Substrate‑level phosphorylation: PEP → pyruvate, ADP → ATP  
    * Produces **1 ATP per G3P** (2 per glucose).

**Net from payoff phase:** 4 ATP \+ 2 NADH per glucose; minus 2 ATP invested \= **2 net ATP \+ 2 NADH**.

## **Fate of pyruvate**

What happens to pyruvate depends on oxygen availability and cell type:

* **Aerobic conditions:**  
  * Pyruvate → **Acetyl‑CoA** (via pyruvate dehydrogenase in mitochondria) → enters **TCA cycle** → oxidative phosphorylation yields much more ATP.+1  
* **Anaerobic conditions (e.g., exercising muscle, RBCs):**  
  * Pyruvate → **Lactate** (via lactate dehydrogenase), regenerating NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue.+1  
* **In yeast and some microbes:**  
  * Pyruvate → **Ethanol \+ CO₂** (alcoholic fermentation).

  ## **Key regulatory points**

Three irreversible, highly regulated enzymes control glycolytic flux:

1. **Hexokinase / Glucokinase**  
   * Inhibited by product (G6P) for hexokinase; glucokinase has higher Km and is insulin‑responsive in liver.  
2. **Phosphofructokinase‑1 (PFK‑1)** – main control point  
   * **Inhibited by:** ATP, citrate, low pH (in muscle).  
   * **Activated by:** AMP, ADP, fructose‑2,6‑bisphosphate (F2,6BP).  
3. **Pyruvate kinase**  
   * **Activated by:** Fructose‑1,6‑bisphosphate (feed‑forward).  
   * **Inhibited by:** ATP, alanine; in liver, phosphorylation (glucagon) inactivates it.

   

**Net Energy Yield per Glucose**

* **2 Pyruvate**  
* **2 Net ATP** (4 produced minus 2 consumed)  
* **2 NADH**

**Overall Reaction:**  
C6H12O6 \+ 2 NAD\+ \+ 2 ADP \+ 2 Pi → 2 Pyruvate \+ 2 NADH \+ 2 H\+ \+ 2 ATP \+ 2 H2O

  
  
  
  
  

# Hexose Monophosphate (HMP) Shunt

_Also known as the **Pentose Phosphate Pathway (PPP)** or **Phosphogluconate Pathway**_

The **HMP pathway** is a metabolic pathway parallel to glycolysis that occurs in the cytosol. It is primarily an **anabolic pathway** designed to generate reducing power and precursors for biosynthesis rather than direct ATP generation.

## 1\. Key Features

-   **Site:** Cytosol (cytoplasm).
-   **Energy Balance:** **No ATP** is directly consumed or produced.
-   **Major Products:**
    
    -   **NADPH:** Used for reductive biosynthesis and cellular antioxidant defense.
    -   **Ribose-5-Phosphate:** Required for nucleotide and nucleic acid (DNA/RNA) synthesis.

## 2\. Pathway Phases

```
Glucose-6-Phosphate
        │
        ▼ (Oxidative Phase — Irreversible | Rate-limiting: G6PD)
Ribulose-5-Phosphate + 2 NADPH + CO₂
        │
        ▼ (Non-Oxidative Phase — Reversible | Transketolase, Transaldolase)
Fructose-6-Phosphate + Glyceraldehyde-3-Phosphate (Links to Glycolysis)
```

### Phase 1: Oxidative Phase _(Irreversible)_

-   **Function:** Generates NADPH and CO2​.
-   **Key Enzyme:** **Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD)** — the committed, rate-limiting enzyme.
-   **Reaction:**
    
    Glucose-6-Phosphate+2NADP++H2​O⟶Ribulose-5-Phosphate+2NADPH+2H++CO2​
    

### Phase 2: Non-Oxidative Phase _(Reversible)_

-   **Function:** Interconverts pentose sugars back into hexoses and trioses to link with glycolysis and gluconeogenesis.
-   **Key Enzymes:**
    
    -   **Transketolase:** Transfers 2-carbon units; requires **Thiamine Pyrophosphate (TPP / Vitamin B1​)** as a cofactor.
    -   **Transaldolase:** Transfers 3-carbon units.

## 3\. Metabolic Significance & Functions

### A. NADPH Production

| Process | Physiological Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Reductive Biosynthesis** | Powers fatty acid, cholesterol, and steroid synthesis (e.g., in liver, adipose tissue, and adrenal glands). |
| **Antioxidant Defense** | Keeps glutathione in its reduced state (G-SH) via _glutathione reductase_, detoxifying reactive oxygen species (H2​O2​). |
| **Respiratory Burst** | Utilized by phagocytes (WBCs) via NADPH oxidase for antimicrobial pathogen killing. |
| **Cytochrome P450 System** | Supports drug and xenobiotic detoxification in the liver. |

### B. Ribose-5-Phosphate Production

-   Essential building block for:
    
    -   **Nucleic Acids:** RNA and DNA synthesis.
    -   **Nucleotide Coenzymes:** ATP, NADH, FAD, and CoA.

## 4\. Tissue Distribution

| Tissue                       | Primary Purpose                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Liver & Adipose Tissue**   | High demand for NADPH for active fatty acid and lipid synthesis.                                                        |
| **Adrenal Cortex & Gonads**  | NADPH required for steroid hormone hydroxylation reactions.                                                             |
| **Lactating Mammary Glands** | Active synthesis of milk lipids and fatty acids.                                                                        |
| **Erythrocytes (RBCs)**      | **Sole source** of NADPH to maintain reduced glutathione and prevent oxidative hemolysis (due to lack of mitochondria). |

## 5\. Clinical Significance

### G6PD Deficiency

-   **Pathophysiology:** An **X-linked recessive** enzymopathy leading to impaired NADPH production.
-   **Clinical Presentation:** Red blood cells cannot regenerate reduced glutathione (G-SH), making them susceptible to oxidative stress. Exposure to triggers causes **acute hemolytic anemia**.
-   **Triggers:**
    
    -   Ingestion of **fava beans** (favism).
    -   Oxidant drugs (e.g., antimalarials like primaquine, sulfonamides, dapsone).
    -   Severe infections.
-   **Evolutionary Advantage:** Confers partial protection against _Plasmodium falciparum_ malaria.

### Thiamine (Vitamin B1​) Deficiency

-   **Pathophysiology:** Reduces the activity of **Transketolase**.
-   **Clinical Use:** Erythrocyte transketolase activation assay is used as a diagnostic test for thiamine deficiency.
-   **Associations:** Contributes to neurological and cardiovascular manifestations such as **Beriberi** and **Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome**.

## 6\. Regulation

-   **Rate-Limiting Step:** Catalyzed by **G6PD**.
-   **Feedback Control:**
    
    -   **Inhibitor:** High \[NADPH\]/\[NADP+\] ratio (feedback allosteric inhibition).
    -   **Activator:** Elevated NADP+ levels.
-   **Hormonal Control:** **Insulin** upregulates the gene expression and synthesis of G6PD in the fed state.
