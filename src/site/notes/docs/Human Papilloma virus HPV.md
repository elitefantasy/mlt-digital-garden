# **Human Papillomavirus**

## **Very short revision points**

* HPV \= Human Papillomavirus.  
* Virus type: non-enveloped dsDNA.  
* Low-risk: 6, 11\.  
* High-risk: 16, 18\.  
* Characteristic smear finding: koilocyte.  
* Major disease: cervical cancer.

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a small, non-enveloped, double-stranded DNA virus that infects squamous epithelium of the skin and mucous membranes. It is a major cause of genital warts, cervical precancer, and cervical cancer.

---

## **1\. Classification and structure**

* Family: Papillomaviridae.  
* Genome: circular double-stranded DNA.  
* Enveloped or non-enveloped: Non-enveloped.  
* Tropism: Epitheliotropic; infects cutaneous and mucosal epithelium.  
* HPV types: more than 100 types have been identified; some sources note more than 200 types overall.

---

## **2\. Important HPV types**

### **Low-risk types**

* HPV 6 and 11 are the classic low-risk types.  
* They are associated mainly with genital warts and benign papillomas.

### **High-risk types**

* HPV 16 and 18 are the most important high-risk types.  
* Other high-risk types include 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58 and related types.

---

## **3\. Diseases caused by HPV**

### **Low-risk disease**

* Anogenital warts (condyloma acuminatum).  
* Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis.

### **High-risk disease**

* Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN).  
* Cervical cancer.  
* Also associated with cancers of the vulva, vagina, penis, anus, and oropharynx.

#### **Exam line**

Persistent infection with high-risk HPV types, especially 16 and 18, is strongly associated with cervical cancer.

---

## **4\. Pathogenesis**

* HPV infects basal cells of stratified squamous epithelium through microabrasions.  
* Viral replication is linked to epithelial cell maturation.  
* Koilocytosis is the characteristic cytopathic effect of HPV infection in cervical cytology.  
* High-risk HPV types can integrate into host DNA and drive malignant transformation.

### **Koilocyte features**

* Enlarged irregular nucleus  
* Perinuclear halo  
* Nuclear hyperchromasia  
* Irregular nuclear membrane

These are seen in cervical smears in HPV infection.

---

## **5\. Clinical features**

Many HPV infections are asymptomatic and may clear spontaneously. Persistent infection may produce:

* Genital warts.  
* Cervical dysplasia.  
* Precancerous lesions.  
* Cancer of cervix and other anogenital sites.

---

## **6\. Diagnosis**

### **Cytology**

Pap smear / cervical cytology may show koilocytes, which suggest HPV infection.

### **Molecular tests**

HPV DNA testing is used to detect high-risk HPV types.

### **Biopsy**

Used when cytology suggests dysplasia or malignancy.

---

## **7\. Prevention**

### **Vaccination**

* HPV vaccines are given to prevent infection with common high-risk and low-risk types.  
* Vaccination is a major preventive strategy against cervical cancer.

### **Screening**

Regular cervical screening with Pap smear and/or HPV testing helps detect precancer early.

### **Safe practices**

Safe sexual practices reduce risk of transmission.

---

## **8\. Treatment**

* Genital warts: local destructive therapy, topical agents, or removal depending on lesion.  
* Precancerous lesions: excision/ablation as indicated.  
* Cancer: managed by oncology protocols.

There is no single curative antiviral that eradicates HPV completely.
